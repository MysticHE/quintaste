# Browse by Dish Role, Cook from Photos

QuinTaste's Cook screen should organise recipes by the role a dish plays in a Singapore meal, and it should teach with photos and structured text rather than video. The adopted taxonomy has one single-select row: **All · Breakfast · Noodles · Rice meals · With rice · Soups · Snacks & kueh · Desserts & drinks**. Vegan, Allium-free, Under 15 or Under 30 min, One pot and Saved only sit in Filters and combine across facets. This is the best-supported design the evidence allows. Every chip has a Singapore or East Asian precedent, seven categories plus All fits the 6–8-item limit for horizontal toolbars, and "chips navigate, filters narrow" follows Baymard's tested filtering guidance. Its weaknesses lie in content and labels, not structure. In the 18-recipe library, "Snacks & kueh" contains no kueh and "Desserts & drinks" contains no drink. The Vegan toggle filters nothing, because every bundled recipe is vegan. "With rice" is an untested label that needs a card sort before the library grows around it. For media, no public controlled study shows that video recipes lead to more home cooking than photo-and-text recipes, and every positive cooking study paired video with a written recipe. Meta-analyses place video's learning advantage mainly in motor skills such as rolling and pleating (d = 1.06), well above its small average advantage (g ≈ 0.23). The recommendation is a photo-led hybrid in four stages: finish hero photos (9 of 18 recipes still show a category tile), add real step stills and 2–4-frame technique strips, and only then test short, silent, captioned per-step loops on 3–5 technique-heavy recipes. QuinTaste has no analytics backend, so both the label decision and the clip decision must be validated through moderated studies or an opt-in local log, not an A/B dashboard.

## Singapore cooks rice plus dishes, so courses make poor chips

Leading recipe services agree on almost nothing about their top-level axis, and the disagreement is informative. Tasty's menu mixes meal type, diet, ingredient and editorial moments such as "Weeknight Dinners" and "Plant-Based Meals" ([Tasty](https://tasty.co/)). **Forks Over Knives, the plant-based leader, browses almost entirely by dish type**: Breakfasts, Pasta & Noodles, Soups & Stews, Desserts and similar, with no cuisine, occasion or time filters ([Forks Over Knives](https://www.forksoverknives.com/recipes/)). East Asian leaders browse by dish form and ingredient. DELISH KITCHEN lists 23 dish types, including rice dishes, noodles, soups, tofu dishes and drinks ([DELISH KITCHEN](https://delishkitchen.tv/categories)). Xiachufang groups soups, congee and staples together ([Xiachufang](https://www.xiachufang.com/category/)). Kurashiru builds menus from 主菜・副菜・汁物 (main dish, side dish, soup) and treats cuisine as a filter ([Kurashiru App Store JP](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%82%AF%E3%83%A9%E3%82%B7%E3%83%AB-%E6%AF%8E%E6%97%A5%E3%81%AE%E7%8C%AE%E7%AB%8B%E3%81%AB-%E3%83%AC%E3%82%B7%E3%83%94%E5%8B%95%E7%94%BB%E3%81%A7%E6%96%99%E7%90%86%E3%81%8C%E3%81%8A%E3%81%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%8F%E4%BD%9C%E3%82%8C%E3%82%8B/id1059134258)). The lesson for an all-plant catalogue comes from Forks Over Knives: once every recipe is vegetarian or vegan, diet no longer divides the library. Dish form becomes the natural primary axis, and diet shrinks to sub-facets.

Meal structure explains why dish form beats Western courses in Singapore. K.C. Chang's **fan–cai** principle describes a Chinese meal as a grain staple plus accompanying dishes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Chinese_cuisine); [Asia Society](https://asiasociety.org/node/1129)). Fuchsia Dunlop describes those dishes as existing to "send the rice down" ([Asian Review of Books](https://asianreviewofbooks.com/invitation-to-a-banquet-the-story-of-chinese-food-by-fuchsia-dunlop/)). Singapore's economy rice follows the same pattern: **steamed rice with two to four homestyle dishes** ([Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/popular-culture/singaporean-chinese-hawker-food-history-and-evolution/)). Home cooking also competes hard with hawker food. The 2025 National Nutrition Survey found **7 in 10 residents eat out daily, nine times a week, up from eight** ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/national-health-surveys-show-singaporeans-are-embracing-healthier-lifestyles--with-a-need-to-improve-our-diet-quality-/)). A home cook choosing what to make is therefore asking "a one-dish meal, or something to go with rice?" rather than "a main or a side?". Breakfast is a culturally distinct set, and FairPrice isolates it ([FairPrice](https://www.fairprice.com.sg/recipes)). Splitting lunch from dinner would add nothing, since FairPrice itself merges them as "Lunch/Dinner".

The product implemented this logic directly. [`src/types/food.ts`](../../../src/types/food.ts) defines the seven categories, and [`CookCategories.tsx`](../../../src/components/recipes/CookCategories.tsx) renders them with All as one radio group in a horizontal row. The [Filters screen](../../../src/screens/food/CollectionFilters.tsx) has three groups. Diet holds Vegan and Allium-free as independent toggles. Time holds Any, Under 15 and Under 30 min as a radio group. More holds One pot and Saved only. Every choice saves immediately and narrows results with AND logic ([`recipe-browse.ts`](../../../src/utils/recipe-browse.ts)). A sort chip cycles through Best fit, Quickest and Recently cooked. Each recipe carries one or two categories.

Rice with lauk (side dishes) and rice with curries are assumed to mirror fan–cai, but nothing in this research verified that, and no Singapore study documents home-meal composition by community.

## The adopted row clears the structure test but not the content test

Structurally, the row holds up. Baymard finds horizontal toolbars unsuitable beyond **6–8 filter types** ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/horizontal-filtering-sorting-design)). NN/g finds that **most people stop after 3–4 carousel pages**, and that a cut-off final item is a strong "more" cue while dots are weak ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-carousels/)). Both are e-commerce or general mobile evidence applied by analogy; no recipe-app study of chip counts exists. The row scrolls horizontally with no indicator, so the clipped last chip acts as the cue. Each chip carries an icon, so the row should be checked at 320 pt with enlarged text to confirm that "Desserts & drinks" stays reachable in a few swipes; this research did not measure it.

The selection model matches the tested guidance. Baymard found participants were **"far less likely to use filters"** when options were mutually exclusive, and recommends OR within a filter type and AND between types ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/allow-applying-of-multiple-filter-values)). The category row is navigation, so single-select is right there. The one radio group in Filters, Time, is also correct, because the thresholds nest: everything under 15 minutes is also under 30. The multi-tag rule works as intended. **Five of 18 entries carry two categories**: both laksas (Noodles + Soups), nasi lemak (Rice meals + Breakfast), fried bee hoon (Noodles + Breakfast) and kaya toast (Breakfast + Snacks & kueh). No dish is forced into an artificial single home.

The content is where it fails. NN/g warns that faceted navigation needs metadata for every facet across current and future content, and that filters help only when they match dimensions users care about ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/filters-vs-facets/)). The library has **18 entries, 16 distinct dishes plus 2 allium-free variations**. Two chips open on two recipes each, and both labels promise something absent. "Snacks & kueh" holds popiah and kaya toast but no kueh, even though kueh is a recognised genre with its own heritage book of 100-plus recipes ([Seabreeze Books](https://www.seabreezebooks.com.sg/products/the-way-of-kueh-savouring-saving-singapores-heritage-desserts)). "Desserts & drinks" holds two desserts and no drink. "Soups" has two distinct dishes, one of them laksa counted twice through its variation. "Under 15 min" matches **two recipes** (the oat porridge and the kangkung). A chip that opens onto two recipes reads as an empty shelf. The fix is content, not UI: add kueh, tong sui or drinks, and sub-15-minute dishes before adding any new control.

Two diet facets behave very differently. **All 18 bundled recipes are vegan, so the Vegan toggle filters nothing today**: Forks Over Knives' lesson in miniature. It becomes useful only once egg- or dairy-containing vegetarian recipes enter the library, and they should, because the audience is both vegetarian and vegan. Allium-free does real work. Three recipes declare garlic, onion or shallots, and the compound-ingredient uncertainty rule can exclude more, so at most 15 of 18 pass. Allium-free also has the strongest local case of any facet. Many Singapore Buddhists avoid the **wu xin (five pungent): garlic, onions, chives, leeks and shallots**. Some keep this strictly; others only on the 1st and 15th of the lunar month ([Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/popular-culture/from-temples-to-shopping-malls-a-century-of-chinese-vegetarian-food-in-singapore/)). The Nine Emperor Gods festival requires a strict vegetarian diet that excludes the same vegetables ([Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/social-practices/the-nine-emperor-gods-festival-in-singapore/)). FairPrice exposes no vegetarian filter at all ([FairPrice](https://www.fairprice.com.sg/recipes)). The closest precedent is Indian, where sites run Jain and "No Onion No Garlic" categories ([Tarla Dalal](https://www.tarladalal.com/recipes-for-veg-recipes-jain-34?page=11); [Piping Pot Curry](https://pipingpotcurry.com/category/recipes/no-onion-no-garlic-jain-recipes/)).

|---|---|---|---|
| Vegan | Toggle | Diet sub-facet in an all-plant catalogue ([Forks Over Knives](https://www.forksoverknives.com/recipes/)) | 18 of 18 (no effect) |
| Allium-free | Toggle | Wu xin and Nine Emperor Gods practice ([Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/social-practices/the-nine-emperor-gods-festival-in-singapore/)) | At most 15 of 18 |

Two adopted choices deserve scrutiny, and one deserves defence. **Baymard found 61% of sites do not promote filters**, although most test participants first looked for filters on a list page, and promoted filters save mobile users "one trip to the separate filtering interface" ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/promoting-product-filters)). The adopted root promotes none. Its second row holds My Recipes and Shopping, and the Filters icon shows only a count badge. The project's layout direction keeps secondary options in Filters, so promotion is a tension to test, not an obvious fix; Under 30 and Allium-free are the natural candidates. Deferring cuisine, occasion, main ingredient, egg-free and gluten-free is defensible. With 18 recipes, most of these would return one or two matches, and personal exclusions such as egg, gluten or peanuts already drive the computed fit badge and the Best fit sort. The `cuisine` field, however, still mixes dish type, heritage and region ("Bowls", "Local", "Asian", "Malay", "Peranakan", "Sichuan"). It must be normalised before it can become a facet.

Two small fixes would harden the scheme. First, the boundary between "Rice meals" and "With rice" currently follows an implicit rule. If the recipe cooks or assembles the rice and the dish is eaten alone, it is a Rice meal: miso bowl, sesame greens & tofu, claypot rice, nasi lemak. If the recipe makes a dish served beside separately cooked rice, it is With rice: kangkung, sweet & sour tofu, mapo tofu. That rule should be written into the authoring guidance, because removing a single "cooked brown rice" line would move sesame greens & tofu across the boundary. Second, "Under 15 min" includes the 15-minute porridge because the comparison is "less than or equal". Either relabel it "15 min or less" or make the comparison strict.

## Three open questions need Singapore users, not more desk research

**The "With rice" label is the riskiest word on the screen.** No study was found of Singapore users' mental model for "With rice" versus "Dishes", "Mains" or "Lauk & sides". Desk research cannot settle it. The right instrument is a closed card sort followed by a first-click test on a phone-sized prototype. Recruit across Chinese, Malay and Indian households, and use dishes from all three repertoires plus deliberate boundary cases: chap chye, sayur lodeh, dhal, sambal tempeh, rice bowls, nasi lemak and laksa. Test each label variant with separate participants. Adopt the label with the highest first-click success on boundary dishes, and fall back to the neutral "Dishes" if none wins clearly. The same study would test the unverified assumption that rice-with-lauk and rice-with-curries households read the category the way fan–cai predicts.

**Festival facets belong in a seasonal row, not the permanent chip row.** The precedents are consistent. FairPrice runs "Occasions" covering Chinese New Year, Hari Raya, Deepavali, National Day and Christmas ([FairPrice](https://www.fairprice.com.sg/recipes)), and Singaporean Malaysian Recipes has a "Festive Recipes" menu ([SMR](https://www.singaporeanmalaysianrecipes.com/)). Vegan Recipe Club browses by season and celebration ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1333585160)), and Xiachufang surfaces seasonal suggestions by solar term (节气) ([App Store CN](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/id460979760)). The vegetarian-specific occasions matter most for QuinTaste. The Nine Emperor Gods festival in the ninth lunar month is described as "the largest Chinese religious festival in Singapore" ([Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/social-practices/the-nine-emperor-gods-festival-in-singapore/)), and this year's runs to **18 October 2026** ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/11/meat-free-fare-at-festival-food-fair)). That is tomorrow relative to this report, which shows how much lead time seasonal content needs. Chap chye is a "must-have" for some Singapore families at Lunar New Year ([Language Council](https://languagecouncils.sg/mandarin/ch/learning-resources/singaporean-mandarin-database/terms/chap-chye)). Mid-Autumn and Vesak are plausible additions but had no recipe-site precedent.

The implementation follows from that evidence. Add an `occasions` field. Show a seasonal collection row on Cook browse, or a temporary Filters value, only during each window and only once enough tagged recipes exist to fill it. Prioritise Lunar New Year and Nine Emperor Gods, because both pair a calendar moment with allium-free eating.

**Time-of-day suggestions have no user-study support, so they must be dismissible suggestions, never defaults.** Time-aware recipe recommenders are evaluated mostly offline ([IJEECS](https://ijeecs.iaescore.com/index.php/IJEECS/article/view/35618); [Oulu](https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/handle/10024/31845)). One 2012 user study found that day of the week affects how people rate dinner recommendations ([LIFESTYLE 2012](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-891/LIFESTYLE2012_paper2.pdf)). A review concludes that it is still not understood which context variables matter most ([Trattner & Elsweiler](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.02760)). Practice exists without evidence: Tasty runs an editorial "Right Now" group that includes breakfasts ([Tasty](https://tasty.co/)), and Kurashiru frames menus by breakfast, lunch and dinner. Pre-selecting the Breakfast chip in the morning would do concrete harm in QuinTaste. Browse state saves immediately per owner, so an auto-selected chip would overwrite the user's own choice and hide **14 of 18 recipes** without telling them. A dismissible "Breakfast ideas" row above the results, with the dismissal remembered, keeps the user in control. The same pattern fits an "Allium-free today" suggestion on the lunar 1st and 15th.

Every question here, and the media question below, runs into one structural fact: **QuinTaste ships no analytics SDK and has no recipe backend**. Passive A/B measurement is therefore unavailable by design. Evidence has to come from moderated sessions or from an opt-in local event log that participants export deliberately. The cooked log already records each Finish per recipe and revision on the device, which makes it a ready-made outcome measure.

## Video wins the feed, while cooks work from text and stills

Video's track record is in reach and acquisition, not in cooking. Tasty's main Facebook page drew nearly **1.7 billion video views in September 2016** ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/just-15-months-old-tasty-driving-buzzfeed-video-facebook/)). Cooking-video views on Facebook then flattened as the platform's algorithm changed, which shows the boom was a distribution effect rather than a durable format advantage ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebooks-food-videos-show-declining-viewership/510086/)). Japan provides the strongest video case. Kurashiru reports **more than 44 million cumulative downloads** ([dely disclosure](https://finance-frontend-pc-dist.west.edge.storage-yahoo.jp/disclosure/20250526/20250526565424.pdf)), and DELISH KITCHEN claims the top user count among Japanese recipe services, based on figures it does not publish ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tv.every.delishkitchen&hl=en)). Analysts link Cookpad's decline to a shift from "search and read" to "watch on video", but they also blame a 2016 management dispute ([note.com](https://note.com/ccre1018/n/n4342c2d9ce31?hl=en)), and the video apps' rise was backed by heavy marketing ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jsimms/2019/09/04/from-app-to-table-japanese-food-app-kurashiru-might-soon-deliver-meal-kits/)). **No public A/B test or cohort study compares retention or cooking for photo-led versus video-led recipe pages.**

Where people actually get recipes still favours written sources. A 2026 US survey of 2,008 people found **food blogs and websites at 48%, YouTube at 40% and TikTok at 22%** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/food-blogs-beat-ai-for-recipes-what-a-2026-study-found/)). Instacart's 2025 survey ranks friends and family first at 41%, ahead of YouTube at 36% ([Instacart](https://company.instacart.com/ideas/cooking-at-home-survey)). Singapore has no survey splitting recipe use by format. Its health authority, however, offers "Written" and "Video" tabs side by side rather than choosing one ([HealthHub](https://www.healthhub.sg/programmes/nutrition-hub/healthy-recipes)). An agency source reports that Lemon8, popular in Singapore, favours multi-image step posts, recipes included ([Impossible Marketing](https://www.impossible.sg/?p=83526)).

Learning research shows where video earns its cost. Höffler and Leutner's meta-analysis found animation beat static pictures overall (d = 0.37). The advantage grew for realistic video (d = 0.76) and peaked at **d = 1.06 when procedural-motor skills were taught** ([Leibniz-IPN](https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis)). Later meta-analyses put the average advantage at only **g ≈ 0.23** ([Instructional Science](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w)). A 2021 re-analysis locates the gain where the specifics of a movement must be learned ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351895693_When_learning_from_animations_is_more_successful_than_learning_from_static_pictures_learning_the_specifics_of_change)). Static images with motion cues can match animation for procedural tasks, and fewer well-chosen pictures beat many ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223831054_Using_video_and_static_pictures_to_improve_learning_of_procedural_contents)). The cooking studies point the same way. In Surgenor et al.'s experiment, video plus a printed recipe raised self-efficacy, yet participants said **a sequence of images showing each stage** would have helped more than text ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315741906_The_impact_of_video_technology_on_learning_A_cooking_skills_experiment)). Kaesberg's five-week study found **no significant difference** between recipe cards and cards plus video, and no change in meals cooked per week ([ISU thesis](https://ir.library.illinoisstate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2192&context=etd); [JACH](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07448481.2021.1978462)). A 2025 pilot of short videos found no significant gain in produce-prep comfort ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707162/)). No randomised trial measures actual dish success by format.

Kitchen behaviour reinforces this. Cooks following instructional video **pause repeatedly** to keep pace with their hands ([CHI 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3313831.3376759)). A CHI 2019 study catalogued seven navigation intents, including pace-control pauses and replay jumps to check a mistake ([CHI 2019](https://kixlab.github.io/website-files/2019/chi2019-VoiceVideoNavigation-paper.pdf)). Participants in a later workshop study valued navigating by action unit ([CHI 2022 workshop](https://kixlab.github.io/website-files/2022/chi2022-workshop-StarLab-paper.pdf)). Even the video-first apps converge on step-addressable media with text. DELISH KITCHEN's kitchen mode repeats each step's short clip alongside the instructions and swipes between steps ([App Store story](https://apps.apple.com/jp/iphone/story/id1254075994)). Tasty drew complaints when fast "TikTok-style cuts" made cooking stages harder to see ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tasty/id1217456898)). A whole app category exists to turn social video back into text: Pestle saves "a clean recipe card" with the video link kept ([Pestle](https://pestleai.com/)), and caption-dependent importers fail when a recipe is only shown or spoken ([Ladle](https://getladle.com/best/apps-to-save-tiktok-and-instagram-recipes)). Sound cannot carry essentials: **69% watch video with sound off in public**, and 80% are more likely to finish a video when captions exist ([3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/)).

## Photo-led now, technique clips only after a measured test

Video's costs land squarely on QuinTaste's constraints. Under WCAG 2.2, prerecorded video needs captions ([SC 1.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html)) and an audio description or a media alternative such as full text steps ([SC 1.2.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-description-or-media-alternative-prerecorded.html)). Auto-starting motion longer than five seconds needs pause, stop or hide controls ([SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). Two 2025 studies with blind and low-vision cooks had to pre-process recipe video into structured steps before it was usable ([AROMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10963); [Vid2Coach](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00717)).

Size is the sharper limit. At an assumed 1.5 Mbps, a 15-second step clip is about 2.8 MB. That is **roughly 26–34 times one of the new 83–109 KB recipe JPEGs**, and nearly double the 1,500,000-byte guard on a user's entire local FoodDocument. Creator video therefore cannot live in the local document at all. Bundled clips would need streaming, a CDN and a separate cache, with explicit download consent for offline use. User-generated video would also need moderation, but the creator flow deliberately ends at "Ready to submit · Not sent". Bandwidth is not the obstacle: Singapore's median mobile download speed reached **164.75 Mbps at end-2025** ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-singapore)). The obstacles are cost, storage, accessibility and review. A video player would also add a dependency the project lacks (`expo-video` is not installed), which must be checked against the SDK 57 documentation first.

The photo side has gaps of its own. Nine of the 18 entries show illustrative hero photos generated from recorded prompts, with provenance in `assets/food/provenance.json` and an "Illustrative image" label in Recipe info. The other nine (eight distinct dishes) still show a "No photo yet" category tile pending [the photo prompts](../../RECIPE_PHOTO_PROMPTS.md). The three original hero images ship as **2.3–2.6 MB PNGs**, about 25 times the size of the newer JPEGs. Re-encoding them as the newer photos were would cut roughly 7 MB from the bundle. Bundled steps carry no media; only private recipes support step photos.

A dish photo is a key recipe-card feature ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707162/)), and a labelled illustrative image can serve recognition and appetite. **Step media that teaches doneness or technique must be a real photograph of the recipe being cooked.** A generated still can show a plausible golden colour, but it cannot show that this recipe reaches it at this heat and time. Cookpad already deletes AI-generated recipe images ([Cookpad Malaysia](https://cookpad.com/my/community)).

| Step type | Example | Medium |
|---|---|---|
| Additive or sequencing (add, stir, simmer) | Simmering ABC soup | Text, with an optional still |
| End-state judgement a still can show | Tofu crisped golden; sambal cooked until the oil separates | Real step photo |
| Motion is the skill | Rolling popiah, folding kueh, pleating dumplings, kneading | 2–4-frame technique strip first; clip only if the strip fails in testing |
| Temporal cue | How fast to stir; when to flip | Clip candidate |

The rollout follows from that table. First, complete hero coverage. Second, shoot real step stills during a test cook of each recipe; the same session also checks the `minutes` values behind the time filters. This needs an optional `photo` field on bundled steps. Third, add 2–4-frame technique strips with motion arrows for popiah, kueh and pleating, the cheap middle ground that the procedural-task research supports. Fourth, and only for techniques where strips demonstrably fail, add 5–15-second clips that follow strict rules:

- silent by default, captioned, with quantities and times shown as text beside them;
- a poster frame, tap-to-play under reduced motion and a visible pause control;
- looping on the current step;
- never autoplaying in browse;
- streamed rather than stored in FoodDocument or AsyncStorage, and excluded from offline Saved unless the user downloads them explicitly;
- editorial-only until a review service exists.

One continuous montage should never be the cooking interface.

### Validation runs on studies because the app has no telemetry

The clip decision needs a within-subject, counterbalanced cook-along on 3–5 technique recipes, comparing strip against clip. Where possible, run it in participants' own kitchens, as the Vid2Coach and AROMA studies did. Measure:

- errors at the technique step and time on that step;
- replays;
- self-efficacy;
- whether participants reach Finish;
- the finished dish, rated blind from photos.

Supplement this with an opt-in exported log of clip plays per step and data per session. Fix the decision rule before running the study: a clip ships for a technique only if it beats the strip by a pre-set margin on technique-step errors. Otherwise the strip stays.

| Decision | Method | Primary measure | Ship rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| "With rice" label | Closed card sort, then first-click test, with Chinese, Malay and Indian participants | First-click success on boundary dishes | Adopt the best-performing label; fall back to "Dishes" |
| Promote Under 30 / Allium-free | Moderated browse tasks, root screen with and without promoted chips | Time to target recipe; Filters opens | Promote only if task time falls and the 320 pt root stays uncluttered |
| Time-of-day row | Opt-in field log over at least two weeks | Taps vs dismissals; row-opened recipes that reach Finish | Keep only if taps exceed dismissals |
| Festival row | Run during the Lunar New Year 2027 window | Row taps; cooked-log Finishes | Keep as seasonal only, never a permanent chip |
| Strip vs clip | Counterbalanced cook-along on 3–5 technique recipes | Technique-step errors, replays, self-efficacy | Clip per technique only above the pre-registered margin |

## Conclusion

Both decisions turn out to be content problems disguised as interface questions. The chip row is structurally right, but each chip is only as honest as the recipes behind it. "Photo-led" is only as credible as the step photos are real. One production habit resolves both: test-cook every new recipe while photographing its steps. That fills the thin chips, verifies the minutes behind the time filters, produces genuine step stills and yields the raw frames for technique strips, so growing the library and validating the media become the same workstream. The order of work is therefore content before controls: kueh, drinks, vegetarian egg or dairy dishes and quick recipes come first, then promoted filters, festival rows or a cuisine facet.

Video should be treated as a per-technique exception that earns its place by measurably reducing errors, not as a format choice. Japan's video leaders show that video buys attention with spending QuinTaste does not have, while the learning evidence gives video a decisive edge only where the hand motion is the lesson. Local-first architecture means every remaining question, from the "With rice" label to promoted filters, time-of-day rows and clips, has to be answered by deliberately designed studies rather than passive analytics. That is slower, but consistent with the app's privacy promise. The largest unknowns remain:

- no Singapore data on home-meal composition by community;
- no recipe-app study on chip counts or time-of-day defaults;
- no randomised trial measuring dish success by media format.
