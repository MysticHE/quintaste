# Rebuild Social Around Diet-Aware Small Tables

QuinTaste's people-first Social loop already matches where the 2025–26 friendship and social-dining field has converged. A member opts in, sends a short introduction, waits for acceptance, chats, and then has every member confirm a public plan; the field has settled on **groups of 4–6** (Timeleft's matched tables, Pie's circles of six, Bumble's move from 1:1 swipes to Groups), on limiting first contact until the recipient accepts, and on one shared plan object with RSVPs and reminders. Its best-known failures mark the edges of the risk: **IRL shut down after about 95% of its claimed users proved to be bots**, and **Geneva had earned no revenue** when Bumble folded it into the relaunched BFF app. No product reviewed shares structured diet or avoided-ingredient information with tablemates or supports five-pungent and lunar-day practice, yet QuinTaste records avoided ingredients and never shows them. The current screens hide these strengths. The first viewport is mostly empty space around a sample-profile link, a declined invitation leaves a group still labelled "Awaiting reply" (B1, confirmed), and an over-capacity guest merge fails with a misleading "could not be read" error (B2, confirmed but milder than the audit stated); ten further defects remain reported but unverified, one stale URL parameter was observed live, and a 17-screen legacy Tables stack is still compiled. Twelve "Now" changes need no backend and keep every local-only honesty label: truthful states, a compact first screen, diet compatibility with a computed "table needs" summary, named confirmations, plan-change system lines, share-my-plan and a Singapore safety page. What gates production is operations, not code. Since 29 June 2026, Singapore's Online Safety Commission has let harassment victims escalate when a platform has not responded adequately within 24 hours, so a staffed report intake, an adult check, PDPA controls and home-dining declarations must exist before real strangers meet.

## Small tables won; IRL and Geneva show the two ways to fail

The field spent 2025–26 converging on the format QuinTaste chose. Bumble relaunched its friendship product on 18 September 2025 as a free, group- and community-first app built on Geneva. It shut Geneva down and migrated that app's groups and messages into the new one ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)). Pie dropped its original one-to-one matching quiz in November 2024, arguing that friendships form in groups ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/19/irl-social-app-pie-is-coming-to-sf-to-make-you-less-lonely)). By 2026 it was sorting each event's RSVPs into **circles of six with a pre-event group chat** ([TechCrunch, Apr 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/)). Washed Up, a 2026 Los Angeles entrant, has users request to join a small-group plan and then chat with the group ([TechCrunch, Apr 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/)). That is the closest live analogue to QuinTaste's invite-accept-chat loop.

The two well-known failures point to opposite risks. IRL raised more than $200M and shut down in June 2023 after a board investigation found **about 95% of its roughly 20M claimed users were bots** ([Yahoo Finance/TechCrunch](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/unicorn-social-app-irl-shut-204818996.html)). Geneva built real communities but had **generated no revenue as of 30 June 2025** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)). Synthetic social proof destroys trust; a free community with no business model eventually disappears. QuinTaste is exposed to the first risk through its fictional sample people and to the second through free gatherings.

### Timeleft and Singapore pricing

Timeleft is the reference for dinner with strangers, and it already operates in Singapore. Its first Singapore dinner ran on 3 July 2024, and Singapore and Malaysia were its first Asian markets ([FMT](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2024/08/08/heres-to-chilling-while-dining-with-total-strangers)). The model has four parts. Members take a personality quiz and are matched on personality, interests and age, not photos or bios ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6466442949)). Tables are scheduled at six to absorb no-shows. Dinners run on Wednesdays. Each diner pays for their own meal ([SG Magazine, Sep 2024](https://sgmagazine.com/lifestyle/i-had-dinner-with-strangers-using-timeleft-app-it-might-be-the-cure-for-the-loneliness-epidemic-in-singapore/)).

Singapore pricing in September 2024 was **S$19.98 for a single dinner, S$149 a month or S$499 for six months**. Members were banned after two unannounced absences ([SG Magazine](https://sgmagazine.com/lifestyle/i-had-dinner-with-strangers-using-timeleft-app-it-might-be-the-cure-for-the-loneliness-epidemic-in-singapore/)). The Singapore App Store now lists in-app purchases at **S$24.90, S$48.98 and S$63.98** but gives no durations, and shows a 4.4 rating from only **139 ratings** ([App Store SG](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/timeleft-make-new-friends-irl/id6466442949)). Current Singapore pricing is therefore unconfirmed, and the local user base looks modest.

Timeleft deliberately avoids pre-meal group chat. Its guide says there is no group chat to manage beforehand; connection requests come after dinner ([Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/blog/how-does-timeleft-work/)). Its safety design is structural rather than verification-heavy. Events are never held in private homes ([Timeleft safety tips](https://timeleft.com/safety-guidelines/)), a person you report is never placed with you again, and banned users get a 30-day appeal ([Timeleft Safety Framework](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-framework/)). Attendance is enforced with money: a no-show is charged **35% of the monthly fee** ([Timeleft help](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/25012703925148-What-Is-the-No-Show-Penalty-Fee)).

The complaints concern billing and table makeup. Reviewers report being the only woman at a table and being seated with much older guests ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/timeleft.com)). Tables of three have sat down where six were booked ([Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1859085/dinner-parties-with-strangers-timeleft)), and one Singapore reviewer found reintroducing herself every week tiring ([Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/?p=867937)).

### Partiful, Luma and Pie

Free invite tools make money from hosts, not guests. **Partiful** stayed free until it added ticketing on 2 June 2026 ([PR Newswire](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260602ny74669/partiful-launches-ticketing-bringing-paid-events-into-the-social-platform-where-people-already-make-plans)) and launched Partiful Plus on 14 October 2026, US-only with no published price. Plus's "Peek" feature lets directly invited guests preview the guest list and the neighbourhood, not the address, before they RSVP ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/partiful-launches-partiful-plus-subscription-with-new-customization-features-302907923.html)). In January 2026 Partiful also added "Crush", an anonymous dating pick among people you have attended events with ([Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/partifuls-crush-tool-brings-dating-style-matching-to-event-app/)), a drift toward dating that QuinTaste should not copy.

**Luma** keeps free events free and charges 5% plus Stripe fees on paid tickets, or US$59–69 a month for Plus ([Luma Help](https://help.luma.com/p/luma-vs-meetup)). It approves guests per ticket type, promotes waitlisted guests by hand, and sends automatic reminders **one day and one hour before** an event, but never to pending guests ([Luma Help](https://help.luma.com/p/update-to-reminders-on-luma)). **Pie** relaunched on 25 August 2026 as "Community Homes" and has about 300k users. When its feed began showing which of your connections were attending events, **time in app rose from about 2 to about 10 minutes** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/andy-dunns-startup-pie-becomes-less-of-an-events-app-and-more-of-a-social-network/)). Social proof works, which is exactly why it has to be real.

### Singapore's plant-based scene

Singapore has no marketplace for plant-based social dining, only individual hosts and fragile community groups. Neighbourhood Supper Club is a vegan home supper club: S$88 for eight courses, capped at 12 diners in tables of six, with sessions selling out within minutes through Telegram ([Eatbook](https://eatbook.sg/neighbourhood-supper-club/)). The Singapore Vegetarian Meetup Group has **5,473 members**. On 17 October 2026 it listed no upcoming events, and recent events were cancelled with one or two RSVPs ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/singapore-vegetarian/)). Demand exists, but a low-friction, small-group format is missing.

## QuinTaste fully covers 10 of 33 benchmark capabilities and leads where rivals are silent

The comparison below uses the 17 October code audit (static and not executed; file:line references to `src/`), a coordinator walkthrough of the live production app on 17 October at 375 px wide, and a re-read of the Social screens for this report. QuinTaste's design sits in a specific place in the field. Like Bumble BFF and Peanut, it shows browsable profiles before a meeting. Like Pie's circles and Washed Up, it opens chat inside a small group. Like Partiful, the person who starts the group chooses who is in it. Browsable profiles raise the safety bar compared with Timeleft's blind tables. QuinTaste offsets that with structure: groups of 2, 4 or 6, chat only after acceptance, a public meeting place, a plan every member must confirm, and permanent separation after a block.

## Twelve local changes make the loop legible before any backend

Every "Now" item below writes through `useMeet().commit` functional updates inside the owner-scoped FoodDocument and stays under its 1.5 MB guard. Each keeps the "Not sent", "Not approved", "No one is contacted" and "On this device" labels, uses semantic tokens from `src/theme/tokens.ts` with no hard-coded colours or directional accent borders, and preserves 48-unit targets, groups of 2/4/6 and gatherings of 2–30. Each must pass `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npx expo lint`, re-run the `social-distill` browser checks and update `docs/PRD_COVERAGE.md` rows QT-021/023/025/026. None of it establishes that the app works on a physical device.

### Redesign proposal: every screen should answer "what happens next?"

#### Information architecture: keep three segments and give each one job

Keep Social as one dock destination: a titleless root, one toolbar (search, Inbox, Me) and one People / Events / My plans segmented row. Design guidance disagrees on where segmented controls end and tabs begin. One guide treats a segmented control as switching content within a single view ([SaaS UI Design](https://www.saasui.design/blog/saas-tabs-segmented-control-ux-patterns)), while Trimble recommends tabs for navigating between content ([Trimble](https://styles.trimble.com/components/mobile/segmented-controls/)). The practical test is whether each segment keeps its own scroll, search and filters. Today the query resets on every switch (`Home.tsx:21`), so the current radiogroup semantics are defensible; if segments later keep independent state, they should expose tab semantics instead.

Each segment should have one job. **People** is for choosing whom to invite, and **Events** is for gatherings with their own category chips. **My plans** should become a timeline. Today it is grouped by record type (`Plans.tsx:18-26`); instead it should have headed sections for Needs you (confirm or re-confirm, unsaved applications), Upcoming (confirmed, sorted by date), Drafts & applications, and Past. These are headings rather than filter tabs, so the rule of no extra tabs on My plans still holds. **Inbox** becomes the single home for conversations, split into Waiting, Chats and Closed.

#### First viewport: one status row, one honest sentence, two actions

| Zone (375 wide) | Today (live, 17 Oct) | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Toolbar and segments | Search · Inbox · Me; People / Events / My plans plus filter | Unchanged; the filter highlight reflects only real filters (N3) |
| Status | "Good Company Starts With Hello" empty-state card with Set up profile | One 48-unit row: initial badge, "Profile 2 of 4 · Not open to plans", Finish. Guests see "Try a local profile" with the return destination preserved |

The aim is that every element in the first viewport is either a fact or an action. That follows the cold-start guidance that empty states should offer concrete next steps rather than a blank list. Fictional people must never count toward any activity or "who's going" indicator ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/85835)).

#### Profile card: logistics and diet before personality

The card reads in this order: name, then diet plus practice chips, then neighbourhood, then availability with an overlap line ("Weekday dinners · matches you"), then preferred group size, a one-line About, at most three interests, and "Avoids … · self-declared".

There is no photo-first layout. The evidence favours activity- and logistics-led connection: older adults in a loneliness-app study wanted features for doing things together ([Exeter](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/132353?show=full)), and Timeleft matches without photos or bios ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6466442949)). The initial-only `PersonBadge` stays, using `theme.accentSoft` and `theme.accent`.

On the Person page, choosing a group of 2 shows a one-line tip: "Meet somewhere busy and public; a group of 4 is easier for a first meal." The 2-person option stays. The framing keeps it clear of one-to-one dating ([Timeleft positioning](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2024/08/08/heres-to-chilling-while-dining-with-total-strangers)).

The plan card is pinned at the top of the chat, replacing today's "Our Plan" link row. It shows the "Table needs" summary, for example "Vegan ×2 · Vegetarian ×1 · avoid garlic, onion (Mei)". That summary is the differentiator. It turns the existing free-text "Food Arrangements & Dietary Needs" field (`GroupTools.tsx:20`) into a starting point everyone can see, instead of a blank box.

Chat should stop forcing a scroll on every content change (`Chat.tsx:20`). It should mark new arrivals as a log wherever React Native Web supports log semantics, and test them with TalkBack and VoiceOver. The accessibility guidance is not to auto-scroll a user who is reading older messages ([azukiazusa](https://azukiazusa.dev/en/blog/accessible-streaming-chat-ui)).

#### Events: label what is local and archive what is past

Events keeps its three contextual categories in the URL. It labels the local list honestly ("Your gatherings · On this device"). Classes and Home dining keep their Apply buttons, with copy that says approval is not connected. In the gathering form, seats become a 2–30 stepper and payment becomes a "Who pays" field. Past gatherings move to the Past section of My plans with Duplicate. Gatherings over six seats get seating guidance (Next).

#### Safety surfaces: one tap from every person, group and chat

Safety & help is a static page linked from four places. The report flow gets lighter while staying protective by default: "Also block" is pre-ticked, and blocking still closes shared groups permanently. The plan form's "Public Meeting Place" label gains one helper suggestion, such as a hawker centre or food court. Hawker centres are public, busy and pay-per-dish, and Singapore's hawker culture is UNESCO-listed ([NEA](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/hawker-culture-is-singapore-s-first-inscription-on-unesco-s-representative-list-of-the-intangible-cultural-heritage-of-humanity)).

## Singapore adds a 24-hour clock, a home-kitchen rulebook and a lunar calendar

The local audience is clear. Singaporeans aged 21–34 report the highest loneliness of any age group in an IPS poll (a mean score of **5.53 against 5.03 for those aged 50–64**). **53%** of that group find it easier to talk to people online, and **56%** sometimes feel anxious talking in person ([IPS/TODAY](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/today_youths-in-singapore-more-likely-to-report-higher-levels-of-social-isolation-and-loneliness-ips-poll_20240129.pdf)). Yet **93.0%** of respondents met all their close friends in person ([IPS 2026](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/sp2026-press-release_160126.pdf)). For this group, chat before the meal lowers the barrier, while the meal itself is what builds friendship.

Repetition matters. Among Singaporeans aged 60 and over, only participation at least weekly was linked to a **19.5-percentage-point lower probability of loneliness**, and only **6.0%** participated that often ([Duke-NUS](https://academic.oup.com/psychsocgerontology/article/80/6/gbaf039/8037582)). Single-person households aged 65 and over doubled from 42,100 to 87,200 between 2014 and 2024 ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/addressing-loneliness-and-psychological-distress-among-seniors-living-alone/)). Singapore ranked **34th of 46** for how easily expatriates find friends ([InterNations via The Independent](https://theindependent.sg/4-southeast-asian-countries-are-friendliest-to-expats-but-singapore-isn-t-one-of-them/)).

The plant-based pool is about **5% fully vegetarian or vegan, plus 18% flexitarian** ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/articles/47585-world-food-day-singapore-major-dietary-preferences-most-favourite-cuisines-2023)). On top of that are Buddhists (31.1% of residents) and Taoists (8.8%) ([SingStat](https://singstat.gov.sg/-/media/files/news/press16062021.ashx)), many of whom keep five-pungent or lunar-day practice. A recurring, small-group, diet-aware format addresses all three groups: young adults, seniors and newcomers.

Before any real stranger meets another through QuinTaste, eight things must exist. The app needs **authenticated accounts** with a verified phone or email and an adult check, and a **staffed report intake** that can decide harassment and stalking reports within 24 hours and issue server-timestamped receipts. It needs a written **enforcement ladder** with appeals and a crisis protocol, and **server-side block, report and separation** that apply across devices. It must be able to carry out **OSC takedown, restriction and identity-disclosure directions**. It needs a **PDPA programme** covering notices, retention, deletion and a 3-day breach plan, a **staffed host review** for paid home dining and classes, and **acceptance testing on physical devices**. None of these exists today, which is why every current Social surface must keep saying "Not sent" and "No one is contacted".

## Lean into shared diets and pre-meal chat; refuse swipes, boosts and synthetic crowds

**Diet and allergen information shared with tablemates** is the clearest gap in the field. Timeleft asks about diet when you book, then tells members to check the menu themselves ([Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/)). Eatwith puts the duty on hosts through its charter ([Eatwith](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/1498030-hosts-charter)). Partiful lets hosts add dietary questions to an RSVP ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/partiful)). None of them shows each tablemate's needs to the rest of the table.

QuinTaste can do this with data it already stores. It can reveal self-declared diet and avoided ingredients to accepted members only, which is the same staged disclosure Eatwith uses for addresses ([Eatwith](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/432234-how-do-i-know-if-a-guest-can-be-trusted)). It can then sum them into a "Table needs" line. Later, the owned menu catalogue in Explore can turn that line into venue choices, though suitability must stay labelled as unconfirmed. Sharing one vocabulary across Social, Cook and Explore also removes the current split between Market's list and the Preferences list.

**Five-pungent and other religious practice** is a real local tradition, not a niche preference. Explore's Allium-free keyword filter and Cook's allium group already serve it. Social should reuse them rather than invent a third model. Lunar-day and festival prompts give recurring reasons to meet, which is exactly what the Duke-NUS frequency finding says matters.

**Chat before the meal** is a deliberate counter-position to Timeleft, which explicitly has no group chat to manage beforehand ([Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/blog/how-does-timeleft-work/)). Its users complain about awkward reintroductions. QuinTaste's anxious-in-person audience prefers to warm up online first. People-nearby users in the CSCW study wanted to build trust before meeting, and most left those apps to do it ([CSCW '17](https://tawannadillahunt.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/paper427.pdf)). Pre-meal chat keeps that trust-building inside the app, where block and report already apply and production moderation can later reach it.

The **free, pay-your-own model** also positions well. Timeleft charges a platform fee before any food is bought. QuinTaste's gatherings cost nothing, and hawker centres let each person order and pay separately.

## Conclusion

QuinTaste is not short of features. Its weakness is that the state of each plan is hard to read. The loop's guards are among the strictest in the benchmark: chat only after acceptance, a full group, every member confirming, edits resetting confirmations, and a block that closes groups for good. But the screens do not tell users where a plan stands or what to do next, and in the declined case they say something false. That shifts the local roadmap away from more discovery and toward the twelve "Now" changes, which make each state visible, truthful and diet-aware. The "Table needs" summary, per-person confirmations and system lines cost little and copy nothing in the market. They are also the parts that will carry over unchanged when real people replace the fictional ones.

The cold-start evidence argues for launching Social narrow and recurring rather than city-wide. The vegetarian Meetup group's cancellations at one or two RSVPs, alongside supper clubs that sell out in minutes, suggest that people are not missing the will to meet; what is missing is a small, safe, diet-aligned format on a predictable rhythm. A production pilot built around one neighbourhood, one weekly hawker slot and real organisers, with Events supplying activity before People has density, matches both Timeleft's density lesson and Chen's atomic-network logic. Four questions remain open. No Singapore data exists on women meeting strangers through friendship or dining apps, the HDB/URA home-dining rules could not be retrieved from primary pages, it needs legal advice whether OSRAA's platform duties apply to an app of QuinTaste's size, and every behaviour described here is unverified on physical devices. The two legal questions should be settled before any Later item ships, not after.
