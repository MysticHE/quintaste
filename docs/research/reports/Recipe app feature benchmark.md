# Close the Cook-Mode Gap, Own Allium-Free

QuinTaste's Cook feature already has the parts of a 2026 cook mode that are hardest to build. Those are screen wake, one-step focus, a persisted resume point, deadline-based concurrent timers, and step-scoped ingredient quantities that follow scaling. It trails the benchmark on cheap, high-frequency utilities: ingredient check-off, kitchen-friendly fractions, a merged shopping list and a cooked log. Above all it trails on content: **3 distinct bundled recipes** against libraries of hundreds to tens of thousands. The 2025–26 field has settled into editorial libraries (NYT Cooking, Tasty), import-and-organize managers (ReciMe, Paprika, Crouton, Pestle) and diet-first planners. Over the same period, four well-known apps shut down: Allrecipes' recipe app, Food Network Kitchen, Yummly, and Mealime, which closes on **21 October 2026**. Three of these destroyed or locked users' saved data, and Mealime deletes it at closure. Ten "Now" changes would close most of the utility gap. All ten work without a backend or a new native module, and all ten respect the binding Cook constraints. QuinTaste's genuine differentiators have no direct competitor among the apps reviewed. They are a computed per-recipe fit that shows conflicts, an allium group that already matches Singapore Buddhist five-pungent practice, and local data ownership with export. The right move is to extend them with tradition presets, compound-ingredient "Unknown" rules and open-format export. They should not be diluted with AI recipe generation, ads, paywalls or unmoderated public features, which would break the "no reviewed-content claims" and local-first rules.

## Four shutdowns and a subscription reset reshaped the 2026 field

The surviving leaders split into three business models, and the money now follows utility rather than recipe access. **NYT Cooking (4.9★ from 548K ratings)** and **Tasty (4.9★ from 433K)** dominate rating volume as editorial libraries ([NYT App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nyt-cooking-recipes-tips/id911422904); [Tasty App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tasty/id1217456898)). The top-grossing Food & Drink app in the US in March 2026 was **ReciMe**, an import-and-organize tool with no editorial library. It was #1 every tracked day from 9 to 21 March ([Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/rankings/us/ios/food-drink-grossing/2026-03-21)) and charges for unlimited social imports beyond a free cap of five per week ([ReciMe](https://www.recime.app/)). In Asia, scale sits with Japanese short-video and community apps. Kurashiru has about 760K ratings, Cookpad Japan about 680K and DELISH KITCHEN about 510K, all at 4.6★ ([Cookpad App Store JP](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id340368403); [DELISH KITCHEN App Store JP](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%83%AC%E3%82%B7%E3%83%94%E5%8B%95%E7%94%BB%E3%81%A7%E6%96%99%E7%90%86%E7%8C%AE%E7%AB%8B%E3%82%92%E7%B0%A1%E5%8D%98-%E3%81%AB-%E3%83%87%E3%83%AA%E3%83%83%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A5%E3%82%AD%E3%83%83%E3%83%81%E3%83%B3/id1177907423)). Even so, Cookpad's FY2025 revenue fell **9.2%** on declining Premium subscribers ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/jpx_tdnet_140120260205549442)).

The status changes matter more to QuinTaste than the rankings, because they show users what happens to their data when a recipe app fails. Allrecipes discontinued its recipe app in March 2023. Its only current iOS listing is a magazine reader rated **1.7★**, and its reviews say saved recipes and notes were lost or locked behind the magazine paywall ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id727866441)). Food Network Kitchen closed on 1 May 2024, and users lost their diet and allergy profiles when they were moved to the website ([AllYourScreens](https://www.allyourscreens.com/100-latest-news/101-us/1513-food-network-kitchen-app-is-going-away)). Yummly went offline in December 2024, and users struggled to save their libraries ([Plan to Eat](https://www.plantoeat.com/blog/2024/12/yummly-is-closing-discover-the-best-meal-planning-alternative)). Mealime, with 4.8★ from 54K ratings, says "all personal data will be deleted" when it shuts down on 21 October 2026, and it mentions no export ([Mealime](https://www.mealime.com/closing)).

Pricing is also being reset. Paprika has long been the "buy once" benchmark, but it entered the Paprika 4 open beta on 28 September 2026 with premium features moving to a subscription ([Paprika news](https://www.paprikaapp.com/news/)). Several former reference apps now look dormant: SideChef has shipped no iOS update since March 2025 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sidechef/id905229928)), Mela none since June 2025 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mela-recipe-manager/id1548466041)), and Samsung Food's biweekly release cadence stopped after May 2026 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planner/id1133637674)).

Across all three models, the cook-mode baseline has converged. Keeping the screen awake is universal. The best-evidenced engagement signal is still Betty Crocker's correlational finding that wake-lock users had **3.1× longer sessions and 50% lower bounce** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/betty-crocker)). The 2025–26 differentiators sit on top of that baseline: ingredient check-off, tappable timers that behave like system timers, landscape split views, and step-scoped quantities. Apple News+ Food shows the amount needed "for the current step" ([Information Today](https://www.infotoday.com/IT/sep25/Pack--Culinary-Websites-Have-Been-Cooking-Up-New-Features.shtml)), while Deglaze sells AI inline quantities as a Pro feature ([Deglaze](https://www.deglaze.app/blog/stop-scrolling-while-you-cook-meet-inline-ingredients)).

The best research points in the same direction: practical failures outrank novelty. A vendor-sponsored survey of 354 cooks names "hard to find ingredients or steps" (**24.1%**), ads (**19.8%**), doneness uncertainty (**11.2%**) and losing one's place (**9.6%**) as the top frustrations ([Allspice Labs](https://www.allspicelabs.com/studies/home-cooks/)). The CHI '24 Best Paper found that kitchen voice assistants fail mainly because they lose task context ([UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10189212)).

Singapore has no dominant local recipe app, and its cooks are time-poor. Cookpad's global app has only **221 ratings** in the Singapore store ([App Store SG](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/cookpad-find-share-recipes/id585332633)), and FairPrice's app is a grocery app rather than a recipe app. An IKEA survey found only **22% of Singapore households cooked at home almost every day**, against 49% in London, Paris and Shanghai. Yet in 2024, **37% preferred a home-cooked meal**, against a 28% global average ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/how-meal-deliveries-in-singapore-are-evolving-as-home-cooking-declines/)). The IKEA figure dates from 2015.

Local sources point to a clear recipe format: short and small. FairPrice's filters lead with "Under 15 mins / Under 30 mins / One Pot Meal" ([FairPrice](https://www.fairprice.com.sg/recipes/)). HPB's localized recipes take 25–45 minutes and serve 2 ([HealthHub](https://www.healthhub.sg/programmes/healthy-ageing/eating-healthy/library-of-recipes)). None of the local or regional recipe resources reviewed exposes a five-pungent-free filter.

The totals are **17 Has, 7 Partial, 19 Missing** out of 43 capabilities. What QuinTaste has is concentrated in the expensive layers, and that sets the strategy.

QuinTaste is ahead of the field in three places. It gives **step-scoped scaled quantities** away free, where Deglaze sells an AI version behind Pro and a 2023 critique of NYT Cooking faulted its steps view for omitting quantities entirely ([Pratt IXD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2023/02/design-critique-nyt-cooking-ios-app)). It has **revision tracking**, which none of the recipe managers surveyed documents. And its **private notes are free**, where NYT restricts them to subscribers ([NYT Help](https://thenewyorktimeshelpcenter.helpjuice.com/360011047512-New-York-Times-Cooking/19627732008468-recipe-comments-and-private-cooking-notes)).

The gaps cluster in shopping (four of five rows missing), quantity formatting and logging. These are mostly local data transforms, not infrastructure.

The single most damaging gap is content depth. With three distinct recipes, new filters, sorts, merged shopping and a planner would have almost nothing to act on. A filter that hides two of three recipes reads as broken rather than useful. Every other recommendation compounds only once the library is large enough to filter, plan and shop across.

## Ten backend-free changes close most of the gap now

The recommendations follow the evidence order. Place-keeping and layout clarity outrank novel features (Allspice; ASSETS '24), and QuinTaste already has place-keeping. The next-highest leverage is content, mise en place, quantities and shopping.

Every "Now" item uses existing owner-scoped state and React Native core APIs (`Share`, `Vibration`), so none needs a new native module or a development build. Each still follows the project's working rules: check the Expo SDK 57 versioned docs before touching an Expo API, pass `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npx expo lint`, and update `docs/PRD_COVERAGE.md`. QT-016/017/018/019, J04/J05 and P04–P06 stay Partial, because none of these changes connects production services.

N2, N3 and N4 all extend the same `doc.cooking` session object and should ship together, so the session schema migrates only once. N7 and N8 share the dietary vocabulary and re-tagging pass. That pass shows why presets must be tradition-specific: the bundled "Ginger coconut laksa" is five-pungent-free, but under a Jain preset it would correctly compute as a **Conflict** because of the ginger.

## Computed conflicts and tradition presets have no direct competitor

QuinTaste's computed fit badge is its strongest asset, and it is rarer than it looks. The mainstream approach is silent exclusion. In Samsung Food, "Diet, Avoidances and Dislikes filter recipes from your personalized recipe recommendations" ([Samsung Food Help](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18365212922644-Managing-Your-Samsung-Food-Account)). That hides a conflict instead of explaining it, and it fails without warning when a recipe arrives by link or search. Plant-based apps mostly rely on static editorial tags.

None of the consumer apps reviewed shows a per-recipe "conflicts with your exclusions" or "unconfirmed" state. QuinTaste shows both, ranks by them, and labels the result as not verification. That framing matches regulator language: the UK FSA found **62%** of people with animal-allergen reactions wrongly trusted vegan labels as safe ([FoodIngredientsFirst](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/consumers-should-not-rely-on-vegan-labeling-for-allergen-free-claims-stresses-uk-industry-body.html)). Leaning in means making the badge more precise, not louder. N8 adds compound-ingredient Unknown rules, N7 adds tradition-specific vocabularies, and X4 recomputes fit on substitutions. Each stays inside the existing "Contains / Unconfirmed" lines and the one-tap Recipe info panel.

The allium work is closer to complete than it appears. The existing group of seven alliums (garlic, onion, leeks, chives, scallions, shallots, asafoetida) already covers every pungent named by Singapore's Chinese Cultural Centre: garlic, onions, chives, leeks and shallots ([Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/popular-culture/from-temples-to-shopping-malls-a-century-of-chinese-vegetarian-food-in-singapore/)). It also has a one-tap "Exclude All Alliums" control and a cross-contact caveat (`ExclusionPicker.tsx:18-23`). What is missing is naming and breadth, not mechanics. Users should be able to pick a preset by the tradition they keep. A Jain preset needs vocabulary the app does not have yet.

QuinTaste must also avoid importing low-FODMAP shortcuts. Garlic-infused oil is the standard low-FODMAP garlic substitute ([Healthify](https://www.healthify.nz/monash-university-low-fodmap-diet-app)), but it keeps exactly the allium character a five-pungent observer avoids. Paired with real allium-free variations and lunar-day surfacing, this is a position no Western or regional recipe app occupies.

Local data ownership has turned from a privacy talking point into a retention feature. The record is consistent: Allrecipes users lost notes in 2023, Food Network Kitchen users lost their diet profiles in 2024, Yummly users scrambled to save their libraries in 2024, and Mealime will delete everything in four days. The durable managers sell the opposite promise. Paprika states that "no internet connection is required to view your recipes" ([Paprika App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/paprika-recipe-manager-3/id1303222868)), and Umami keeps viewing and export available even after its trial lapses ([Umami App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1597523594)). QuinTaste is already local-first and owner-isolated, with export and deletion. N10 makes that portable in open formats. Free private notes and revision tracking strengthen the story: QuinTaste keeps the cook's own record, and edits never silently overwrite what was cooked.

## Conclusion

The benchmark's most-shipped 2026 innovation, AI import from social video, is the one QuinTaste should adopt last. Its value depends on reliability the field has not achieved, and its output would collide with QuinTaste's no-reviewed-claims rule. The field's most neglected need is trustworthy diet fit for specific traditions, and QuinTaste is already structurally positioned to own it. The audit shows the expensive mechanics are done: a persisted session, step-scoped scaling, deadline timers and computed conflicts. That turns the roadmap into mostly content and local data work rather than infrastructure.

The binding constraint is not engineering but **library depth**: a well-tagged set of about two dozen allium-aware Singapore recipes is what makes every other "Now" item useful. Some evidence remains thin, and it should gate the Later items rather than block the Now items. No Singapore-specific recipe-app usage survey was found, demand for a rice-plus-dishes planner is inferred rather than measured, the frustration figures come from a single vendor-sponsored survey, no retention data exists for cooked logs, and physical-device behaviour for all timer and alert work is still unverified.
