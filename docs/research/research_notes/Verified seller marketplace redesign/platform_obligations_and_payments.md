# Platform obligations and payments: a Singapore food marketplace that connects buyers with SFA-licensed businesses and home-based food businesses (status as at 17 October 2026)

Scope: what QuinTaste, acting as the platform operator (an intermediary, not a food business), must do or would be wise to do in Singapore, and which design choices trigger extra regulation. This is factual research, not legal advice. Items marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed from a primary source in this session. Items marked **LAWYER** need confirmation by Singapore counsel before product decisions rely on them. Sources were accessed 17 Oct 2026. Singapore Statutes Online (SSO) blocked automated access partway through the session. The FSSA 2025 text was read from the SSO PDF (an informal consolidation "in force from 1/7/2026"). The PSA, CPFTA, PDPA, OCHA and OSRAA statutory texts were **not** read directly; those sections rely on MAS/agency pages, law-firm material and search excerpts.

## 1. Food law: licensing, SFA expectations of platforms, and the Food Safety and Security Act 2025

### Takeaway
A pure online intermediary needs no SFA licence. The FSSA 2025 expressly excludes from "food business" an intermediary that provides services for reward, "such as an internet service provider or an online auction location". Food-safety responsibility sits with the seller. However, SFA expects platforms to support information display, traceability and recalls through the voluntary SS 687:2022. Under the FSSA, a platform that takes payment for the placement of food content (promoted listings) can be treated as "causing or authorising" an advertisement, which brings strict-liability advertising offences into play.

### Cited Findings
**Licensing status of platforms and sellers**
- SFA groups online food businesses into three types. The first, "online food sellers", includes "e-commerce platforms [that] act as intermediaries" and resellers. All online and physical food businesses must comply with prevailing food laws, "even where no SFA licence is needed, such as for e-commerce platforms and group-buy organisers". The page was last updated 10 Mar 2025, with a site footer of 17 Oct 2026. — [SFA: Types of Online Food Businesses](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/online-food-businesses/types-of-online-food-businesses)
- SFA's FAQ for online sellers says sellers at physical premises need a licence. Home-based food businesses (HBFBs) and resellers buying from licensed wholesalers "are not required to be licenced by SFA". All of them remain subject to the Sale of Food Act and the Environmental Public Health Act (EPHA), and SFA "will take enforcement action for any infringements". — [SFA Guidelines for Food Sellers Selling Food Online via E-commerce Platforms (20 Sep 2021), FAQ i](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- The same guideline states that "a food seller is responsible for the safety of the food it offers for sale to the public", and that the food should come from SFA-regulated sources (paras 2 and 8). — [SFA 2021 guideline](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)

**Information display and recalls (what platforms are expected to support)**
- Prepacked food sold online is subject to the Food Regulations labelling requirements: name, complete ingredient list including allergens, net contents, local business name/address and country of origin (para 13). Sellers are "encouraged" to show the same information on the listing, either as text or as a legible label photo, and a clear label photo "will suffice" (para 14, FAQ iii). — [SFA 2021 guideline](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- For non-prepacked food, including cooked meals, the same guideline recommends displaying: name/photo, net contents, origin, operator name/address, storage instructions, allergens, and the "SFA licence/registration number (where applicable)" (para 15). — [SFA 2021 guideline](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- On a recall, sellers should "remove all online listings from the platforms where the affected food product is put up for sale". Where possible they should post notices online and contact all buyers (para 19). Sellers should also give a contact detail on each listing (para 20). — [SFA 2021 guideline](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- SFA points to **SS 687:2022 "Guidelines for Food E-commerce"**. It is not mandatory and is sold on the Singapore Standards eShop. It covers roles across the e-commerce chain, standard information at point of sale and delivery (ingredients, allergens, sources, expiry), and traceability and recall responsibilities, including complaint handling. It "applies to online food sellers, e-commerce platforms, and food delivery companies" and is designed for small and micro-businesses. The page was last updated 19 Feb 2025. — [SFA: Requirements for Online Food Sellers](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/online-food-businesses/requirements-for-online-food-sellers)
- SFA tells consumers to seek information "from the sellers or the e-commerce platform" before buying. They should report errant sellers through the SFA online feedback form (FAQ vi). — [SFA 2021 guideline](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- From 30 Jan 2026, prepacked food must carry a lot identification number or mark, under the Food (Amendment) Regulations 2025 as reflected in SFA's guide. The guide was last revised 28 Nov 2025. — [SFA: A Guide to Food Labelling and Advertisements](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-manufacturing-and-storage/a-guide-to-food-labelling-and-advertisements.pdf)

**Home-based food business (HBFB) rules a platform should encode in listing rules**
- HBFBs need no SFA licence but must comply with food laws. Under the current rules:
  - SFA may direct an HBFB to stop operations or recall food.
  - No catering is allowed, including buffet lines and packed meals.
  - No sales to SFA-licensed establishments or temporary fairs.
  - No ready-to-eat raw seafood.
  - Ingredients must be legally imported or bought from licensed suppliers, not from other HBFBs.
  - Selling through delivery platforms is not restricted, and online promotion is allowed.
  - HDB/URA rules ban signage at the home.
  
  The page was last updated 04 Aug 2026. — [SFA: Requirements for Home-based Food Businesses](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)

**Food Safety and Security Act 2025 (FSSA)**
- The FSSA was passed on 8 Jan 2025 and assented to on 3 Feb 2025. It has 17 Parts and 405 sections and comes into force "in tranches". Tranche 1 (defined food, non-packaged drinking water) commenced on 28 Nov 2025. SFA's page gives no dates for later tranches. — [SFA: Food Safety and Security Act](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/legislation/food-safety-and-security-act)
- Section 402 commenced on 27 Feb 2025 (S 136/2025). Selected s 3(1) definitions, including "advertise", "content" and "label", commenced on 28 Nov 2025 (S 709/2025). — [SSO S 136/2025](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL-Supp/S136-2025/Published/20250226?DocDate=20250226); [Lexplosion summary of S 709/2025](https://lexplosion.in/singapore/food-safety-and-security-act-2025-compliance-update-key-takeaways-from-the-commencement-notification-building-on-our-part-1-3-series/)
- One law firm describes a phased rollout "commencing in the second half of 2025 and concluding by 2028". — [Shook Lin & Bok](https://shooklin.com/singapore-enacts-comprehensive-food-safety-and-security-act/)
- SSO shows an informal consolidation "in force from 1/7/2026", amended by Act 15 of 2026. — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- **FSSA s 5(2):** "food business" includes a business "carried on as a home business or ... within the individual's private residence". HBFB sellers are therefore food businesses under the FSSA. — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF, s 5](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- **FSSA s 5(3)(a)–(b):** "food business" excludes a business that "carries on any other business besides trading in food and ... acts as an intermediary between persons who trade in food by providing, for reward, premises or a place ... or services (such as an internet service provider or an online auction location)". It also excludes one that "provides, for reward, services in connection with or for the purpose of trading in food (such as an event organiser or an organiser of a market at which food is sold)". — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF, s 5(3)](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- **FSSA First Schedule:** licensable retail classes (restaurants, takeaway/ready-to-eat preparation premises) are defined as premises "(but not a home)". Home kitchens therefore remain non-licensable under the FSSA. — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF, First Schedule items 1 and 4](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- **FSSA s 7 (advertising):** "Publish in Singapore" covers "posting the content as online material" and "publishing the content on an app". Under s 7(3)(b), a person is treated as causing or authorising publication if it "receives consideration for the display, placement or location of the content ... where the display, placement or location ... is determined by systems or processes that are agreed between the parties". Under s 7(4), unpaid personal opinions by individuals are not advertising. — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF, s 7](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- The FSSA defines "menu" to include one "displayed on an online location of a person who sells, or causes to be sold, the items of food online". — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF, s 3](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- **FSSA s 184:** publishing, or causing or authorising publication of, an advertisement containing a false description of food is an offence. The maximum fine is S$10,000 for a non-individual, or S$5,000 and/or 3 months for an individual. The prosecution need not prove knowledge (s 184(3)), and the advertisement is presumed false unless the accused adduces contrary evidence (s 184(5)). — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF, s 184](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- **FSSA s 188 defences:**
  - s 188(1): a business of transmitting or making data available "such that persons undertaking it have no control over the nature or content".
  - s 188(3): an advertising publisher acting in the ordinary course of business with "no financial interest in the food".
  - s 188(4): these defences are lost if the person was previously informed in writing by SFA, "ought reasonably to have known", or is the proprietor of the food business.
  
  The Act also preserves s 26 of the Electronic Transactions Act 2010 for network service providers (s 188(5)). — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF, s 188](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- **FSSA ss 178–179:** food is "falsely described" if it "is not of the composition ... nature or origin represented by the manner in which [it] is advertised ... or offered for supply". Misleading or deceptive conduct "in the course of carrying on a food business" is an offence, with a fine of up to S$15,000 and/or 6 months for an individual first offender. Section 186 provides a due-diligence defence. — [SSO FSSA 2025 PDF, ss 178–179, 186](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918)
- SFA's labelling guide (revised Nov 2025) contains **no definition of "vegetarian" or "vegan"**. Its only related guidance is that alternative-protein products should carry qualifiers such as "mock", "cultured" or "plant-based" to show their true nature. — [SFA: A Guide to Food Labelling and Advertisements](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-manufacturing-and-storage/a-guide-to-food-labelling-and-advertisements.pdf)

### Inferences
- QuinTaste as a commission-earning listing and ordering intermediary fits the s 5(3)(a) FSSA exclusion. The exclusion requires carrying on "other business besides trading in food" and acting as an intermediary "for reward". If QuinTaste buys and resells food as principal, or prepares or packs it, it would likely become a food business and lose the exclusion. **LAWYER**
- A vegan or vegetarian claim that is false (for example, egg or dairy in a "vegan" cake) is a "false description" (s 178(c)) by the seller. The seller bears primary exposure under ss 179/184 and the CPFTA. The platform's risk rises if it sells paid placement (s 7(3)(b)), edits or authors the claim (for example, auto-applied "Vegan" badges), or keeps a listing after SFA writes to it (s 188(4)).
- Product implications follow from this:
  - Keep diet labels as seller-declared.
  - Show the source of each claim.
  - Provide fast takedown after an SFA or consumer report.
  - Avoid platform-asserted "verified vegan" badges unless backed by evidence.
  - If promoted listings are sold, screen promoted food content.
- Practical expectations drawn from the SFA guideline and SS 687 that a platform can operationalise:
  - Collect and display the SFA licence number for licensed sellers.
  - Collect an HBFB attestation covering no catering, no raw ready-to-eat seafood and licensed ingredient sources.
  - Use structured allergen and ingredient fields.
  - Require a seller contact detail.
  - Provide a recall or takedown workflow that can delist and message past buyers of a product.
  - Provide a "report to SFA" pointer.
- HBFB "no catering" means pre-order features should not enable buffet or bulk packed-meal catering orders by HBFB sellers. Order-size limits or category rules are a sensible control. **LAWYER** on where the line sits.

### Gaps
- Which FSSA Parts are in force at 17 Oct 2026 beyond Tranche 1 (28 Nov 2025) is unknown. This includes Part 10 advertising (ss 177–188) and Part 4 food business duties. The 1 Jul 2026 consolidation date reflects an amending Act, not necessarily commencement. **UNVERIFIED.** Until the operative Parts commence, the Sale of Food Act and EPHA presumably continue to apply (**UNVERIFIED**).
- The SS 687:2022 text is paywalled and was not read, so the exact platform clauses on recall and traceability are unknown.
- No SFA statement was found that platforms must verify seller licences or remove listings on request. SFA's expectations are framed at sellers, plus the voluntary SS 687.
- No public FSSA guidance yet addresses online marketplaces specifically.

## 2. E-commerce marketplace regulation: MHA Transaction Safety Ratings, OCHA Codes of Practice, TR 76

### Takeaway
None of these is mandatory for a small, undesignated platform. The TSR rates only "major marketplaces" with significant local reach or scam volume. The OCHA E-Commerce Code binds only services formally designated by the competent authority (currently Carousell and Meta's Facebook Marketplace, Ads and Pages). TR 76 is a voluntary national standard. Their criteria are nonetheless the de facto Singapore benchmark for seller verification, payment protection and reporting. QuinTaste could become subject to OCHA designation if scam activity grew.

### Cited Findings
**Transaction Safety Ratings (TSR)**
- The TSR, launched 14 May 2022 by the Inter-Ministry Committee on Scams, covers major marketplaces that facilitate transactions "from multiple sellers to multiple buyers, with a significant local reach or a significant number of e-commerce scams reported". Ratings run from one to four ticks and reflect user authenticity, transaction safety, loss-remediation channels and effectiveness. Marketplaces adopting TR 76 "would score better". — [MHA 14 May 2022 release](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/introduction-of-e-commerce-marketplace-transaction-safety-ratings-and-revised-technical-reference-76-on-e-commerce-transactions/)
- The current TSR page assesses five features:
  1. measures to verify seller authenticity;
  2. monitoring of fraudulent seller behaviour;
  3. secure payment options;
  4. maintenance of transaction records and user data;
  5. reporting and dispute resolution.
  
  It also takes scam-report effectiveness into account. Current ratings are four ticks for Amazon, Lazada, Shopee and TikTok Shop, two for Carousell, and one for Facebook Marketplace. The page states TR 76 "was updated in 2022". — [MHA TSR page](https://www.mha.gov.sg/e-commerce-marketplace-transaction-safety-ratings/)
- The TSR 2025 edition was published 30 Aug 2025 and added TikTok Shop with four ticks. It cited 11,665 e-commerce scam cases in 2024, up 19.2% on 2023. This comes from a search excerpt of the MHA release, whose URL now returns 404. — [MHA TSR 2025 release (as indexed)](https://www.mha.gov.sg/mediaroom/press-releases/e-commerce-marketplace-transaction-safety-ratings-2025/)
- Carousell's TSR profile lists several measures:
  - a local mobile number is needed to list or chat;
  - Singpass-verified blue tick;
  - Singpass KYC for high-risk scenarios from June 2024;
  - chat filters that block emails and links;
  - a "Buy" button with Buyer Protection, where payment is held after a report is made.
  
  The page was last updated 30 Jan 2026. — [MHA TSR: Carousell](https://www.mha.gov.sg/e-commerce-marketplace-transaction-safety-ratings/carousell/)

**Online Criminal Harms Act 2023 (OCHA): Code of Practice for E-Commerce Services**
- The Code was issued under OCHA s 21(1)(b), effective 26 June 2024. It applies only to "designated e-commerce services" designated under s 20(a). Designated providers have six months from their Code Application Notice to implement it. The provisions are summarised below. — [SPF: OCHA Code of Practice for E-Commerce Services (PDF)](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/Spf/Advisories/OCHA/COP-for-Online-Communication-Services/COP-for-E-Commerce-Services.ashx)

  | Section | Requirement |
  |---|---|
  | A1–A2 | Proactive detection and action against scam accounts; an easily accessible user reporting mechanism |
  | A3–A4, A7 | Fast-track channel for law-enforcement reports; informing law enforcement of scam trends; expedited channels for emergency information requests |
  | A5 | Retain data of scam-linked accounts (identity records, transactions, logs, IPs, metadata) for at least 90 days |
  | A6 | Preserve records that law enforcement requests for as long as needed |
  | B1–B3 | Reasonable verification against inauthentic or bot accounts; extra verification when an account behaves suspiciously; strong login verification |
  | B4 | Optional "verified" account status |
  | B5 | "Subject users who advertise or post about the sales of goods and/or services, or those who intend to do so, to verification against Government-issued records" |
  | B6 | Offer "as an option ... payment protection mechanisms that require the delivery of goods or services to be verified before payment is released to sellers" |
  | C1 | Annual report to the competent authority |

- The designated e-commerce services are Carousell, Facebook Marketplace, Facebook Advertisements and Facebook Pages. MHA's assessment of 10 Mar 2025 recorded these positions:
  - **Carousell:** verification of selected sellers, with the pilot extended to 30 Jun 2025. All-seller verification could be required by 1 Oct 2025 if scams did not drop significantly.
  - **Facebook Marketplace:** not required to verify all sellers.
  - **Facebook Advertisements:** Meta to verify all advertisers by end-June 2025.
  - **Payment-protection requirement (B6):** "remains waived", with reassessment in 2025.
  - **Approach:** "risk-calibrated, outcome-based", driven by SPF scam statistics.
  
  — [MHA 10 Mar 2025 assessment](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/assessment-of-carousell-and-meta-enhanced-verification-measures-under-the-e-commerce-code-of-the-online-criminal-harms-act/); [SPF Codes of Practice listing](https://www.police.gov.sg/knowledge-hub/legislation/online-harms-act-overview/codes-of-practice)
- MHA selected these services because they "pose the highest risks of e-commerce scams in Singapore". — [Rajah & Tann summary](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/implementation-of-the-online-criminal-harms-act-codes-of-practice-for-designated-online-services-come-into-force/)

**TR 76:2022 (Guidelines for Electronic Commerce Transactions)**
- TR 76 is a Singapore Standards Council technical reference, revised in 2022 with industry input. It sets out best practices across pre-, during- and post-purchase activities, customer support and merchant verification, "to better enable merchant authenticity, improve transaction security and aid enforcement". It is described as "the national standard"; adoption is encouraged through the TSR rather than mandated. It is sold on the Singapore Standards eShop. — [MHA 14 May 2022 release](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/introduction-of-e-commerce-marketplace-transaction-safety-ratings-and-revised-technical-reference-76-on-e-commerce-transactions/); [MHA TSR page](https://www.mha.gov.sg/e-commerce-marketplace-transaction-safety-ratings/)
- A key recommendation is that e-marketplaces should determine what information to collect from merchants and what verification processes to follow. — [Allen & Gledhill summary](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/21965/inter-ministry-committee-on-scams-introduces-e-commerce-marketplace-transaction-safety-ratings-and-revised-technical-reference-76-on-e-commerce-transactions)

**CASE Standard Dispute Management Framework for E-marketplaces (voluntary)**
- CASE launched the framework on 26 Nov 2021. Its best practices include verifying merchant identity and keeping dispute channels accessible at all times. — [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/19838/case-launches-framework-for-faster-dispute-resolution-to-give-greater-assurance-to-consumers-shopping-on-e-marketplaces)
- Shopee adopted it by MOU in April 2023 and committed to resolving returns and refunds in 2.5 working days on average, against CASE's recommended seven days. CASE has asked e-marketplaces to "authenticate sellers, establish escrows to protect consumers' monies and improve dispute resolution processes". — [CASE–Shopee press release, Apr 2023](https://www.case.org.sg/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Press-Release-CASE-and-Shopee-sign-MOU-to-better-protect-consumer-rights-through-adoption-of-CASE-framework.pdf)

### Inferences
- A small vegetarian marketplace is outside the TSR and OCHA scopes today. Designation is discretionary and risk-based, with no published user-number threshold.
- Building to the OCHA E-Commerce Code and TSR features from day one is cheap insurance and a trust signal. The main features are:
  - government-record verification of every seller (Singpass/Myinfo, ACRA UEN, SFA licence number);
  - an in-app report button;
  - 90-day retention of data on scam-flagged accounts;
  - a law-enforcement contact channel;
  - an optional payment-protection or "release after fulfilment" mode.
- B6-style payment protection, where funds are released after fulfilment, implies the platform or its PSP holds funds. This intersects with the PSA analysis in section 4.

### Gaps
- No 2026 TSR edition was found. The 2025 release URL now 404s, so whether a 2026 update exists is **UNVERIFIED**.
- The outcome of MHA's 2025 reassessment is not known: whether Carousell was required to verify all sellers from 1 Oct 2025, and whether B6 remains waived. **UNVERIFIED.**
- OCHA penalties for breaching a Code, and the s 20 designation criteria in statutory text, were not read (SSO blocked).
- TR 76:2022 and the CASE framework texts are not freely available, so clause-level content (for example, specific escrow guidance) was not verified.

## 3. Consumer protection: CPFTA, price transparency, reviews, CASE, and liability for "vegan/vegetarian" claims

### Takeaway
The CPFTA applies to e-commerce as to offline trade. No marketplace-specific CPFTA duties were found. Platform pricing must show mandatory fees in the headline price, following the CCS/CCCS Price Transparency Guidelines in effect since 1 Nov 2020. Fake or manipulated reviews are an active enforcement priority, with action against 45 businesses and a fake-review provider announced 30 Sep 2026. A false "vegan" claim is primarily the seller's unfair practice. Platform exposure depends on its role, which needs **LAWYER** confirmation.

### Cited Findings
- MTI's written PQ reply of 1 Mar 2024 described the consumer-protection position for e-commerce:
  - the CPFTA covers e-commerce and offline transactions alike, including false or misleading representations;
  - consumers are pointed to CaseTrust retailers and to e-marketplaces that adopted CASE's dispute framework, plus the MHA TSR;
  - consumers with e-commerce disputes can approach CASE;
  - no marketplace-specific CPFTA obligation was described.
  
  — [MTI/METI written PQ reply, 1 Mar 2024](https://www.meti.gov.sg/newsroom/written-reply-to-pq-on-consumer-protection-for-e-commerce-marketplace)
- The CCCS Guidelines on Price Transparency took effect 1 Nov 2020 and cover drip pricing, price comparisons, discounts and use of "free". They set three main rules:
  - unavoidable or mandatory charges ("taxes, surcharges, service fees, etc.") should be included in the headline price;
  - charges that cannot be calculated in advance must be disclosed clearly and prominently together with the headline price, including third-party fees;
  - an opt-in approach is recommended for add-ons.
  
  — [CCS Guidelines on Price Transparency](https://www.ccs.gov.sg/consumer-protection/legislation-and-guidelines/guidelines-on-price-transparency/); [Guidelines PDF](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/45/6dd1e832-b475-499e-a409-db556eac23c7/CCCS%20Guidelines%20on%20Price%20Transparency_HR.pdf)
- On 30 Sep 2026 the Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore (styled "CCS" on its site) announced undertakings from 45 businesses and a fake-review provider (Reputifly). The businesses must stop procuring fake reviews, remove them, report progress and post a prominent public apology for six months. Two businesses declined and remain under investigation. The fake reviews appeared on Google, Facebook, Tripadvisor, Carousell, Yelp and Trustpilot. — [CCS media release, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.ccs.gov.sg/media-and-events/newsroom/announcements-and-media-releases/ccs-takes-action-against-45-businesses-and-a-fake-review-provider-for-fabricating-online-reviews/)
- Earlier fake-review cases include a furniture retailer (Loft Home Furnishing, 2024) and a car-detailing firm that used ChatGPT to write reviews (July 2025). Posting fake reviews in a consumer transaction is treated as an unfair practice under the CPFTA. — [CCS: Loft Home Furnishing](https://www.ccs.gov.sg/media-and-events/newsroom/announcements-and-media-releases/loft-home-furnishing-caught-posting-fake-customer-reviews-533/); [Lexology on AI fake reviews](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=662fde46-fd30-4466-8eb5-38affd42329f)
- CCCS's consumer-protection mandate expanded on 1 Jul 2025, when product safety and legal metrology functions transferred from Enterprise Singapore. — [CCS: CCCS Expands Consumer Protection Mandate](https://www.ccs.gov.sg/media-and-events/newsroom/announcements-and-media-releases/cccs-expands-consumer-protection-mandate/)
- SSO lists a CPFTA amendment by Act 5 of 2025, effective 9 Mar 2025. Its content was not determined. — [SSO CPFTA 2003](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/act/cpfta2003) (via search excerpt)
- Consumers who meet unfair practices can approach CASE (www.case.org.sg, 6277 5100). In egregious cases, CCCS can seek court declarations and injunctions. — [Rajah & Tann: Consumer protection in Singapore](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/consumer-protection-in-singapore-five-key-observations-for-businesses-in-a-heightened-enforcement-climate/)

### Inferences
- **Checkout design.** Any platform service fee, delivery fee or small-order fee must be in the headline or total price shown on the listing and menu. Variable delivery fees must be flagged prominently alongside the price. Add-ons such as tips and packaging should be opt-in.
- **Reviews design.**
  - Allow reviews only from verified completed orders.
  - Block seller self-reviews and incentivised reviews without disclosure.
  - Do not suppress negative reviews ("review gating").
  - Keep moderation logs.
  
  The 2026 CCS action shows reviews are a live enforcement area. A platform that curates reviews misleadingly could itself be engaging in an unfair practice. **LAWYER**
- **Vegan/vegetarian claims.**
  - With no statutory definition, a claim is judged as a representation. A false claim falls under CPFTA unfair practice and FSSA s 178/179 false description (section 1).
  - Platform mitigation: seller attestations and warranties, structured ingredient disclosure, seller-declared labels, a "report incorrect diet label" flow, and suspension for repeat false claims.
  - Avoid platform-made "certified vegan" claims unless verified.

## 4. Payments: Payment Services Act 2019 licensing, exemptions, and marketplace payment providers (Stripe Connect, HitPay); direct PayNow

### Takeaway
If QuinTaste itself accepts buyer money and pays it out to sellers, it would likely be providing a licensable payment service under the PSA. "Incidental" provision is not exempt, and MAS has given marketplace operators offering payment services as an example of needing a licence. The usual low-regulation route has two features:
- A MAS-licensed PSP is the merchant acquirer or money transmitter, and funds flow PSP to seller.
- QuinTaste only passes payment instructions and takes its commission through the PSP.

MAS treats data-only providers as outsourcing, not regulated. Stripe Payments Singapore has held an MPI licence since 1 Jul 2026, and HitPay's Platforms product routes payouts directly to sub-merchants. Buyer-pays-seller-directly by PayNow keeps QuinTaste out of the PSA but forgoes payment protection that the TSR, OCHA Code and CASE treat as best practice. **LAWYER** to confirm the chosen flow.

### Cited Findings
**PSA scope and licensing**
- The PSA regulates seven payment services. MAS identified regulated services as those with "a clear payments nexus, the service providers process funds or acquire transactions for merchants, and the service providers contract or deal with the consumer or the merchant". "Service providers that process only data (e.g. payment instructions) and not money are treated as outsourcing services", and "we do not require providers of payment instrument aggregation services and data communications platforms to be licensed" (MAS FAQ Q7.1–7.2). — [MAS PSA FAQs (11 May 2020) PDF](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/MAS/Fintech/Payment-Services-Act/Payment-Services-Act-FAQ-11-May-2020.pdf)
- Carve-outs include limited-purpose e-money and payment services "solely incidental to or necessary for regulated activities" of a regulated financial services company (Q7.3). That incidental carve-out applies only to regulated financial institutions. — [MAS PSA FAQs PDF](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/MAS/Fintech/Payment-Services-Act/Payment-Services-Act-FAQ-11-May-2020.pdf); [MAS Payments Service Licensing FAQs (updated 29 Apr 2024)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/contact-us/faqs/payments-faqs/payments-service-licensing-faqs)
- **Commercial agent exclusion** (First Schedule Part 2, para 2(a)): it covers a person in the business of acting as an authorised agent "for the purposes of negotiating or concluding the sale or purchase of goods or services on behalf of the payer or payee". MAS's example is a motor vehicle dealer accepting payment for the vehicle company (FAQ Q8, added 13 Apr 2020). — [MAS PSA FAQs PDF](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/MAS/Fintech/Payment-Services-Act/Payment-Services-Act-FAQ-11-May-2020.pdf)
- WongPartnership (27 Sep 2019) set out the definitions and examples relevant to marketplaces:
  - **Merchant acquisition:** accepting and processing a payment transaction for a merchant under a contract with the merchant, resulting in a transfer of money to the merchant "regardless of whether the service provider comes into possession of the money".
  - **Domestic money transfer:** includes "accepting any money from any person for transfer to the payment account of a different person".
  - **"Platform offering products of different merchants":** flagged as potentially domestic or cross-border money transfer, raising the query "Is the platform a reseller or commercial agent?".
  - **Reseller route:** a platform "may not be conducting payment services where it is acting as a re-seller ... (as principal)" and is "the intended recipient of the funds".
  
  — [WongPartnership: Navigating the Payment Services Act 2019 (slides)](https://www.wongpartnership.com/upload/EmailEDM/Navigating%20the%20Payment%20Services%20Act.pdf)
- Covenant Chambers (updated 20 Jan 2020) states that "MAS has given the example of online marketplace operators offering payment services" and that "such operators must have a licence". It treats incidental services as carrying similar risks. — [Covenant Chambers: Note on Payment Services Act](https://www.covenantchambers.com/articles/note-on-payment-services-act)
- **Licence tiers.** A Standard Payment Institution licence covers activity at or below these limits; exceeding them requires a Major Payment Institution licence:
  - S$3m monthly transactions for any one payment service;
  - S$6m for two or more services;
  - S$5m daily outstanding e-money.
  
  MPIs must safeguard customer money through a bank guarantee, trust account or other prescribed means. — [MAS: Licensing for Payment Service Providers](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/payments/standard-payment-institution-licence)
- **Account issuance:** a business that does not operate the payment account does not provide account issuance. One that operates and tops up accounts needs a licence unless exempt. — [MAS Payments Service Licensing FAQs](https://www.mas.gov.sg/contact-us/faqs/payments-faqs/payments-service-licensing-faqs)

**Stripe (Connect + PayNow)**
- Stripe Payments Singapore Pte Ltd "is a holder of the Major Payment Institution (MPI) licence under the PS Act 2019 with effect from 1 July 2026". It is licensed for account issuance, domestic and cross-border money transfer, merchant acquisition and e-money issuance. Customer money is held in trust accounts with a Singapore safeguarding institution. **Superseded:** earlier versions of this page described a temporary exemption pending the MPI licence. — [Stripe Singapore disclosures](https://stripe.com/en-sg/legal/singapore-disclosures); earlier wording via [Stripe zh-sg disclosures (as indexed)](https://stripe.com/zh-sg/legal/singapore-disclosures)

  - connected accounts need the `paynow_payments` capability and correct MCCs;

  - QR codes expire after 1 hour;
  - the statement descriptor shows "STRIPE PAYMENTS SINGAPORE PTE. LTD."
  
  — [Stripe Docs: PayNow](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/paynow)
- **Stripe Connect pricing (SG page):**

**HitPay (Platforms)**

No monthly or setup fees. — [HitPay blog: lower payment processing fees](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/lower-payment-processing-fees-singapore) (via search excerpt; confirm on [hitpayapp.com/pricing](https://hitpayapp.com/jp/pricing))

**Direct PayNow (no in-app payment)**
- The OCHA E-Commerce Code B6 expects designated platforms to offer payment protection that releases funds only after verified delivery. - The TSR scores "availability of secure payment options". ### Inferences
- **Model A — platform-held funds (QuinTaste's own bank account or wallet collects, then pays sellers):** likely domestic money transfer and/or merchant acquisition. Stored buyer balances or credits may add account issuance or e-money. This needs a MAS SPI licence, with MPI and safeguarding above the thresholds. It is not realistic for a small app. **LAWYER**
- **Model B — licensed PSP with sub-merchant accounts (Stripe Connect, HitPay Platforms):** each seller is KYC'd by the PSP and money settles PSP to seller. QuinTaste sends payment instructions (data) and receives its commission via the PSP. This aligns with MAS's data-only outsourcing position.
  - In Stripe, prefer direct or destination charges where the seller is merchant of record.
  - Separate-charges-and-transfers, holding funds on the platform balance for later release, makes the platform look more like a collector.
  - Confirm the chosen charge type with Stripe and counsel. **LAWYER**
- **Escrow-like "release on fulfilment":** this is achievable inside the PSP, for example Stripe delayed payouts or manual payout schedules on connected accounts, without QuinTaste holding money. The funds then remain under the licensed PSP's safeguarding.
- **Model C — buyer pays seller directly by PayNow and the app only records orders:** no PSA exposure for QuinTaste, but there are trade-offs:
  - no refund or dispute tooling;
  - higher scam exposure, since PayNow direct transfers are the classic scam rail;
  - no "secure payment option" for TSR-style trust;
  - sellers' PayNow proxies may expose personal names or mobile numbers, a PDPA consideration for HBFB individuals.
  
  It also means QuinTaste cannot collect commission at payment time and would need to invoice sellers separately.
- **Model D — platform buys and resells as principal:** this avoids PSA payment-service characterisation (WongPartnership), but QuinTaste likely becomes a "food business" and the CPFTA "supplier", losing the FSSA s 5(3) exclusion. This is not recommended.

## 5. Tax: GST registration and seller-income reporting

### Takeaway
QuinTaste must register for GST only if its own taxable turnover (commissions, subscriptions, fees) exceeds S$1m in a calendar year, or is expected to exceed S$1m in the next 12 months. The electronic-marketplace "deemed supplier" rules target imported low-value goods and overseas digital services, not local food sold by local sellers. No IRAS regime requiring food marketplaces to report seller income was found. IRAS pre-fill exists only for private-hire drivers.

### Cited Findings
- **Compulsory GST registration:**
  - The test is taxable turnover over S$1 million at the end of a calendar year (retrospective view), or expected to exceed S$1 million in the next 12 months (prospective view).
  - Retrospective applications are made 1–30 January, with registration effective 1 March.
  - Prospective applications are due within 30 days of the forecast date. For liabilities on or after 1 Jul 2025, registration takes effect two months after the forecast.
  - Forecasts need evidence such as contracts; targets alone do not count.
  - Late registration brings backdated GST, fines up to S$10,000 and a 10% penalty.
  
  The page was last updated 15 Sep 2026. — [IRAS: Do I Need to Register for GST](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/gst-registration-deregistration/do-i-need-to-register-for-gst)
- IRAS: "whether you are a local or an overseas operator of an electronic marketplace, you may be regarded as the supplier of the low-value goods supplied by the local and overseas suppliers through your marketplace under certain conditions". A local marketplace operator may likewise be the deemed supplier of digital services made by overseas suppliers. — [IRAS: GST on Imports of Low Value Goods](https://www.iras.gov.sg/irashome/GST/GST-registered-businesses/GST-and-Digital-Economy/GST-on-Imports-of-Low-Value-Goods/) (via search excerpt); [IRAS OVR seminar slides](https://www.iras.gov.sg/irashome/uploadedFiles/IRASHome/GST/OVR%20Seminar%20Slides(1).pdf)
- IRAS pre-fills platform income only for private-hire car drivers who opt in with Grab, GoJek, TADA or Ryde. Platform workers must keep records for at least 5 years and file their own income. — [IRAS: Filing responsibilities of platform workers](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/tax-obligations-of-platform-workers/filing-responsibilities-of-platform-workers)
- IRAS publishes a tax guide for online sellers and service providers: sellers declare their own business income. — [IRAS: Tax guide for online sellers](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/tax-obligations-by-industry-trade-or-profession/tax-guide-for-online-sellers-and-service-providers)
- The OECD Model Rules for Reporting by Platform Operators (2020, with an optional goods module) would require seller identity (name, address, TIN, date of birth) and income reporting where adopted. — [OECD Model Rules (2020)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2020/07/model-rules-for-reporting-by-platform-operators-with-respect-to-sellers-in-the-sharing-and-gig-economy_379520db/d7973047-en.pdf)

### Inferences
- With commission-only revenue, GST registration is distant for a small app: about S$1m in fees, which is roughly S$6.7m GMV at a 15% take rate.
- Local food sold by local sellers is not caught by the low-value-goods or overseas-service deemed-supplier rules. Each GST-registered seller charges GST on its own sales. **LAWYER/tax adviser** to confirm for a platform-as-agent structure.
- Even without a reporting duty, keep per-seller payout statements, which Stripe and HitPay dashboards provide. They help HBFB sellers meet their own filing duty and help the platform answer IRAS enquiries.

## 6. PDPA: seller identity documents, NRIC rules, retention, DPO, and permissible verification data

### Takeaway
The PDPA applies to all seller and buyer data. NRIC numbers and copies should not be collected unless required by law or necessary to verify identity to a high degree of fidelity. NRIC numbers must not be used for authentication, with a phase-out deadline of 31 Dec 2026 and stepped-up PDPC enforcement from 1 Jan 2027. Verification should prefer Singpass/Myinfo, UEN and SFA licence numbers over NRIC images. Penalties reach 10% of Singapore turnover (above S$10m turnover) or S$1m.

### Cited Findings
- The PDPC Advisory Guidelines for NRIC and other national identification numbers (2018, reissued Feb 2020) set the general rule: organisations should not collect, use or disclose NRIC numbers or copies. The same treatment covers birth certificate numbers, FIN, work permit numbers and passport numbers. Collection is permitted only where required by law or necessary to accurately establish or verify identity to a high degree of fidelity, and consent is still needed unless a PDPA exception applies. PDPC's page states that, in light of the MDDI statement of 13 Dec 2024, "these Advisory Guidelines will be updated. In the meantime, these guidelines remain valid". — [PDPC: NRIC Advisory Guidelines](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/guidelines-and-consultation/2020/02/advisory-guidelines-on-the-personal-data-protection-act-for-nric-and-other-national-identification-numbers) (via search excerpt); [PDPC Technical Guide](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/Help-and-Resources/2018/08/Technical-Guide-to-Advisory-Guidelines-on-the-PDPA-for-NRIC-and-Other-National-Identification-Numbers)
- The Technical Guide advises avoiding NRIC numbers as user names or unique identifiers in apps and public-facing systems. — [PDPC Technical Guide](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/organisations/resources/guidance-by-topic/technical-guide-to-advisory-guidelines-on-the-pdpa-for-nric-and-other-national-identification-numbers)
- The PDPC–CSA joint advisory of 26 Jun 2025 says NRIC numbers, full or partial, should not be used as passwords or to authenticate a person, including as default passwords. — [PDPC announcement, Jun 2025](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/news-and-events/announcements/2025/06/joint-advisory-against-using-nric-numbers-for-authentication-by-pdpc-and-csa); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/singapore/singapore-issues-advisory-to-stop-use-of-nric-numbers-for-authentication)
- PDPC announced on 2 Feb 2026 that private organisations must stop using full or partial NRIC numbers for authentication by 31 Dec 2026. From 1 Jan 2027, PDPC will step up enforcement, with possible directions or financial penalties. Identification uses, as opposed to authentication, are distinguished. — [Baker McKenzie, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/02/singapore-pdpc-to-ban-nric-authentication-use-by-end-2026); [MDDI: Responsible use of NRIC numbers](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/responsible-use-of-nric-numbers-across-public-and-private-sectors/)
- **Financial penalties** for intentional or negligent contraventions: up to 10% of annual Singapore turnover for organisations with turnover above S$10m; otherwise up to S$1m per breach. — [CMS Expert Guide: Singapore](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/the-impact-of-gdpr-in-non-eu-countries/singapore)
- **Retention limitation:** organisations must cease retention or dispose of personal data when it is no longer needed for any business or legal purpose. — [PDPC: Data Protection Obligations](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/Overview-of-PDPA/The-Legislation/Personal-Data-Protection-Act/Data-Protection-Obligations)

### Inferences
- **Seller verification data minimisation:**
  - **Licensed businesses:** UEN and entity data via Myinfo business (ACRA), SFA licence number and premises address, plus a representative verified through Singpass.
  - **HBFBs (individuals):** Singpass/Myinfo person data (verified name, possibly residential address for the HDB/URA scheme) rather than NRIC uploads.
  - **NRIC:** store only if a law requires it. No law requiring platforms to hold seller NRICs was found. If Myinfo returns NRIC/UINFIN, request only the attributes needed. Never use NRIC numbers as identifiers or for authentication; the 31 Dec 2026 deadline is imminent.
- **Retention:** define retention for verification artefacts, for example for the life of the seller account plus a set period for disputes. Align it with the OCHA-style 90-day minimum for scam-flagged account data, adopted voluntarily, and the 5-year record-keeping that IRAS expects of sellers.
- **Exposure from direct PayNow (Model C):** showing HBFB sellers' personal mobile numbers or PayNow names raises disclosure concerns. Prefer in-app payment through a PSP.

### Gaps
- The PDPA s 11(3) DPO requirement was not read directly (SSO blocked). Secondary sources conflict: some say a DPO must be designated with contact details made public, others say "encouraged". The PDPA has long required designating at least one individual responsible for compliance, but this is **UNVERIFIED** in this session. **LAWYER**

## 8. Online Safety (Relief and Accountability) Act 2025 (OSRAA): duties for apps with reviews and chat

### Takeaway
OSRAA was passed 5 Nov 2025. The Online Safety Commission and specified statutory torts began on 29 Jun 2026, initially covering five harms:
- harassment (including sexual harassment);
- doxxing;
- stalking;
- intimate image abuse;
- image-based child abuse.

Platforms hosting user content, which would include reviews and in-app chat, owe a duty to respond reasonably once they receive a valid Online Harm Notice. The Commissioner can direct takedowns or account restrictions and can require identity information. Larger platforms face extra requirements, such as response times.

### Cited Findings
- MinLaw announced on 14 May 2026 that specified statutory tort provisions and the Online Safety Commission would start on 29 Jun 2026. — [MinLaw/MDDI announcement](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety--relief-and-accountability--act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/); [MinLaw](https://www.mlaw.gov.sg/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety-relief-and-accountability-act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/)
- The Act covers 13 harm categories, five initially, with the rest "progressively implemented". Duties apply to three groups:
  - communicators (posters);
  - administrators (people running groups or pages);
  - platforms (services hosting content).
  
  The Commissioner can direct takedown, restrict accounts, and require platforms to provide end-user identity information such as name or contact details. Victims may obtain that information for specified purposes, such as suing. — [Allen & Gledhill, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/33139/partial-commencement-of-online-safety-relief-and-accountability-act-2025-and-online-safety-commission-to-begin-operations-on-29-june-2026)
- The platform duty "to respond reasonably when notified of harm is triggered upon the victim sending an Online Harm Notice ... in the prescribed form and manner". Certain platforms "with greater reach" face additional requirements, such as response times. For harassment and stalking, victims generally report to the platform first and may go to the OSC if the platform fails to respond adequately within 24 hours. One law firm reports response windows as short as six hours for the most serious harms on larger designated platforms. — [MDDI: OSC begins operations](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/the-online-safety-commission-begins-operations-on-29-june-2026-/); [WongPartnership](https://www.wongpartnership.com/insights/detail/online-safety-relief-and-accountability-act-2025-provisions-relating-to-online-safety-commission-and-certain-statutory-torts-take-effect-on-29-june-2026) (via search excerpts)
- Victims can sue communicators, administrators or platforms in court for damages or injunctions. The OSC can require platforms to provide information that may identify perpetrators. — [MinLaw OSRAA resource page (last updated 28 Jun 2026)](https://onlinesafety.mlaw.gov.sg/resources/osraa/)

### Inferences
- Reviews, profiles, listing photos and chat make QuinTaste a "platform" hosting user content. Size thresholds apply only to the extra duties. Minimum build:
  - an easily found Online Harm Notice and report channel accepting the prescribed form;
  - a triage queue able to act well within 24 hours;
  - takedown and account-restriction tools;
  - logs;

- Doxxing risk is specific to food marketplaces: HBFB sellers' home addresses for pickup. Show only the area until an order is confirmed, and let sellers report doxxing.

## 9. Liability allocation in comparable terms, insurance, and a consolidated design-choice trigger summary

### Takeaway
Singapore and regional marketplaces push food-safety and legal-compliance responsibility onto merchants. They reserve the platform's rights to inspect, penalise, suspend and remove. No legal requirement for sellers or platforms to carry product liability insurance was found. Insurance is a contractual or commercial choice.

### Cited Findings
- Grab Singapore's Merchant Code of Conduct requires merchant-partners to:
  - "hold valid licenses to operate and other permits as may be required by the relevant authorities", and update them on expiry or revocation;
  - comply with food, labour, health, safety and environmental laws;
  - maintain safe and hygienic practices and follow consumers' allergy and dietary instructions.
  
  Grab may conduct safety and health inspections, impose penalties if food does not meet food-safety standards, and suspend or terminate access. The page shows no date and has no indemnity or insurance clause. — [Grab SG: Code of Conduct, Merchant Partner](https://www.grab.com/sg/terms-policies/code-of-conduct-merchant/)
- A draft GrabFood Vietnam Terms of Service states Grab "is not responsible or liable for the merchant's food preparation, food hygiene and safety, and does not verify compliance". This is Vietnam, not Singapore, and is a draft. — [Grab VN food terms draft](https://www.grab.com/vn/en/food-terms-draft/)
- Carousell requires a local mobile number, offers Singpass verification, and restricts the protected "Buy" button to eligible sellers. Carousell keeps transaction and communication records and may share them with regulators or law enforcement. — [MHA TSR: Carousell](https://www.mha.gov.sg/e-commerce-marketplace-transaction-safety-ratings/carousell/)
- SFA treats "food safety [as] a joint responsibility of the government, the food industry, and the consumers", while "food sellers are responsible for the safety of the food they offer". — [SFA 2021 guideline, para 20](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)

### Inferences
**Seller terms to include, based on the patterns and law above** (**LAWYER** to draft):
- seller is the food business, merchant of record and supplier;
- warranties covering SFA licence validity (with licence number), or HBFB status and compliance with HBFB rules;
- accurate ingredient, allergen and diet claims, with no false "vegan/vegetarian" descriptions;
- recall cooperation: delist on notice and contact buyers;
- cooperation with SFA, CCS and law enforcement;
- indemnity for food-safety and claim-related losses;
- the platform's right to inspect evidence, suspend, delist and withhold payouts within the PSP's rules;
- price-transparency compliance;
- no fake reviews.

**Buyer terms:**
- clear statement that QuinTaste is an intermediary, not the food business;
- diet labels are seller-declared;
- dispute and refund process (CASE framework-style timelines);
- reporting channels.
