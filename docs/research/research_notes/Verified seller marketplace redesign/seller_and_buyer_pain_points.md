# Pain points of Singapore home-based food businesses, small licensed food sellers, and their buyers (vegetarian/vegan emphasis)

Research date: 17 October 2026. Scope is Singapore unless a line says otherwise. "Quantitative" marks measured or official figures; "Anecdote" marks single-business stories; "Vendor claim" marks figures from companies selling a related product. Reddit (r/singapore, r/SingaporeEats) could not be searched: the search tool returned "domains are not accessible" for reddit.com, so no Reddit evidence is included.

## 1. How Singapore home-based food businesses (HBFBs) find customers and take orders, and how large the scene is

### Takeaway
No one counts HBFBs. SFA says it does not track them, and the HDB/URA scheme needs no registration, so any size figure is a proxy. The evidence shows a social-first, fragmented model: Facebook groups during Covid-19, then Instagram and WhatsApp DMs, timed pre-order "drops", WhatsApp order-form tools such as Take App, Carousell's "Homemade bakes" category, and a handful of small home-cook marketplaces with unclear traction.

### Cited Findings
**Size (all proxies; no official count)**
- Quantitative/official: A written parliamentary reply on 14 Oct 2025 says SFA "does not track the number of home-based food businesses as they are not required to be licensed". The MP had asked for yearly counts since Covid-19. — [MSE written reply, 14 Oct 2025](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-home-based-food-businesses/)
- Official: A 3 Oct 2022 reply says SFA does not track home-based private dining businesses either and does not expect a substantial increase. — [MSE written reply, 3 Oct 2022](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-home-based-private-dining-businesses-in-singapore/)
- Official, via ST: URA and HDB do not track home-business numbers. The HBB scheme dates from the 1980s and needs no approval or licence. — [Straits Times via Yahoo, 15 Jun 2025](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/home-based-food-businesses-stir-210000014.html)
- Quantitative proxy: A Straits Times search found "more than 150 listings" of home F&B businesses (private dining, home cafés and bars), and ST said the true number is likely higher. — [Straits Times via Yahoo, 15 Jun 2025](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/home-based-food-businesses-stir-210000014.html)
- Directory claim: The "Home Bakers of SG" non-profit directory says it lists "More than 500 home bakers". This is self-curated and unverified. — [Home Bakers of SG](https://homebakersofsg.wixsite.com/website)
- UNVERIFIED and contradicted: A promotional blog about the Acuppa marketplace says "over 1,500 home-based food businesses registered in a year (SFA 2022 report)". This conflicts with SFA's statement that it does not track HBFBs, and no such SFA report was found. — [Maxthon blog, 19 Sep 2025](https://blog.maxthon.com/2025/09/19/how-acuppa-transformed-singapores-home-food-scene/), contradicted by [MSE reply, 14 Oct 2025](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-home-based-food-businesses/)

**Demand during Covid-19 (quantitative survey)**
- Visa/CLEAR survey of 1,000 Singaporeans aged 18–65, run in Sep 2020 and published 3 Sep 2021:
  - 31% said they shop more often at home-based businesses.
  - 14% expect buying from home-based businesses to remain a habit after the pandemic.
  - 41% said they shop more often via social media.
  - 71% said they use home delivery more often.
  — [Visa press release](https://www.visa.com.sg/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases/nearly-three-in-four-consumers-in-singapore-shop-online-more-frequently-due-to-covid-19-visa-study.html)

**Channels**
- Facebook groups were the Covid-era launchpad:
  - Tiap Tiap (Peranakan kueh) started in 2020 by posting in the "Singapore Home-cooked Delights" Facebook group. Strangers ordered and posted reviews there, which spread the word without marketing.
  - Banks and other organisations that wanted to support home businesses also placed orders.
  - The business later added "a structured order system".
  — [Vulcan Post, 7 Jul 2026](https://vulcanpost.com/912146/tiap-tiap-peranakan-food-singapore/)
- Quantitative: The "Hawkers United – Dabao 2020" Facebook group, which home bakers also used, reached 25,000 members in two days and about 238,000 later. Later coverage reported over 298.5K. — [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/696286/hawkers-united-facebook-group-singapore/)
- The "Pasar United" and "Sweets United" Facebook groups hosted people who had lost income selling family recipes. No member count is available. — [City Nomads](https://citynomads.com/food-from-the-heart-home-based-food-businesses-in-singapore-to-support/)
- Instagram/WhatsApp DMs and pre-order drops (listicles from 2020–2023):
  - Vegan or vegan-option home bakers take orders by Instagram DM, WhatsApp, a contact form or email.
  - Several open weekly pre-order windows that sell out.
  - Some accept WhatsApp orders only, not Instagram DMs.
  - Pickup is typically at an MRT station, with flat delivery fees around S$10.
  - Attribution across these listicles was not isolated per baker, so treat individual details as needing verification. — [Honeycombers home bakers](https://thehoneycombers.com/singapore/home-bakers-singapore/); [Eatbook home bakers](https://eatbook.sg/home-bakers/); [Her World](https://www.herworld.com/life/foodanddrink/must-know-local-home-bakers-bakeries-to-follow-on-instagram)
- Anecdote: Whiskdom opened orders "every Monday from 10am to 7pm". Its waitlist grew from 750 in July 2020 to about 3,000 two months later, which the founder said would take more than 2.5 years to clear. Growth came entirely from word of mouth. — [Vulcan Post, 27 Aug 2020](https://vulcanpost.com/710960/whiskdom-home-baker-singapore/)
- WhatsApp order-form tools: Take App began in Singapore during the pandemic so customers could pick items and pay without back-and-forth messages. The seller receives the order on WhatsApp.
  - Vendor claim: over 50% of Singapore stores on Take App are food businesses.
  - Vendor claim: the free tier allows 50 orders a month with no commission, and payment options include PayNow QR, cards, bank transfer and cash on delivery.
  - Vendor claims on scale conflict: 20,000+ merchants (2023 article) versus 300,000+ merchants (company About page).
  — [Take App Singapore page](https://www.take.app/country/singapore); [Take App About](https://www.take.app/about); [Vulcan Post, 24 Mar 2023 (sponsored)](https://vulcanpost.com/821127/take-app-simplify-whatsapp-ordering-singapore/)
- Carousell has a dedicated "Homemade bakes" category with a quota of 5 listings a month and a 60-day listing duration. — [Carousell SG category quota](https://support.carousell.com/hc/en-us/articles/15871353891097--Singapore-Category-based-listing-quota-for-all-categories)
- Carousell has a "home based food" search results page. — [Carousell](https://www.carousell.sg/home-based-food/q/)
- Kuih raya sellers on Carousell use "close once full" limits, deposits with the balance paid on collection, and fixed cut-off dates (e.g., 22 Mar 2025). — [Carousell kueh raya listings](https://www.carousell.sg/food-drinks/kueh-raya/q-196/), summarised via search
- Own websites: Some home bakers sell through their own sites plus Instagram, e.g., Baking with Gina's pineapple tarts. — [Time Out Singapore](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/restaurants/home-based-bakers-were-looking-forward-to-trying)
- Home-cook marketplaces already exist in Singapore. None has published traction data that could be verified:
  - **ManyChef** (May 2024): Stripe payments, PayNow payouts, orders up to 60 days ahead, filtering by kitchen type including "Vegan". It describes no seller verification. — [ManyChef press release, 27 May 2024](https://manychef.com/sg/manychef-singapore-launches-food-ordering-platform-featuring-hdb-ura-home-based-food-businesses-hbfbs/)
  - **BlockChef** (Jan 2024): launched with 24 creators and aimed for 10,000 by 2024. No evidence shows that target was met. — [Alvinology, 9 Jan 2024](https://alvinology.com/2024/01/09/singaporean-entrepreneur-launches-blockchef-an-online-food-marketplace-empowering-local-home-cooks/)
  - **Acuppa** (Nov 2024): vendor claim of about 20 growing to 300+ listings in under a year (unsourced), and a "chicken-and-egg" problem early on. — [Maxthon blog](https://blog.maxthon.com/2025/09/19/how-acuppa-transformed-singapores-home-food-scene/)
  - **Ownmades**: seller tools, free for 45 days. — [Ownmades merchant](https://merchant.ownmades.com/)
  - **Hcook**, **Share Food Singapore** and **The Collective** are older and smaller. — [Home & Decor](https://www.homeanddecor.com.sg/design/news/3-food-delivery-apps-for-ordering-home-cooked-meals/); [Honeycombers on The Collective](https://thehoneycombers.com/singapore/home-based-business-singapore-the-collective/)
- Some HBFBs sell on delivery apps: "Have A Bowl", a Bishan home-based business, took a foodpanda self-pickup order in March 2026. — [MS News, 10 Mar 2026](https://mustsharenews.com/foodpanda-false-refund/)
- TikTok Shop generally requires a registered Singapore business entity, which many HBFBs lack. — [Equinet Academy guide](https://www.equinetacademy.com/blog/how-to-sell-on-tiktok-shop-in-singapore-beginner-guide/)

### Inferences
- The typical order journey is: discovery on social media, then a DM or WhatsApp chat, then PayNow transfer and screenshot, then self-collection or a courier. Take App's founding premise, that owners had to "manually respond to and track many WhatsApp conversations", supports this.
- Covid-19 created a demand spike. In the Visa survey, only 14% of buyers expected the habit to stick, which matches Tiap Tiap's qualitative observation that many home businesses "faded" after restrictions eased. A marketplace should not assume the 2020 base still exists.
- Existing Singapore home-cook marketplaces are thin, and none shows verification. "Verified" plus vegetarian/vegan specialisation is unclaimed positioning, but cold-start (chicken-and-egg) risk is real.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifies channel shares for HBFBs (Instagram vs WhatsApp vs Facebook vs Telegram vs Carousell). Telegram-channel use by Singapore food sellers produced no citable sources.
- No count of vegan or vegetarian HBFBs exists. Reddit threads could not be accessed.
- There is no official Grab merchant page on whether HBFBs can join GrabFood. A secondary blog, about 585 days old, claims Grab requires an SFA licence valid for at least 2 months. This is UNVERIFIED, and the exact page was not isolated; candidates are the [Moonbeam HBFB guide](https://themoonbeam.co/the-comprehensive-guide-to-starting-your-own-home-based-food-business/) and the [Smart City Kitchens guide](https://www.smartcitykitchens.com/blog/how-to-start-a-home-based-food-business-in-singapore-2026-complete-guide/). SFA's own page says SFA does not restrict HBFBs from delivery platforms ([SFA](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)), and at least one HBFB was on foodpanda in March 2026 ([MS News](https://mustsharenews.com/foodpanda-false-refund/)). A HardwareZone thread titled "Now home businesses can set up in Foodpanda?" exists, but its date and content were not read ([HWZ](https://forums.hardwarezone.com.sg/threads/now-home-businesses-can-set-up-in-foodpanda.6709668/)).

## 2. Seller pain points (HBFBs)

### Takeaway
The documented pains are:
- Capacity ceilings set by the rules (no outside staff, no catering, no sales to retailers), which produce waitlists and sell-outs.
- Hours spent on manual DM order-taking, plus collection no-shows.
- Payment fraud (fake PayNow screenshots) and false refund claims on platforms.
- Costly, unreliable delivery for fragile food, at about S$10–18 per drop.
- Delivery-app commissions of roughly 25–30%.
- Regulatory and legitimacy anxiety, which grew in 2025 as restaurateurs and hawkers publicly challenged HBFBs.

Hard numbers are scarce. Most evidence is anecdote or official rules.

### Cited Findings
**Regulatory frame that shapes seller pain (official)**
- SFA requirements page (last updated 4 Aug 2026). HBFBs may not:
  - offer catering;
  - sell to SFA-licensed outlets (hawkers, restaurants, cafés, canteens);
  - prepare food for temporary fairs;
  - sell ready-to-eat raw seafood;
  - buy ingredients from other HBFBs (they are unlicensed);
  - use imported ingredients except through licensed importers.
- The same page says SFA "does not restrict" HBFBs from selling on delivery platforms such as GrabFood or foodpanda, but sellers must confirm each platform's own requirements. HBFBs may promote online and on labels, but no signage is allowed at the home.
- WSQ Food Safety Course Level 1 is encouraged.
— [SFA, Requirements for HBFBs](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- Official: SFA does not require HBFB owners to complete Food Safety Course Level 1 because their risk is "currently low", though it encourages it. — [MSE written reply, 24 Sep 2025](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-home-based-food-business-owners-/)
- Official: Under the new Food Safety and Security Bill, HBFBs stay unlicensed. SFA keeps powers to investigate food poisoning, issue directions, and "prescribe activities" that non-licensable businesses must not do, for which non-compliance becomes an offence. — [SFA FSSB consultation response, Nov 2024](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/public-consultation/summary-of-responses-to-comments-received-from-fssb-public-consultation-and-engagements_081124_.pdf)
- The Food Safety and Security Act 2025 replaces the Sale of Food Act. Tranche 1 took effect on 28 Nov 2025, per a secondary source. — [Act text, SSO](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025?ViewType=Pdf&_=20251229201918); [Covarage guide](https://covarage.com/guides/regulatory-change/fssa-tranche-1-28-november-2025-fb-product-liability)
- HDB scheme: only residents may work in the business, with no outside employees and no signage. — [GoBusiness HBB scheme](https://licensing.gobusiness.gov.sg/e-adviser/home-based-business/home-based-business-scheme)
- Quantitative/official, via ST:
  - MOM says domestic helpers may not assist. It received 14 complaints about this from 2022 to 2024, and the fine is up to S$10,000.
  - SFA recorded six enforcement cases against home businesses in 2024.
  - URA and HDB do not cap guest numbers but investigate complaints and may require operators to scale down.
  — [Straits Times via Yahoo, 15 Jun 2025](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/home-based-food-businesses-stir-210000014.html)

**Capacity, workload, burnout (anecdote)**
- Whiskdom's founder worked more than 16 hours a day while still employed, which she called "not going to be sustainable". She could not hire help because of HBB rules and moved to a central kitchen to scale. — [Vulcan Post, 27 Aug 2020](https://vulcanpost.com/710960/whiskdom-home-baker-singapore/)
- Tiap Tiap's founder handled production and logistics alone and once delivered to 150 locations in two days. She later moved to a 500 sq ft central kitchen and opened a S$500K shop in June 2026. About 40% of customers are repeat buyers from the home-based days. — [Vulcan Post, 7 Jul 2026](https://vulcanpost.com/912146/tiap-tiap-peranakan-food-singapore/)
- Lucky House private kitchen runs three tables and up to 30 diners a night, faces last-minute cancellations, and worries about neighbour complaints. — [ST via Yahoo, 15 Jun 2025](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/home-based-food-businesses-stir-210000014.html)
- Vegan/vegetarian festive capacity:
  - NVD (licensed) closed its vegan mooncake pre-orders "due to overwhelming responses and limited production capacity". — [NVD mooncakes](https://www.naturevegedelights.com.sg/nvd-mooncakes/)
  - Oatberry ran batch-based vegan mooncake pre-orders with dated batches. — [Oatberry](https://oatberry.com/en-us/pages/mooncakes)
  - The Clean Addicts' 2026 vegan mooncake box is listed as sold out. — [The Clean Addicts](https://thecleanaddicts.com/collections/wholefood-mooncakes-2026)

**Collection no-shows and order handling (anecdote)**
- A Sembawang home baker hung an order on her gate for a late customer. It was found half-eaten in a stairwell, and she then limited collection to allocated time slots. Another baker said the hardest part was fitting schedules around collections that customers ignored. — [Mothership, Sep 2020](https://mothership.sg/2020/09/home-baker-order-half-eaten/); [search summary of Wild Child SG, Jul 2020](https://wildchild.sg/2020/07/18/young-ambition-home-baker/)
- Take App's founding problem was owners manually responding to and tracking "many WhatsApp conversations". Its founder also cited customers "who are not tech-savvy and less committed" as a challenge. — [Vulcan Post, 24 Mar 2023](https://vulcanpost.com/821127/take-app-simplify-whatsapp-ordering-singapore/)
- BlockChef said home cooks struggled to "handle delivery logistics, set up online stores, and navigate the competitive online marketing space" after the pandemic. — [Alvinology, 9 Jan 2024](https://alvinology.com/2024/01/09/singaporean-entrepreneur-launches-blockchef-an-online-food-marketplace-empowering-local-home-cooks/)

**Payment fraud and refunds**
- Fake PayNow screenshots against food businesses. These are mostly licensed F&B, but the mechanism matches HBFB workflows:
  - A customer sent 35 doctored PayNow screenshots via WhatsApp to Home of Seafood (Joo Chiat), costing S$4,619.90. The restaurant required a screenshot before processing orders and did not reconcile against bank records. — [Mothership, Sep 2023](https://mothership.sg/2023/09/customer-scam-joo-chiat-restaurant-fake-paynow-screenshots/)
  - A 33-year-old woman pleaded guilty to obtaining more than S$9,000 of food over two years with edited screenshots. — [Malay Mail, 12 Nov 2025](https://malaymail.com/news/singapore/2025/11/12/woman-used-doctored-screenshots-to-cheat-singapore-restaurants-of-more-than-s9000-worth-of-food-over-two-years/198138)
  - Other cases: S$576.60 at House of Seafood ([Stomp](https://www.stomp.sg/singapore-seen/house-seafood-sends-out-576-60-order-after-getting-paynow-screenshot-turned-out-be-fake)); S$196.20 at a Katong Thai restaurant ([Mothership, Jan 2025](https://mothership.sg/2025/01/fake-paynow-thai-restaurant/)); S$55.50 at an East Coast hawker ([Mothership, Jan 2025](https://mothership.sg/2025/01/paynow-scam-hawker-east-coast/)); S$272.25 at an East Coast café ([Mothership, Sep 2024](https://mothership.sg/2024/09/woman-scam-cafe/)).
- SPF says some e-commerce scam victims are sellers who deliver goods after receiving fake "proof of payment" screenshots. — [SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- Platform refund abuse (anecdote, HBFB):
  - Have A Bowl (Bishan) photographed a S$64.60 foodpanda self-pickup order, and CCTV showed the customer leaving with it. The customer then claimed missing items, and foodpanda deducted the refund from the seller's earnings.
  - The seller was left with S$11.59. foodpanda reversed the deduction two days later, after the seller supplied evidence and posted on Facebook.
  — [MS News, 10 Mar 2026](https://mustsharenews.com/foodpanda-false-refund/)

**Delivery logistics and cost**
- Quantitative snapshot, updated 4 Jun 2024, for Bishan to Woodlands with an item under 3 kg:
  - GOGOX S$10 (with promo, 2–3 hrs)
  - Lalamove S$15
  - GrabExpress S$17 (subject to surge)
  - RydeSEND S$18.10
- The same article says GrabExpress does not handle fragile items such as cakes, RydeSEND accepts cakes at the sender's risk, and Lalamove has a food service.
— [DollarsAndSense](https://dollarsandsense.sg/business/lalamove-gogox-rydesend-grabexpress-which-delivery-service-home-based-businesses-use/)
- Lalamove says fares vary with traffic, order volume, driver availability, tolls and surcharges. — [Lalamove SG](https://www.lalamove.com/en-sg/personal)

**Delivery-app commissions and platform exit**
- Commission estimates conflict, and most come from vendors:
  - Eats365 says Grab has publicly stated GrabFood merchant commissions of 25–30%, plus an activation fee of S$100 (own device) or S$300 (Grab tablet).
  - Klikit lists GrabFood at 18–22% and foodpanda at 15–20%, with pandaPro at 12–15%.
  - Another comparison puts foodpanda at 25–35%.
  - A 2021 TODAY report put the three apps at 25–35%.
  — [Eats365](https://www.eats365pos.com/sg/blog/post/grabfood-vs-foodpanda-sg); [Klikit](https://klikit.io/en/learn/food-delivery-commission-fees-singapore-guide); [Klikit comparison](https://klikit.io/en/resources/comparisons/delivery-platforms-comparison-singapore)
- Deliveroo announced its Singapore wind-down on 25 Feb 2026 and stopped taking orders on 4 Mar 2026 after 11 years, under DoorDash ownership. That leaves GrabFood and foodpanda as the main apps. — [Mothership, Feb 2026](https://mothership.sg/2026/02/deliveroo-close/); [AsiaOne](https://www.asiaone.com/lifestyle/deliveroo-singapore-cease-operations); [Business Wire, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260224973889/en)

**Legitimacy and reputation pressure**
- Restaurateurs quoted by ST called home F&B "double standards", "cheating the system" and a regulatory "blind spot". One estimated a home restaurant could net S$15,000–17,000 a month. The Restaurant Association of Singapore asked for clearer definitions. — [ST via Yahoo, 15 Jun 2025](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/home-based-food-businesses-stir-210000014.html)
- A hawker's TikTok (Jul 2025) said HBBs avoid rent, licensing and manpower costs and called for licensing and safety checks. — [MS News, 25 Jul 2025](https://mustsharenews.com/tiktok-fb-home-based-sellers/)
- Reviews as leverage: a home baker tried to get a customer to remove a 1-star review after botching a S$400 cake (headline only; old). — [Mothership, Sep 2017](https://mothership.sg/2017/09/spore-home-baker-tried-to-get-customer-to-remove-1-star-review-after-botching-s400-cake/)
- Regulatory shocks: during the April 2020 circuit breaker, HBFBs were not allowed to operate. One owner said her orders "went back to zero". — [Vulcan Post, 2020](https://vulcanpost.com/697458/pleas-of-home-based-food-businesses-singapore/), via search summary

**Marketing and discovery**
- Promotional agency claims: TikTok has overtaken Instagram for Singaporeans under 30, and Instagram organic reach is 10–25% of followers. Both are UNVERIFIED and unsourced. — [Marketing agency blog](https://marketingagency.sg/social-commerce-singapore/), via search summary
- Vendor claim: Acuppa cites home chefs lacking "efficient ways to reach customers beyond their immediate circles". — [Maxthon blog](https://blog.maxthon.com/2025/09/19/how-acuppa-transformed-singapores-home-food-scene/)

### Inferences
- The binding constraints are capacity, because household-only labour and no catering are allowed, and admin time spent on DMs, payment checks and collection coordination. Features that save admin time and smooth capacity are likely to matter more to HBFBs than raw discovery once demand exists. These include slot caps, cut-offs, deposits and automated payment confirmation.
- Fake-screenshot fraud and false refund claims both come from weak payment and evidence trails. Gateway-confirmed PayNow (dynamic QR plus webhook) and handover proof (timestamped pickup confirmation) address real, documented losses.
- Courier delivery costs S$10–18 per drop and couriers handle cakes unevenly, so pickup-point coordination and batched delivery days are likely more viable than on-demand delivery for HBFB economics.
- The 2025 public debate means HBFBs may value a credible "verified" badge (identity plus SFA rule compliance plus optional Food Safety Course Level 1 certificate) as reputational cover. Verification must not imply SFA licensing, because HBFBs are not licensed.
- The SFA rule against buying ingredients from other HBFBs means a marketplace should not encourage HBFB-to-HBFB supply. Business-to-business listings for HBFBs should be restricted to licensed suppliers.

### Gaps
- No Singapore survey quantifies HBFB no-show rates, unpaid-order rates, time spent on admin, average order value, or income.
- No Singapore-specific evidence was found on Instagram algorithm changes hurting HBFB sales; only agency blogs exist.
- No official GrabFood or foodpanda policy on HBFB onboarding was found. Grab's 25–30% figure is relayed by a POS vendor, not read on a Grab page.
- Burnout evidence is limited to a few profiles from 2020–2026.

## 3. Buyer pain points (including vegetarian, vegan, five-pungent-free and Jain buyers)

### Takeaway
The documented buyer worries are:
- Food safety at unlicensed kitchens. SFA tells consumers to "exercise discretion", and there are rare but serious outbreaks (Peachy Sugarmaker 2021, 47 ill) and six SFA enforcement actions in 2024.
- Paying upfront to strangers. E-commerce is Singapore's most common scam type, and pre-order deposits by PayNow are the textbook mechanism.
- Brands that hide who actually cooks the food. Twelve web-based meal brands, including one called "Vegetarian Buffet", were all supplied by one caterer whose licence was cancelled.
- Unreliable ingredient and allergen information. Vegetarian and Jain buyers face hidden onion or garlic in spice mixes and an unregulated "plant-based" label.
- Constant sell-outs and pre-order windows.

### Cited Findings
**Food safety and trust in unlicensed kitchens**
- SFA media reply (4 May 2022): consumers "should exercise discretion when buying from home-based businesses". HBFBs that follow SFA guidelines need no licence, and growing businesses should move to licensed premises. — [SFA media reply](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/news-publications/newsroom/before-2024/news-publications-reply-on-home-based-food-businesses-hbfbs)
- Quantitative: The Peachy Sugarmaker home-based business outbreak in August 2021 sickened 47 people, about a dozen of whom were hospitalised, reportedly with Salmonella. SFA suspended its sales from 13 Aug to 28 Sep 2021, and the operator had to sanitise, replace equipment and pass Food Safety Course Level 1. — [Food Safety News, Aug 2021](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2021/08/home-business-linked-to-outbreak-in-singapore/); [Goody Feed](https://goodyfeed.com/the-peachy-sugarmaker/)
- Quantitative: SFA recorded six enforcement cases (recalls or stop orders) against home businesses in 2024. — [ST via Yahoo, 15 Jun 2025](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/home-based-food-businesses-stir-210000014.html); [MS News, Jul 2025](https://mustsharenews.com/tiktok-fb-home-based-sellers/)
- No public SFA suspension of an HBFB over gastroenteritis was found for 2023–2025. Recent large outbreaks involved licensed caterers, e.g., Shiok Kitchen Catering (95 people, Dec 2023) and Stamford Catering (182 people across 2024–25). — [MOH, Shiok Kitchen](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/suspension-of-shiok-kitchen-catering-pte-ltd-s-food-business-operations/); [The Independent SG, Stamford](https://theindependent.sg/singapore-food-agency-fines-caterer-s-8-000-after-182-people-suffered-gastroenteritis-food-safety-lapses-found/)

**Who is actually cooking: the brand-to-kitchen opacity**
- In May 2024 SFA cancelled Sakura Buffet's catering licence over complaints of undercooked or mouldy food and insects. SFA found 12 web-based brands supplied by it and told consumers not to order from them. They included confinement-meal brands, "Home Tingkat Catering" and "Vegetarian Buffet". CASE received 22 related complaints between 8 and 10 May 2024. — [AsiaOne](https://www.asiaone.com/singapore/catering-firm-sakura-buffets-licence-cancelled-after-reports-meals-insects-sfa-urges-not); [Mothership, May 2024](https://mothership.sg/2024/05/sakura-buffet-sfa-case/)
- Quantitative (CASE, 2024):
  - Food and beverage complaints rose 24%, from 571 to 708, "largely due to" the Sakura Buffet case.
  - E-commerce complaints hit a record 4,641 (+25%), of which about 9% were F&B.
  - Prepayment losses quadrupled to about S$1.93M, from S$476K, driven by "sudden business closures and companies becoming unresponsive after consumers had made payment".
  — [CASE media release, Feb 2025](https://www.case.org.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Media-Release-CASE-sees-prepayment-losses-more-than-quadruple-in-2024-entertainment-related-complaints-nearly-triple.pdf)

**Allergen and ingredient transparency**
- SFA requires prepacked food to declare allergens: gluten cereals, crustacea, eggs, fish, peanuts, soybeans, milk (including lactose), tree nuts, and sulphites at 10 mg/kg or more. There is no legal framework for "may contain" precautionary labels. — [SFA food allergy](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-safety-tips/food-risk-concerns/food-allergy-intolerance); [SFA labelling guide](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-manufacturing-and-storage/a-guide-to-food-labelling-and-advertisements.pdf)
- No HBFB exemption was found, but no HBFB-specific labelling guidance was found either. Since 30 Jan 2026 an ingredient list must have a heading such as "Ingredients" (secondary summary). — [K&L Gates summary](https://www.khlaw.com/insights/singapore-revises-food-labeling-rules-and-refines-food-regulatory-framework)
- On digital listings, a respondent to SFA's consultation asked for labelling laws to cover foods on digital platforms. SFA pointed to the voluntary standard SS 687:2022 (Guidelines for E-Commerce), which covers what to include in sale listings, and reminded consumers "to exercise caution when purchasing food online". — [SFA FSSB consultation response, Nov 2024](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/public-consultation/summary-of-responses-to-comments-received-from-fssb-public-consultation-and-engagements_081124_.pdf)
- A trade outlet reported in March 2025 that Singapore's tightened prepacked labelling rules excluded online platforms (headline only). — [BakeryandSnacks, 3 Mar 2025](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2025/03/03/singapore-tightens-pre-packaged-food-labelling-regulations-with-focus-on-gluten-online-platforms-excluded/)

**Vegetarian, vegan, five-pungent-free and Jain specifics**
- Jain catering guidance warns that "many spice mixes contain hidden onion or garlic". — [Nalan Jain catering guide](https://www.nalan.com.sg/blog/jain-food-catering-menu-guidance)
- Strict Jain practice also avoids root vegetables (potato, carrot, beetroot, radish, yam), honey and mushrooms, and Tekka hawker stalls are described as "hit-or-miss". This comes from a travel blog, so it has low authority. — [Happyfares Jain guide](https://www.happyfares.in/vegetarian-jain-food/singapore)
- A Tripadvisor review says a no-onion, no-garlic restaurant still uses potatoes unless asked, so buyers must confirm preparation. — [Tripadvisor, Gokul](https://www.tripadvisor.com.sg/ShowUserReviews-g294265-d2628156-r554725595-Gokul_Vegetarian-Singapore.html)
- A blog (low authority) says Singapore dishes labelled "vegetarian" may contain egg, dairy or fish sauce, that the 素 sign means only no meat, and that "plant-based" is unregulated. — [The Kind Bowl blog](https://thekindbowl.wixsite.com/home/post/the-honest-guide-vegan-vs-vegetarian-confusion-in-singapore-restaurants)
- Supply exists: ManyChef offers a "Vegan" kitchen-type filter, and vegan home bakers operate on Instagram. — [ManyChef](https://manychef.com/sg/manychef-singapore-launches-food-ordering-platform-featuring-hdb-ura-home-based-food-businesses-hbfbs/); [Honeycombers](https://thehoneycombers.com/singapore/home-bakers-singapore/)

**Availability and ordering friction**
- Sell-outs and timed drops are the norm:
  - Whiskdom's waitlist reached about 3,000. — [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/710960/whiskdom-home-baker-singapore/)
  - Some kueh makers' limited batches are "usually snapped up within minutes". — [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/restaurants/home-based-bakers-were-looking-forward-to-trying)
  - Raya home bakers have pre-order cut-off dates. — [GoCompare Raya 2026](https://www.gocompare.sg/raya-cookies-singapore-2026-10-best-places-to-buy-kuih-raya-for-hari-raya-aidilfitri/)
- The Visa survey found 31% of buyers bought more often from home businesses during Covid-19, but only 14% expected the habit to persist. — [Visa, 2021](https://www.visa.com.sg/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases/nearly-three-in-four-consumers-in-singapore-shop-online-more-frequently-due-to-covid-19-visa-study.html)

### Inferences
- Buyer trust requires two links: a verifiable person or business behind the storefront, and the actual kitchen location (home vs licensed premises) behind the brand. The Sakura Buffet case shows that brand storefronts can hide the real kitchen, and one of them used a vegetarian name.
- Vegetarian, five-pungent-free and Jain buyers need structured, seller-declared ingredient data with "contains" and "may contain" and compound-ingredient disclosure, not a single "vegetarian" tag. Hidden alliums in sauces and spice mixes are the documented failure mode. Declarations should be labelled seller-declared rather than certified.
- Following SS 687:2022 for listing content would align with SFA's stated expectations even though it is voluntary.
- Because SFA itself tells consumers to use discretion and does not license HBFBs, a marketplace must not imply SFA approval of home kitchens.

### Gaps
- No Singapore consumer survey on trust in HBFBs, willingness to pay upfront, or allergen worries specific to home food was found.
- No documented 2022–2026 case was found of "vegetarian" or "vegan" home food containing meat or alliums in Singapore. Searches returned nothing.
- No data exists on how many buyers avoid HBFBs because of payment risk.

## 4. Small licensed F&B businesses (cafés, vegan bakeries, vegetarian stalls) selling online

### Takeaway
Licensed small operators are squeezed by GrabFood and foodpanda commissions, commonly cited at 25–30%, and lose customer data to the apps. Deliveroo's March 2026 exit cut them to two main apps. The usual response is a direct-ordering channel (Oddle, own site, Take App), especially for festive pre-orders, where capacity-limited batches sell out (vegan mooncakes in 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Commission ranges conflict: 25–30% (Eats365, citing Grab), 18–22% for GrabFood and 15–20% for foodpanda (Klikit), and 25–35% for foodpanda in another comparison. Klikit says merchants with 200+ orders a month may negotiate 2–5 points off. All sources are vendor blogs. — [Eats365](https://www.eats365pos.com/sg/blog/post/grabfood-vs-foodpanda-sg); [Klikit](https://klikit.io/en/resources/comparisons/delivery-platforms-comparison-singapore)
- Vendor analysis: at 25–30% commission a S$20 meal nets about S$14–15 before food, labour and packaging. Platforms keep customer data, so restaurants "pay commission repeatedly to reacquire the same customer". — [Warely](https://warely.ai/food-delivery-commission-in-singapore-platforms-vs-your-own-channel/); [MegaPOS](https://www.megapos.com.sg/is-fully-relying-on-delivery-platforms-still-viable-for-f-b-businesses-in-singapore)
- Oddle pricing conflicts: one comparison cites about 10% commission, another S$300 a month (1-year term) covering up to S$3,000 of orders, plus 6.5% above that. Both are third-party and unofficial. — [Chachacha comparison](https://www.chachacha.com.sg/best-online-ordering-system-singapore/); [Eats365 ordering systems](https://www.eats365pos.com/sg/blog/post/online-ordering-systems-singapore)
- Festive pre-orders through direct channels (Oddle pages) continue. For example, Goodwood Park listed CNY 2026 pre-orders from 11 Jan for collection or delivery from 23 Jan to 3 Mar 2026. No Oddle festive volume data was found. — [Daniel Food Diary, CNY 2026](https://danielfooddiary.com/2026/01/25/chinesenewyear2026/)
- Vegan and vegetarian festive capacity:
  - NVD's vegan mooncake pre-order closed early because of demand and limited capacity. — [NVD](https://www.naturevegedelights.com.sg/nvd-mooncakes/)
  - Mdm Ling Bakery's vegetarian (not vegan) gift sets sell out before the festival. — [Mdm Ling](https://www.mdmlingbakery.com/exploring-halal-and-vegetarian-mooncake-options/)
  - Our Vegan Bakery (Kovan) requires 2-day advance orders with a 12–2pm collection window. — [HappyCow](https://www.happycow.net/reviews/our-vegan-bakery-northeast-singapore-359442)
- Fraud risk for licensed merchants: scammers impersonate food-delivery merchant-partners to redirect payouts. The SPF 2025 brief names Grab, Deliveroo and foodpanda. — [SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- Deliveroo's exit took effect on 4 Mar 2026. Most restaurants were already also on GrabFood and/or foodpanda. — [MoneyBees](https://themoneybees.co/blog/deliveroo-singapore-guide); [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2026/02/deliveroo-close/)

### Inferences
- A low or zero-commission marketplace that hands licensed sellers the customer relationship (repeat ordering, saved buyers, festive reminders) addresses the most-cited licensed-seller complaint. Pre-order batch scheduling with capacity caps fits their festive peaks (CNY, Mid-Autumn, Deepavali, Hari Raya).
- Licensed sellers can be verified against SFA licence records, which is stronger than HBFB verification. The marketplace may need two tiers: "SFA-licensed premises" versus "home-based (unlicensed by law; identity verified)".

### Gaps
- No primary data covers Singapore small-F&B direct-ordering adoption or festive revenue share. Oddle and Grab official pricing pages were not retrieved.
- No Deepavali-specific seller data was found.

## 5. Features sellers and buyers say they want, or that existing tools emphasise

### Takeaway
Very little Singapore evidence is a stated "wish list". Most is revealed preference: what sellers built or adopted (order forms, weekly drop windows, collection time slots, deposits, cut-offs) and what platforms and regulators emphasise (secure payment, government-ID verification, listing information under SS 687). Treat this section as features implied by documented pains, not survey-validated demand.

### Cited Findings
- **Order forms in place of DMs:** Take App lets customers choose items "without having to ask the owners questions". It offers PayNow QR, card and COD, a customer database and newsletter tools, and planned WhatsApp Q&A bots. — [Vulcan Post, 2023](https://vulcanpost.com/821127/take-app-simplify-whatsapp-ordering-singapore/); [Take App SG](https://www.take.app/country/singapore)
- **Pre-order scheduling and capacity windows:** Whiskdom took orders only on Mondays 10am–7pm, and vegan mooncake sellers ran batch dates (Oatberry). — [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/710960/whiskdom-home-baker-singapore/); [Oatberry](https://oatberry.com/en-us/pages/mooncakes)
- **Allocated collection slots:** a home baker made these stricter after a missed pickup. — [Mothership, 2020](https://mothership.sg/2020/09/home-baker-order-half-eaten/)
- **Deposits and cut-offs:** Raya and CNY home sellers use deposits and order-close dates. — [GoCompare](https://www.gocompare.sg/raya-cookies-singapore-2026-10-best-places-to-buy-kuih-raya-for-hari-raya-aidilfitri/); [Kwan Pastry](https://kwanpastry.com/)
- **Dynamic PayNow QR:** static PayNow QR lets customers mistype amounts or omit references, which dynamic QR avoids. A UEN-linked QR hides personal account numbers. These are vendor claims. — [Wise PayNow QR guide](https://wise.com/sg/blog/how-to-generate-paynow-qr-code); [Wise PayNow Corporate](https://wise.com/sg/blog/paynow-corporate)
- **Marketplace feature sets:**
  - ManyChef: Stripe payments, PayNow payouts, orders up to 60 days ahead, seller dashboard, cuisine and "Vegan" filters. — [ManyChef](https://manychef.com/sg/manychef-singapore-launches-food-ordering-platform-featuring-hdb-ura-home-based-food-businesses-hbfbs/)
  - Ownmades: menu updates, real-time order tracking, discovery by nearby customers. — [Ownmades](https://merchant.ownmades.com/)
  - Acuppa: secure payments and storytelling content, with ratings and reviews only proposed (vendor). — [Maxthon blog](https://blog.maxthon.com/2025/09/19/how-acuppa-transformed-singapores-home-food-scene/)
- **Verification and secure payment as anti-scam measures (official):**
  - MHA's E-commerce Marketplace Transaction Safety Rating (since May 2022) recommends "user verification against Government-issued documentation and secure payment options". Marketplaces with these features (Amazon, Lazada, Shopee, TikTok Shop) saw "significantly fewer" e-commerce scams than Carousell and Facebook Marketplace.
  - Meta's government-ID verification for all Facebook ads coincided with about 38% fewer Facebook scam cases in Jul–Sep 2025 than in the same period of 2024.
  — [SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- **Evidence trail for disputes:** an HBFB with order photos and CCTV won a false missing-item dispute with foodpanda. — [MS News, Mar 2026](https://mustsharenews.com/foodpanda-false-refund/)
- **Listing information standard:** SFA encourages sellers to follow SS 687:2022 for e-commerce listings. — [SFA FSSB consultation response](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/public-consultation/summary-of-responses-to-comments-received-from-fssb-public-consultation-and-engagements_081124_.pdf)
- **Low or no commission:** competitors lead with free registration or "no commission" offers (Take App free tier, ManyChef free kitchen registration, Ownmades 45 days free). The Philippine marketplace Suki Neighbors offers zero commission (regional, labelled). — [Take App](https://www.take.app/country/singapore); [ManyChef](https://manychef.com/sg/manychef-singapore-launches-food-ordering-platform-featuring-hdb-ura-home-based-food-businesses-hbfbs/); [Suki Neighbors (Philippines)](https://sukineighbors.com/compare/can-home-cooks-join-grabfood-or-foodpanda)

### Inferences
Priority features follow from documented pains:
1. Pre-order drops with per-slot capacity caps and cut-offs.
2. Gateway-confirmed PayNow (dynamic QR plus automatic reconciliation) or escrow-style hold until pickup, which removes fake screenshots and upfront-to-stranger risk.
3. Pickup slots with reminders and no-show and deposit policies.
4. A structured ingredient and allergen panel aligned with SS 687 and SFA's allergen list, with five-pungent, Jain, egg and dairy flags marked as seller-declared.
5. A tiered verified badge: identity via government ID or Singpass/UEN; SFA licence for licensed premises; optional Food Safety Course Level 1 certificate for HBFBs.
6. An order dashboard replacing DMs and spreadsheets.
7. Timestamped handover proof for disputes.
8. Low or no commission plus festive-season tooling.

Reviews are commonly proposed, but evidence that buyers or sellers asked for them in Singapore is thin, and review pressure has caused conflict (the 2017 1-star case).

### Gaps
- No Singapore survey of HBFB or buyer feature preferences was found. Feature demand is inferred from behaviour and official anti-scam guidance.
- No evidence was found on willingness to pay subscription fees versus commission among Singapore HBFBs.

## 6. Scam landscape relevant to seller verification

### Takeaway
E-commerce scams remained Singapore's most-reported scam type in 2025: 6,703 cases and S$16.7M lost. The standard pattern is "pre-order, then pay a deposit by PayNow, then the seller vanishes", mostly on Carousell (29.0%) and Facebook Marketplace (22.2%). Food has been targeted directly through 2023 Facebook and Instagram mooncake scams (27 victims, S$325K in a month) and food-themed phishing ads. Official guidance credits government-ID verification and secure payments with reducing scams.

### Cited Findings
- **Overall 2025 (quantitative, SPF):**
  - Scam cases fell 27.6% to 37,308, and losses fell 17.9% to about S$913.1M.
  - 81.8% of scams involved self-effected transfers.
  — [SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf); [SPF news, Feb 2026](https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/police-life/2026/02/scams-and-cybercrime-fell-by-almost-a-quarter-in-2025)
- **E-commerce scams (quantitative, SPF 2025):**
  - Cases fell 42.5% to 6,703 (from 11,665 in 2024) but remained the most-reported scam type. Losses fell 4.6% to about S$16.7M; the average loss was S$2,503.
  - Carousell accounted for 29.0% of cases and Facebook Marketplace for 22.2%.
  - Victims "expressing interest in the product or pre-order" were told to pay "an initial deposit via PayNow or bank transfer".
  - 43.8% of victims were aged 30–49.
  - Pokémon cards made up 13.6% of cases. No food category was broken out.
  - Some victims were sellers who received fake payment screenshots.
  — [SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- **First half of 2025:** e-commerce cases fell 55.2% to 3,237 (from 7,224). E-commerce scams claimed 34.6% of youth victims. — [SPF Mid-Year Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Mid-Year-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Police-News-Release---Mid-Year-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- **Food-themed phishing:** SPF says victims meet "food-related advertisements" on Facebook, TikTok and Instagram that lead to phishing links. There were 2,670 cases of social-media-ad-led phishing in 2025. — [SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- **Mooncake scam (official advisory, 5 Sep 2023):**
  - At least 27 victims lost at least S$325,000 in August 2023.
  - Facebook and Instagram ads moved buyers to WhatsApp, then to malicious APK "payment" links.
  - In another variant, victims paid by PayNow, were told the order was cancelled, and were sent an APK for a "refund".
  — [SPF advisory](https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/news/2023/20230905_police_advisory_on_new_variant_of_scams_involving_the_sale_of_mooncakes); [MS News](https://mustsharenews.com/mooncake-scam/)
- **Verification works, per official claims:**
  - Codes of Practice (June 2024) require Meta, Telegram, WeChat and Carousell to have anti-scam systems.
  - Meta's government-ID checks on all Facebook ads coincided with about 38% fewer Facebook scam cases (Jul–Sep 2025 year on year).
  - The Transaction Safety Rating recommends government-ID user verification and secure payment.
  - Carousell scam cases fell 24.6% and Facebook Marketplace 23.5% in 2025; Shopee fell 60.4%.
  — [SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- **Platforms used to contact scam victims (SPF 2025):** social media cases were Facebook 51.9%, TikTok 26.0% and Instagram 14.2%. Messaging cases were WhatsApp 53.5% and Telegram 37.9%. — [SPF Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025](https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf)
- **Prepayment and closure losses (CASE 2024):** about S$1.93M, four times the 2023 figure. CASE advises that large prepayments go to accredited businesses. — [CASE, Feb 2025](https://www.case.org.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Media-Release-CASE-sees-prepayment-losses-more-than-quadruple-in-2024-entertainment-related-complaints-nearly-triple.pdf)
- No 2024–2026 SPF advisory specifically on mooncake or CNY-goodies scams was found in searches.

### Inferences
- The HBFB ordering pattern (a social ad or post, then a WhatsApp chat, then a PayNow deposit for a future pre-order) is the same pattern SPF describes for e-commerce scams. Buyers have rational reasons to hesitate, and genuine sellers bear that trust cost.
- Government-ID verification (Singpass/Myinfo, or UEN for businesses) and in-app, platform-confirmed payment directly match MHA's Transaction Safety Rating recommendations. They are the strongest evidence-backed basis for a "verified seller" feature.
- Keeping chat and payment inside the app, with no off-platform payment links and no APKs, addresses the documented mooncake variant.

### Gaps
- SPF does not break out food or pre-order-food scams. The share of e-commerce scams involving food is unknown.
- No 2024–2026 festive-food scam advisory was found, and none was found for Hari Raya or Deepavali goodies specifically.
