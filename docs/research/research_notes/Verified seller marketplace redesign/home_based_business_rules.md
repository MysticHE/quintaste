# Singapore home-based food business (HBFB) rules: what a verified-seller marketplace can lawfully support

Research as of 17 October 2026. Primary = sfa.gov.sg, mse.gov.sg, ura.gov.sg, mccy.gov.sg, SFA PDFs, the Act text. Secondary = law-firm, commercial and news pages, labelled where used. "Snippet-only" means the claim came from a search-result excerpt of the page and the page itself was not opened (fetch was blocked or returned 404). UNVERIFIED means no adequate source was found.

## 1. SFA requirements for HBFBs: licence, sourcing, prohibitions, hygiene, enforcement, and which products are allowed

### Takeaway
On 17 Oct 2026 an HBFB still needs no SFA licence, but food law applies in full. HBFBs may not cater (SFA counts buffet lines and packed meals as catering), supply SFA-licensed retailers, prepare food for temporary fairs, sell ready-to-eat raw seafood, or use unlicensed, self-imported or travel-purchased ingredients. Baked goods, kueh, sauces, frozen dumplings and drinks are not named as prohibited. They are allowed only if made at home for direct sale to consumers at small scale. Meal-prep or bento "packed meals" sit inside SFA's catering prohibition, and SFA does not define where that line falls.

### Cited Findings
**Licence status and legal basis**
- SFA's "Requirements for Home-based Food Businesses" page (FAQ dated 04 Aug 2026; footer 16 Oct 2026) says HBFBs "currently do not require an SFA licence to operate" but must still comply with food safety requirements — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- The reason for the exemption is scale: HDB and URA allow HBFBs only "on a small scale", which limits how much food is prepared and sold, so SFA does not license them (Second Reading closing speech, Food Safety and Security Bill, 8 Jan 2025, para 4.1) — [MSE, SMS Koh Poh Koon speech](https://mse.gov.sg/latest-news/2nd-reading-food-safety-security-bill-closing-speech-sms-koh-poh-koon)
- HBFBs remain subject to the Environmental Public Health Act and the Sale of Food Act and must ensure food is safe to eat (written replies of 4 Oct 2021 and 3 Oct 2022) — [MSE written reply, 4 Oct 2021](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-hbfb/); [MSE reply on home-based private dining, 3 Oct 2022](https://mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-home-based-private-dining-businesses-in-singapore)
- Sale of Food Act s 15: a person "must not sell food that the person knows or ought reasonably to know is unsafe" (or unsuitable). EPHA s 40 bars possessing for retail sale food that is "unsound or unfit for human consumption" (both quoted in SFA's e-commerce guidelines Annex A, 20 Sep 2021) — [SFA e-commerce guidelines PDF](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- SFA does not count HBFBs because they are unlicensed (reply of 14 Oct 2025) — [MSE reply, 14 Oct 2025](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-home-based-food-businesses/). Under the 2021 reply, HDB and URA keep no records either, because no approval is needed — [MSE reply, 4 Oct 2021](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-hbfb/)

**Prohibitions on the SFA page (current Oct 2026)**
- **Catering:** prohibited. SFA counts buffet lines and packed meals as catering, and only SFA-licensed businesses may offer catering. Licensed caterers may not cater from home either — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- **Supplying retailers:** an HBFB is "not allowed to sell your food to food establishments licensed by SFA", such as hawker stalls, restaurants, coffee shops, cafes, canteens and temporary fair stalls — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses); [SFA "About HDB/URA scheme" page, body dated 27 Mar 2026](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/about-hdb-ura-home-based-business-scheme)
- **Temporary fairs:** an HBFB may not prepare food at home for a temporary fair, even with a fair stall licence, or sell to stallholders at a fair — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- **Ready-to-eat raw seafood:** strictly prohibited, for example sashimi, oysters and raw marinated seafood, including sea urchins, clams and fish eggs — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- **Sourcing:** use only legally imported ingredients or ingredients from licensed manufacturers and suppliers. Do not buy from other HBFBs, because they are unlicensed. Food brought back from travel is for private consumption only. Importing for sale needs an SFA licence — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses). Food brought in by returning travellers or by parcel post is "only allowed for personal consumption and not for sale", and selling it without an import permit is an offence — [SFA e-commerce guidelines, 20 Sep 2021](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- **Meat and seafood:** operators should sell only meat and seafood legally imported under the Wholesome Meat and Fish Act. This is snippet-only, attributed to an SFA HBFB page that now returns 404 — [SFA HDB/URA page (snippet)](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/home-based-food-business)

**SFA's reasons for the catering, retail and fair bans**
- SFA forum reply, 6 Dec 2024: HBFBs "are not allowed to distribute food to retail food establishments and temporary fair stalls", nor to cater, because doing so "could lead to them operating beyond the capacity afforded by a home setting". HBFBs that want to grow should consider "licensed food premises" — [SFA, "Risk-based approach in regulating industry", 6 Dec 2024](https://www.beta.sfa.gov.sg/news-publications/newsroom/2024/risk-based-approach-in-regulating-industry)
- SFA hygiene guidelines for HBFBs (updated 29 Apr 2020):
  - Do not offer catering services, "including food stations and stalls at events". Large quantities in small kitchens mean dishes are prepared in advance, which raises contamination risk in the 5°C–60°C danger zone.
  - "If you intend to prepare food for a larger number of clients, or prepare food in large quantity for each order, you should shift to a proper licensed food premises."
  - [SFA Guidelines on Food Safety & Hygiene Practices for Residents Preparing Food under HDB/URA Scheme (PDF, 29 Apr 2020)](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/tools-and-resources/food-retail-and-businesses/sfa-good-food-hygiene-tips-for-residents-preparing-food-under-hdb-ura-guidelines)
- In 2021, SFA said food under the scheme is prepared "for only a very small number of people" and that operators who follow its hygiene guidelines "will not require a licence from SFA" (SFA e-commerce guidelines FAQ v, 20 Sep 2021) — [SFA e-commerce guidelines PDF](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)

**SFA hygiene guidelines for HBFBs (29 Apr 2020, still linked from the 2026 SFA page)**
- Keep food preparation areas clean.
- No pets roaming freely in the house.
- Obtain supplies from licensed or approved sources and check expiry dates.
- Store food covered, with raw food sealed below cooked or ready-to-eat food.
- Thaw safely and do not refreeze.
- Use separate gloves and utensils for raw and ready-to-eat food.
- Keep hot food above 60°C and cold food below 5°C, and "Do not cook food too early".
- Do not handle food when unwell, and wash hands.
- "Wear face mask during food preparation" (a COVID-era 2020 tip).
- Source: [SFA hygiene guidelines PDF](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/tools-and-resources/food-retail-and-businesses/sfa-good-food-hygiene-tips-for-residents-preparing-food-under-hdb-ura-guidelines)

**Enforcement and recall**
- SFA acts on breaches such as selling unsafe food. HBFBs implicated in incidents "may be directed to stop operations immediately or to recall food products sold" — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses); [SFA forum reply, 6 Dec 2024](https://www.beta.sfa.gov.sg/news-publications/newsroom/2024/risk-based-approach-in-regulating-industry)
- SFA can direct an HBFB to stop if its activities threaten food safety, and the FSS Bill "does not change this" (8 Jan 2025, para 4.3) — [MSE speech](https://mse.gov.sg/latest-news/2nd-reading-food-safety-security-bill-closing-speech-sms-koh-poh-koon)
- Home-based private dining is permitted under the HDB and URA schemes and is unlicensed. SFA does not track how many exist (3 Oct 2022) — [MSE reply, 3 Oct 2022](https://mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-home-based-private-dining-businesses-in-singapore)

### Inferences
- **Product types for the app (none of these is expressly listed by SFA, except baking):**
  - Cakes, cookies, breads and kueh: lawful. Small-scale baking is the scheme's own listed example (URA and HDB). Ingredients must come from licensed sources, and no raw ready-to-eat seafood may be used.
  - Sauces, sambal, achar, frozen dumplings and frozen kueh: no SFA prohibition was found. They are lawful at small scale for direct consumer sale. Risks are food safety for low-acid or oil-preserved products (e.g. garlic in oil; not researched) and the scheme's limits on bulk storage and non-domestic equipment.
  - Drinks: no HBFB-specific prohibition was found. Alcohol requires a separate liquor licence, which was not researched (see Gaps).
  - Meal-prep and bento: highest risk. "Packed meals" are expressly a form of catering, and the 2020 guidance says large-quantity orders belong in licensed premises. A cautious reading:
    - Individually made-to-order meals for a household are probably not "catering".
    - Office or event orders, multi-person bento runs, buffet trays and recurring bulk meal-prep subscriptions are likely "catering" or beyond home scale.
    - SFA has not published a numeric threshold, so this distinction is an inference.
- **What the app should block:**
  - "Catering", "buffet", "event", "corporate order", "party tray", "bento for N pax" listing types.
  - B2B or wholesale sales to cafes and shops.
  - Pop-up or fair stall listings.
  - Raw ready-to-eat seafood. This is unlikely on a vegetarian app, but some vegetarian products contain fish sauce or roe.
  - Resale of other sellers' food.
- **Seller declarations the app could require:** licensed ingredient sourcing, and no resale of travel-bought or self-imported food.

### Gaps
- SFA has published no quantitative definition of "packed meals", "large quantity" or "small scale" (orders/day, pax/order, kg) for HBFBs. The FSSA speech's "200 kg per month" small-scale figure applies to community gardens and edible-plant producers, not HBFBs, and should not be transposed.
- No SFA statement specifically on meal-prep subscriptions, frozen foods, sauces or bottled drinks from HBFBs was found.
- Alcohol and liquor licensing, and the safety of low-acid preserved products, were not researched.

## 2. HDB and URA home-based business scheme conditions (HDB flats, private condos, landed homes)

### Takeaway
In HDB flats (HDB scheme) and private homes (URA scheme), residents may run a small home business without approval, under these conditions:
- Only owners, registered occupants or tenants (with the owner's consent) may operate it.
- No non-resident employees or third-party operators.
- No signs, posters or ads at the premises.
- No non-domestic heavy equipment, bulk storage, frequent loading or unloading, vans or trucks, or use as a distribution centre.
- No nuisance to neighbours.

Breaches lead to orders to stop or relocate. Online promotion is allowed. Condo residents must also check their MCST by-laws.

### Cited Findings
- **URA Home-Based Business Scheme page (last updated 23 Jun 2026):**
  - Residents "may carry out small scale home-based business activities without requiring URA's approval".
  - The business must be operated by owners, registered occupants or tenants (tenants need the owner's consent). Third parties may not run a business there, and "no non-resident employee" may work there.
  - No advertisements, signage or posters at the premises.
  - "No large-scale storage or frequent loading and unloading of goods" and no use as a distribution centre.
  - "No use of heavy equipment or appliances that are not intended for domestic use".
  - Residents must be considerate of neighbours, and the premises must remain a residential dwelling. Otherwise operations must cease or relocate, e.g. to commercial units.
  - Activities must also meet SFA (food safety) and SCDF (fire safety) rules.
  - Permitted example: "Food business such as baking on a small scale".
  - Source: [URA Home-based businesses](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/property-and-business-owners/business-owners/home-business/home-based-businesses/)
- **Government summary of HDB/URA conditions (4 Oct 2021):** "the business should not materially change the residential nature of the premises. Residents must also not employ any persons outside the household, use non-domestic heavy equipment, move goods by vans and trucks, and inconvenience their neighbours, amongst others." HBFBs that want to expand beyond what their homes support "should set up licensed food premises outside their homes" — [MSE reply, 4 Oct 2021](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-hbfb/)
- **HDB's own page:** listed by SFA as [hdb.gov.sg/.../homebased-business-scheme](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/home-ownership/home-business/homebased-business-scheme). The fetch returned only the page shell, so HDB's own wording was not verified here. SFA's HBFB pages (27 Mar 2026) say the scheme lets HBFBs operate "without requiring approval from the HDB or Urban Redevelopment Authority (URA), provided they meet all the scheme's requirements" — [SFA HBFB landing page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses)
- **Two different schemes:** the Home Office Scheme (administrative work, with up to two non-resident employees, and registration with HDB) is separate from the Home-based Small Scale Business Scheme, which allows no outside staff. Secondary — [PropertyGuru HDB home business guide](https://www.propertyguru.com.sg/property-guides/hdb-home-office-and-home-based-business-guide-48790); [sbo.sg guide](https://sbo.sg/business/basics/start-business-from-hdb-flat-singapore/). GoBusiness reportedly lists "catering / restaurants" as not permitted under the Home Office Scheme (snippet-only; GoBusiness returned 403) — [GoBusiness scheme selector](https://www.gobusiness.gov.sg/home-based-businesses/determine-your-home-business-scheme)
- **Online advertising:** SFA's FAQ (04 Aug 2026) says HBFBs may promote "online or with product labels" but may not display advertisements, signage or posters at their residence under HDB and URA guidelines — [SFA requirements page FAQ](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- **Fire safety:** SCDF imposes no fire-safety requirements on HBFBs beyond those for a residential setting, given their small scale (14 Oct 2025) — [MSE reply, 14 Oct 2025](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-home-based-food-businesses/)
- **Home-based private dining:** permitted under the HDB (flats) and URA (private homes) schemes. SFA "will continue to monitor the industry and periodically review the regulatory requirements" (3 Oct 2022) — [MSE reply, 3 Oct 2022](https://mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-home-based-private-dining-businesses-in-singapore)
- **Condominiums (MCST):** GoBusiness tells condo residents to check with the MCST or managing agent whether the activity complies with by-laws. MCSTs may adopt by-laws or house rules under the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act to address amenity issues. Snippet-only — [GoBusiness scheme provisions](https://gobusiness.gov.sg/home-based-businesses/understand-scheme-provisions-and-guidelines). On 26 Jun 2026, BCA clarified that by-laws cannot restrict an owner's right to lease or deal with the unit. This concerns renting, not home businesses (secondary news) — [The Star, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/06/26/condo-by-laws-cannot-limit-owners-right-to-rent-out-units-singapore039s-building-and-construction-authority)
- **Neighbour disputes (secondary news):** a Yishun HDB resident complained of cheese smell and noise from a neighbour's home pizza dinners, and the host adjusted — [MustShareNews](https://mustsharenews.com/yishun-hdb-pizza-complaint/)
- **Superseded (2020 COVID circuit breaker):** home-based food businesses could operate only for delivery or collection, with $1,000 fines warned. These restrictions are no longer in force (secondary) — [99.co](https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/covid-19-home-based-businesses/)
- **Recent changes, 2023–2026:**
  - No change to the HDB or URA conditions was found. The URA page update of 23 Jun 2026 carries the same conditions.
  - Parliamentary activity: 24 Sep 2025 (course not mandatory); 25 Sep 2025 (food poisoning statistics); 14 Oct 2025 (tracking and fire safety); 9 Sep 2026 (temporary fairs "will consider") — [MSE reply, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-home-based-food-businesses--participation-in-temporary-fairs/)

### Inferences
- **Customer pickup:** the scheme has no numeric customer cap in the sources found. "Inconvenience their neighbours", "materially change the residential nature" and "no frequent loading/unloading" point to staggered, appointment-based pickup or courier delivery, with no queues or walk-in retail. The app should support pickup time slots, a per-slot order cap, and a "delivery-only" seller option.
- **Staffing:** "No non-resident employees" means a seller cannot list paid helpers or a team who do not live in the home. App verification should capture that the operator is the owner, occupant or consenting tenant of the address.
- **Advertising:** in-app listings, photos and social media are lawful advertising. The app must never ask sellers to put QR stickers, banners or "Collect here" signs at their door or on common property.
- **Delivery logistics:** "Distribution centre", "vans and trucks" and "bulk storage" limits imply the app should not let a home seller act as a fulfilment hub for other sellers, or accept bulk orders that need commercial transport.

### Gaps
- HDB's own current conditions page text (fetch blocked) and any HDB numeric limits on visitors per day were not found. A 2021 MND answer on "more than five distinct guests a day" was referenced in search results but not retrieved.
- No MND, HDB or URA parliamentary reply on home-based business conditions from 2023–2026 was retrieved. An MND written answer titled "defining criteria and management regulations for home-based businesses" exists, but its text and date were not accessible — [MND](https://www.mnd.gov.sg/newsroom/speeches/view/written-answer-by-ministry-of-national-development-on-defining-criteria-and-management-regulations-for-home-based-businesses)
- Landed property is covered generically by URA's "private residential premises". No landed-specific rule was found.
- No sample condo by-law text on home businesses was found.

## 3. Is a food hygiene course (WSQ Food Safety Course Level 1) required for HBFB operators?

### Takeaway
Not mandatory as of Oct 2026. SFA "encouraged" it in 2025 and recommends it on its 2026 page. A 2021 proposal to make it mandatory was not adopted.

### Cited Findings
- 24 Sep 2025 reply: "SFA does not require home-based food business owners to complete the Workforce Skills Qualification Food Safety Course" (Level 1). "Nonetheless, they are encouraged to do so." SFA "regularly reviews its food safety regime" — [MSE reply, 24 Sep 2025](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-home-based-food-business-owners-/)
- SFA's 2026 page lists enrolling in WSQ Food Safety Course Level 1 as one of three ways to get started with food safety. It is a recommendation, not a condition — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- **Superseded proposal:** on 4 Oct 2021, SFA said it was "considering the need to make it mandatory" for HBFB owners to take the WSQ Food Safety Course and "will consult the public". As of the 24 Sep 2025 reply, it is still not required — [MSE reply, 4 Oct 2021](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-hbfb/)
- **Superseded course name:** the 2020 hygiene guidelines call it the "Basic Food Hygiene Course (also known as 'WSQ Follow Food and Beverage Safety and Hygiene Policies and Procedures')" — [SFA hygiene guidelines PDF, 29 Apr 2020](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/tools-and-resources/food-retail-and-businesses/sfa-good-food-hygiene-tips-for-residents-preparing-food-under-hdb-ura-guidelines)
- **Course as a remedial condition:** after the 2021 Peachy Sugarmaker outbreak, the operator passed Food Safety Course Level 1 as part of resuming. Snippet-only from the SFA notice, which now returns 404 — [SFA notice (snippet)](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/news-publications/circulars-and-notices/food-hygiene-notices/2021/prohibition-of-the-distribution-and-sale-of-food-prepared-by-the-peachy-sugarmaker-pte-ltd-lifted-after-meeting-required-standards)
- **Licensed businesses differ:** licensed retail food establishments must provide "mandatory training for all food handlers" — [SFA forum reply, 6 Dec 2024](https://www.beta.sfa.gov.sg/news-publications/newsroom/2024/risk-based-approach-in-regulating-industry)

### Inferences
- The app may ask for a WSQ FSC Level 1 certificate as an optional trust badge or a platform policy requirement, but must not describe it as legally required. If the platform makes it mandatory, it should say that this is a QuinTaste policy.

### Gaps
- The FSSA introduces food-worker registration for licensable businesses (s 103), but no source says this will extend to HBFBs.

## 4. Labelling of homemade food sold online (ingredients, allergens, best-before)

### Takeaway
The Food Regulations' general labelling rules apply to prepacked food. Non-prepacked food (food loosely packed or packed on the seller's premises, takeaway made to order) is exempt. SFA nonetheless recommends that online sellers display the same information: name, ingredients, allergens, storage, seller name and contact. False or misleading claims are prohibited on labels and in advertisements regardless of exemption. Whether an HBFB's sealed, packed-in-advance products count as "prepacked" is not settled in the sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Prepacked food:** general labelling under the Food Regulations applies to "all prepacked food products for direct sale to consumers, including food sold via e-commerce platforms". Required items are common name, complete ingredient list including allergens, net contents, local operator's name and address, and country of origin. For online listings, a legible photo of the label suffices — [SFA e-commerce guidelines, 20 Sep 2021](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- **Non-prepacked food** (e.g. cooked meals) is "exempted from the general labelling requirements". SFA recommends that sellers show:
  - name/description/photo
  - net contents
  - origin
  - operator name and address
  - storage instructions
  - allergens
  - SFA licence or registration number where applicable
  - Source: [SFA e-commerce guidelines](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- **Exemptions in the SFA labelling guide (amended through Nov 2025):**
  - Exempt from Regulation 5: food weighed, counted or measured in front of the purchaser, and food "loosely packed in the retailer's premises".
  - Examples include "food that is prepared and packaged on the premises from which it is sold" and takeaway food prepared on order (Regulation 6).
  - From 30 Jan 2026, ingredient statements on prepacked food need a heading such as "Ingredients".
  - Allergen groups to declare include cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs and others (Table 2).
  - Source: [SFA, A Guide to Food Labelling and Advertisements](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-manufacturing-and-storage/a-guide-to-food-labelling-and-advertisements.pdf)
- **Misleading claims:** under Regulation 9 of the Food Regulations, false or misleading statements, words, pictures or marks about the nature, composition or origin of food are not allowed on labels and advertisements — [SFA labelling guide](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-manufacturing-and-storage/a-guide-to-food-labelling-and-advertisements.pdf)
- **SS 687:2022 Guidelines for Food E-commerce** cover point-of-sale information (ingredients, allergens, source, expiry), traceability and recall, and stakeholder roles. They are aimed mainly at small and micro businesses and are voluntary ("Although the standard is not mandatory"). Online sellers should give customers product information and food safety tips — [SFA Requirements for Online Food Sellers, 19 Feb 2025](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/online-food-businesses/requirements-for-online-food-sellers)
- **Traceability (recommended):**
  - Sellers are recommended to keep traceability records: batch or lot and expiry; supplier one step back; recipients one step forward.
  - On a recall, sellers should remove online listings, display notices and contact buyers.
  - Sellers are advised to provide contact details on listings.
  - Source: [SFA e-commerce guidelines](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Required listing fields:** the app should require these for every HBFB listing, regardless of the legal exemption:
  - ingredients
  - declared allergens using SFA's Table 2 groups
  - storage instructions
  - consume-by or best-before guidance
  - net quantity
- This matches SFA's recommendation and SS 687, and protects allergic users. Mark them "Seller-declared".
- **Seller address:** SFA recommends "name and address of local food business operator". For HBFBs, the address is a private home. The app should hold the verified address privately, publish only the area or postal sector and the seller's business name, and give the full pickup address only to confirmed buyers. This privacy approach is an inference, not SFA guidance.
- **Prepacked goods:** if an HBFB sells shelf goods packed in advance (e.g. bottled sambal, jarred cookies) that the buyer cannot see packed, the cautious path is to treat them as prepacked and require a full label photo. Whether the "prepared and packaged on the premises from which it is sold" exemption covers online and delivery sales from a home is UNVERIFIED.

### Gaps
- No SFA statement specifically addresses labelling by HBFBs, or whether delivery or online sales fall outside the on-premises packing exemption.
- No SFA definition of a "vegetarian" or "vegan" claim was found in the labelling guide; see Q8.

## 5. Business registration, tax and payments

### Takeaway
A sole HBFB trading only under the owner's full NRIC name need not register with ACRA (Business Names Registration Act 2014). Any brand or descriptive name, e.g. "Kueh by Tan Mei Ling", requires registration. All business income is taxable and must be reported as self-employed income. GST registration is required above $1 million taxable turnover. No source addressed PayNow or platform payments for HBFBs.

### Cited Findings
- **ACRA exemption:** individuals who do business under only their full names are exempt from registration because they are identifiable. Under the Business Names Registration Bill second reading (8 Oct 2014), all persons carrying on business must register, but "individuals who carry on business in Singapore under only their full names will be exempt" — [ACRA BNRA second reading PDF](https://acra.gov.sg/docs/default-source/default-document-library/legislation/business-names-registration-act/bnra_2ndreading.pdf)
- **ACRA examples:** "Tan Ah Kow Trading" or "Tan Ah Kow Plumbers" must register. "Name" means the full name on the identity card. Voluntary registration is possible — [ACRA Key Legislative Reforms BNRA (updated 28 Dec 2015)](https://acra.gov.sg/docs/default-source/default-document-library/about-bizfile/help-resources/1-Key-Legislative-Reforms_BNRA_Jan15-(Updated-as-of-28-Dec-2015).pdf). ACRA's offences page lists unregistered business as an offence, except for a sole proprietor using only their full legal name (snippet-only) — [ACRA common offences](https://www.acra.gov.sg/manage/sole-proprietorship-partnerships/key-requirements-common-offences/common-offences/)
- **Secondary (law firms):** part-time or casual activity for gain still counts as "carrying on business". A "Home-Baked Cakes by Lim Ah Kao"-style name requires registration. Work-pass holders cannot generally be sole proprietors, and Dependant's Pass holders need a Letter of Consent — [CNP Law guide](https://www.cnplaw.com/cnplaw-business-guide-series-the-solo-business-owner/); [e27 / law-firm article, 16 Apr 2023](https://e27.co/going-solo-legal-considerations-for-starting-a-small-business-in-singapore-20230416/)
- **IRAS records:** IRAS's self-employed page (last updated 13 Jun 2025) covers sole proprietors and platform workers and says: "Retain accounting records and supporting documents for five years" — [IRAS tax obligations of self-employed persons](https://iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/tax-obligations-of-self-employed-persons)
- **GST (snippet-only from IRAS search results):**
  - All businesses, including individuals with trade income, must register for GST if annual taxable turnover exceeds $1 million, or is reasonably expected to in the next 12 months (register within 30 days of the forecast).
  - Failing to register is an offence, with a penalty of 10% of GST due and a fine of up to $10,000.
  - IRAS has prosecuted online sellers for incorrect returns and for failing to register for GST.
  - Source: [IRAS, Online seller convicted (snippet)](https://www.iras.gov.sg/news-events/newsroom/online-seller-convicted-for-submitting-incorrect-income-tax-returns-and-for-failure-to-register-for-gst)
- **Income tax:** income from gig or part-time work is taxable (snippet-only) — [GoBusiness taxes and GST](https://gobusiness.gov.sg/run-and-grow/taxes-and-gst)

### Inferences
- **Brand names:** if an HBFB shows a brand name in the app (most will), ask for its UEN or ACRA registration. The exception is a seller who lists under their exact full NRIC name with no descriptive words. That rule is checkable at onboarding.
- **Tax:** the app should remind sellers that sales income is taxable. A downloadable sales statement would help them report income and keep records for five years. The app should not give tax advice.
- **GST:** a typical HBFB is far below $1M, so GST display will usually not apply.

### Gaps
- Exact IRAS thresholds were not verified on IRAS pages: the reported net trade income above $6,000 filing trigger and the 2-line/4-line statement revenue thresholds came from secondary snippets only.
- PayNow (NRIC or mobile versus UEN), card acceptance via a platform, and whether a marketplace collecting buyer funds needs a licence under the Payment Services Act 2019 were not researched. Flag these for the payments researcher.
- Whether an unregistered full-name sole proprietor can hold a business PayNow UEN: UNVERIFIED.

## 6. Delivery and pickup: courier rules, temperature and time, pickup at home

### Takeaway
HBFBs may deliver, or use couriers and delivery platforms. Neither the HBFB nor the delivery business needs an SFA licence, but transport must be hygienic and keep hot or cold chains. SFA's numeric guidance is: hot above 60°C, cold below 5°C, and the 5–60°C danger zone. The delivery standards (SS 672, SS 687) are voluntary. Pickup at home is constrained by the scheme's no-nuisance and no-signage rules. No numeric pickup limit was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Food delivery businesses:**
  - They are "not required to obtain a licence from the SFA". Hot or cold chains "should be maintained, for the duration of the delivery".
  - Pack ready-to-eat meals "in a clean and sanitised area, separated from raw food".
  - Operators "must ensure that the way in which food is delivered to customers does not compromise food safety".
  - Delivery workers do not need WSQ FSC Level 1.
  - SS 672:2021 (Guidelines on Delivery of Ready-to-Eat Food), covering self-delivery and freelance couriers, is voluntary.
  - Page dated 24 Oct 2024.
  - Source: [SFA Requirements for Food Delivery Businesses](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/food-delivery-businesses/requirements-for-food-delivery-businesses)
- **Transport under SOFA and EPHA:** food must be transported hygienically. Vehicles must be clean, and receptacles must prevent contamination (20 Sep 2021) — [SFA e-commerce guidelines](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- **Temperature and timing:** keep hot foods above 60°C and cold foods below 5°C, and "Do not cook food too early". Bacteria multiply in the 5°C–60°C danger zone — [SFA HBFB hygiene guidelines, 29 Apr 2020](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/tools-and-resources/food-retail-and-businesses/sfa-good-food-hygiene-tips-for-residents-preparing-food-under-hdb-ura-guidelines)
- **Home pickup limits (scheme):**
  - No frequent loading or unloading.
  - No distribution centre.
  - No vans or trucks.
  - Do not inconvenience neighbours.
  - No signage at the premises.
  - Sources: [URA](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/property-and-business-owners/business-owners/home-business/home-based-businesses/); [MSE reply, 4 Oct 2021](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-hbfb/)
- **Secondary:** a home-cafe guide says no queues or dine-in customers (except home private dining) and no advertising the flat as a public cafe. This has no official citation, so treat it as UNVERIFIED — [99.co HDB home cafe guide](https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/hdb-home-cafe-complete-guide/)

### Inferences
- **Time-bound pickup:** the app can support courier booking or self-delivery and timed pickup. It should show a "consume within X hours / keep refrigerated" field set by the seller. SFA sets no HBFB-specific hold time, so the field should not cite one.
- **Pickup address privacy:** share the exact home address only with the confirmed buyer, close to pickup time. This reduces privacy exposure and walk-ins, and aligns with the no-nuisance intent.
- **Pickup volume:** cap concurrent pickup slots to avoid queues in HDB corridors and lift lobbies.
- **Packaging:** for perishable or chilled items such as dairy cakes, require the seller to declare chilled packaging (e.g. ice packs) when choosing courier delivery.

### Gaps
- SFA guidance for HBFBs gives no maximum time between cooking and consumption. SS 672's specific holding times are paywalled and were not read.
- No official numeric limit on daily pickups or visitors to an HDB flat was found.

## 7. Delivery-app listing, platform facilitation, and enforcement cases

### Takeaway
SFA explicitly does not restrict HBFBs from selling on delivery platforms such as GrabFood or foodpanda, but each platform sets its own approval rules. No official Grab or foodpanda Singapore policy on accepting HBFBs was found. Under the FSSA, an intermediary that only provides an online service for reward is not itself a "food business" (s 5(3), commenced 28 Nov 2025). Recorded HBFB incidents are few: about one suspected food poisoning case a year, none causally linked (2020–2025), plus the 2021 Peachy Sugarmaker outbreak, which led to a stop-sale order.

### Cited Findings
- **SFA FAQ on delivery platforms (04 Aug 2026):** "Are HBFBs allowed to sell their food on delivery platforms (e.g. GrabFood, Food Panda)?" Generally yes: SFA does not restrict this, HBFBs may promote online, and they should check platform requirements directly — [SFA requirements page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- **Online sales without a licence (20 Sep 2021):** HBFBs and resellers buying from licensed wholesalers "are not required to be licenced by SFA" to sell via e-commerce, but are subject to SOFA and EPHA. Consumers should report errant sellers via SFA's feedback form — [SFA e-commerce guidelines](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-retail/guidelines-for-selling-food-online-via-e-commerce-platforms.pdf)
- **FSSA s 5(3) (statute text):** a "food business" does not include a business that "acts as an intermediary between persons who trade in food by providing, for reward, premises or a place ... or services (such as an internet service provider or an online auction location)". It also excludes event or market organisers. Sections 5(1)–(3) commenced on 28 Nov 2025 (secondary) — [FSSA 2025 text (informal consolidation, version in force from 1/7/2026, PDF copy)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikisource/en/9/9e/Food_Safety_and_Security_Act_2025.pdf); [Mondaq/Lexplosion, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/food-and-drugs-law/1713356/food-safety-and-security-act-2025-compliance-update-key-takeaways-from-the-commencement-notification-building-on-our-part-13-series)

SFA responses were published 11 Nov 2024, but details were not given in the speech — [MSE speech, 8 Jan 2025](https://mse.gov.sg/latest-news/2nd-reading-food-safety-security-bill-closing-speech-sms-koh-poh-koon)
- **Grab requirements (secondary, UNVERIFIED for HBFBs):** a third-party vendor guide (Jul 2026, written for cloud kitchens) lists ACRA registration and an SFA licence among GrabFood merchant prerequisites. This conflicts with HBFBs' unlicensed status, so it may not reflect Grab's treatment of home sellers — [Klikit guide](https://klikit.io/en/learn/grabfood-setup-checklist-cloud-kitchen-singapore)
- **Incident statistics:**
  - 25 Sep 2025 reply: "Over the past five years, SFA investigated an annual average of one suspected case of food poisoning incident linked to consuming food sold by home-based food businesses". The investigations "did not find any causative link" — [MSE reply, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-food-poisoning-related-to-home-based-food-businesses/)
  - 4 Oct 2021 reply: fewer than five feedback or incident cases a year on average. One 2019 case was attributed to an HBFB, and SFA prohibited commercial food preparation and distribution from that unit — [MSE reply, 4 Oct 2021](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-hbfb/)
- **The Peachy Sugarmaker Pte Ltd (Aug 2021):**
  - The joint MOH/SFA release reported 15 gastroenteritis cases (9 hospitalised) after food prepared on 5 and 7 Aug 2021 by "a home based business". Distribution and sale were prohibited from 13 Aug 2021 — [MOH release](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/prohibition-of-the-distribution-and-sale-of-food-prepared-by-the-peachy-sugarmaker-pte-ltd/)
  - The ban was lifted on 28 Sep 2021 after the business disposed of ready-to-eat, thawed and perishable food, cleaned and sanitised, replaced equipment and utensils, and the operator passed FSC Level 1. The later notice cites 47 persons affected (12 hospitalised) between 5 and 9 Aug 2021. No causative agent was reported. Snippet-only; the SFA URL returns 404 — [SFA notice (snippet)](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/news-publications/circulars-and-notices/food-hygiene-notices/2021/prohibition-of-the-distribution-and-sale-of-food-prepared-by-the-peachy-sugarmaker-pte-ltd-lifted-after-meeting-required-standards); [Food Safety News (secondary)](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2021/08/home-business-linked-to-outbreak-in-singapore/)

There is no timeline, so the prohibition stands — [MSE reply, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-home-based-food-businesses--participation-in-temporary-fairs/)

### Inferences
- **Platform status:** listing and taking orders for HBFBs is lawful. If QuinTaste only provides the online service and payment for reward, the s 5(3)(a) exclusion suggests it is not itself a "food business" under the FSSA definitions.
- **Facilitation risk:** platform delivery that physically handles food could still bring delivery-related duties. And encouraging prohibited activities (catering, B2B or fair supply) would expose sellers and reputationally the platform.
- **Recall plumbing:** SFA can order an HBFB to stop selling or recall food. The app should build:
  - per-seller suspension
  - delisting
  - a buyer-notification path, mirroring SFA's recommended online recall steps
  - order records that support "one step forward" traceability

## 8. Halal and "Muslim-owned" claims; honesty of vegetarian and vegan claims

### Takeaway
Halal certification is voluntary. Using the MUIS halal mark, or issuing a halal certificate without MUIS approval, is an offence (AMLA s 88A(5): fine up to $10,000 and/or 12 months; snippet-level). MUIS states that halal claims by non-Muslim-owned, uncertified businesses are an offence, while it does not penalise Muslim-owned businesses' own claims. Vegetarian and vegan claims have no specific SFA definition found, but false or misleading descriptions of composition are prohibited (Food Regulations reg 9). The FSSA adds a misleading-conduct offence (Part 10), whose commencement is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **Muslim-owned labels and the MUIS logo (5 Oct 2020 reply):**
  - Halal certification is voluntary.
  - Some Muslim owners "prefer to highlight their faith, either through labels that indicate that the establishment is Muslim-owned". Such businesses should "take care to ensure that their food is halal".
  - "The Muis halal logo also must not be used or modified by businesses that have not attained official certification".
  - "Action will be taken against businesses that make wilful misrepresentations."
  - Source: [MCCY reply, 5 Oct 2020](https://www.mccy.gov.sg/about-us/news-and-resources/educating-f-b-operators-on-the-use-of-halal-labelling-and-halal-certification/)
- **MUIS FAQ (ask.gov.sg; snippet-only, page returned 403):** "it is an offence for non-Muslim owned companies which are not halal-certified to be making any halal claims". MUIS "does not penalise Muslim-owned companies should they make halal claims about their operations" — [ask.gov.sg MUIS FAQ](https://ask.gov.sg/muis/questions/clks0tqia00pkl90f9jrg42ea). The statutory basis for the non-Muslim-owned claim offence was not located (secondary search summary).
- **AMLA s 88A(5):** penalises issuing a halal certificate or using a specified halal certification mark (or a colourable imitation) without MUIS approval: fine up to $10,000, imprisonment up to 12 months, or both (snippet-level). In *Angliss Singapore Pte Ltd v PP*, a fine for affixing the halal mark without approval was reduced on appeal from $9,000 to $2,000 — [Angliss v PP (vLex)](https://sg.vlex.com/vid/angliss-singapore-pte-ltd-842794759); [AMLA on SSO](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/AMLA1966-R8)
- **Signage is not proof:** in 2020, MUIS said Arabic names or Quranic verses do not confirm Muslim ownership (Al-Afsana, Hougang; secondary news) — [Mothership, Sep 2020](https://mothership.sg/2020/09/indian-muslim-food-establishment-with-quranic-verse-on-signboard-not-halal-certified//amp)
- **Certification requirements (secondary consultancy summary):** MUIS certification requires 2–3 Muslim staff per premises and a halal quality management system. Whether a home kitchen can be certified is UNVERIFIED — [3E Accounting guide](https://www.3ecpa.com.sg/resources/industry-guide/starting-a-halal-certified-business-in-singapore/)
- **Vegetarian and vegan claims:**
  - Under reg 9 of the Food Regulations, false or misleading statements or marks about the nature, composition or origin of food are not allowed on labels and advertisements.
  - Names of alternatives to conventional food "should be accompanied by appropriate qualifying terms" such as "mock" or "plant-based".
  - The guide also gives "vegetarian mock meat" as an example in its allergen section.
  - Source: [SFA labelling guide (amended Nov 2025)](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/food-manufacturing-and-storage/a-guide-to-food-labelling-and-advertisements.pdf)
- **FSSA misleading-conduct offence (Part 10):** a person commits an offence if, in carrying on a food business, they engage in conduct "that is misleading or deceptive or is likely to mislead or deceive" about labelling or supply of food. Penalties for individuals are a fine up to $15,000 and/or 6 months, higher for repeat offenders. Section numbering differs between the arrangement (s 177) and body text (s 179) in the copy read — [FSSA text (PDF copy)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikisource/en/9/9e/Food_Safety_and_Security_Act_2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Halal:** the app should not offer a free-text or toggle "halal" claim. Allow "MUIS halal-certified" only with certificate verification (number and expiry). Treat "Muslim-owned" as a self-declaration with an explicit note that it is not halal certification. Never render the MUIS logo unless verified. For a vegetarian and vegan app, the simplest compliant default is no halal field for HBFBs unless certified.
- **Vegan and vegetarian:** treat these as seller-declared composition claims. A false "vegan" listing, e.g. a cake containing egg or ghee, is a misleading composition claim under reg 9. Require ingredient lists consistent with the claimed diet, and give buyers a reporting path. QuinTaste's existing "Seller-declared" labelling convention fits this. The app should not label HBFB food "certified vegan", because no Singapore statutory vegan certification was found.

### Gaps
- No SFA or statutory definition of "vegetarian" or "vegan" was found.
- No case of enforcement against false vegetarian claims was found.
- The Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act's application to misleading listings was not researched.
- The statutory source for MUIS's "non-Muslim halal claim is an offence" statement was not located.
- Whether HBFBs can obtain MUIS certification is UNVERIFIED.

## 9. Food Safety and Security Act 2025: will HBFBs need licensing or registration when Part 4 commences?

### Takeaway
Statute text: the FSSA explicitly treats home businesses as "food businesses" (s 5(2)). It makes licensable only the classes in the First Schedule, and several retail classes expressly exclude premises that are "a home". Catering, temporary-fair stalls and non-retail manufacture for sale to other food businesses carry no home exclusion. Section 104 lets regulations prescribe prohibited conduct for non-licensable businesses such as HBFBs (fine up to $5,000). Ministerial commentary (8 Jan 2025) says HBFBs are not required to be licensed and that the Bill does not change SFA's powers to stop them. As of the latest sources found (Dec 2025), Part 4 licensing had not commenced; no 2026 commencement was confirmed.

### Cited Findings
Statute text is quoted from a PDF copy of the Act headed "Informal Consolidation – version in force from 1/7/2026", Act No. 7 of 2025. SSO itself returned 403 — [FSSA PDF copy](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikisource/en/9/9e/Food_Safety_and_Security_Act_2025.pdf); [SSO FSSA2025](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/FSSA2025).

- **s 5(1)–(2):** "food business" covers handling food for supply or supply of food, "regardless of whether ... commercial, charitable or community", and "includes a food business carried on as a home business or that is an occupation carried on by an individual within the individual's private residence".
- **s 5(4)(b):** for a non-licensable food business, the "proprietor" is "the occupier of those premises".
- **s 6 and First Schedule:** a "licensable food business" is a class listed in the First Schedule. The Minister may amend the Schedule by Gazette order after considering risk, scale, the type and number of consumers, and other safeguards (s 6(3)).
- **First Schedule Part 1 (Retail), excerpts:**
  - Item 1: dine-in restaurants, cafés and bars, for food prepared in premises "(but not a home)".
  - Item 4: preparing and packing, or preparing, packing and delivering, "takeaway meals or ready-to-eat food, on the order of a customer" in premises "(but not a home)" (e.g. bubble tea, sandwich kiosk).
  - Item 6: raw meat, fish or spoilage-prone retail on premises "(but not a home)".
  - Item 8: catering, defined as food "prepared and served to consumers ... away from the food premises" under an agreement fixing "predetermined type", "predetermined number of people", "predetermined time" and "predetermined cost". There is no home exclusion.
  - Item 11: "a food stall in a temporary fair where food is prepared for sale". There is no home exclusion.
- **First Schedule Part 2 (Non-retail), item 4:** manufacturing, kneading, marinating, mixing, forming or wrapping etc. "for the purpose of sale to any other food business (whether wholesale or retail and whether licensable or not) or for export". No home exclusion was seen.
- **s 102:** offences relating to an "Unlicensed licensable food business". Penalty details for each subsection were not fully extracted.
- **s 103:** offences for deploying unregistered food workers in licensable businesses.
- **s 104 "Disallowed activities for non-licensable food business":** a proprietor of a non-licensable food business commits an offence if they engage in conduct prescribed in regulations under Part 15 as "prohibited conduct", or in prescribed "disallowed circumstances", knowing or reasonably knowing this. Penalty: fine not exceeding $5,000.
- **s 128:** the Director-General may direct recall of food that is "unsafe or unsuitable" or "mislabelled or incorrectly identified". On failure to comply, the DG may take steps and recover costs. Commencement of s 128 was not confirmed.
- **Ministerial commentary (8 Jan 2025):**
  - HBFBs "are not required to be licensed by SFA". SFA does not track their number.
  - HBFBs "must not sell unsafe food or food prepared under unsanitary conditions". SFA can direct a stop, and "the Bill does not change this".
  - Clause 104 "prohibits an unlicensed food business from undertaking certain activities". SFA will prescribe these in regulations, citing higher-risk examples such as "the supply of uncooked sashimi".
  - Source: [MSE, SMS Koh Poh Koon closing speech](https://mse.gov.sg/latest-news/2nd-reading-food-safety-security-bill-closing-speech-sms-koh-poh-koon)
- **Commencement (secondary):**
  - From 28 Nov 2025, the definitions (ss 3–10 including s 5), defined food and pre-market approval (Part 5), non-packaged drinking water (Part 6) and Parts 12, 13, 14 and 16 are in force.
  - Part 4 licensing "will gain force in a future phase", with no date given.
  - Source: [Mondaq/Lexplosion, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.mondaq.com/food-and-drugs-law/1713356/food-safety-and-security-act-2025-compliance-update-key-takeaways-from-the-commencement-notification-building-on-our-part-13-series)
  - Phased implementation "by 2028" (snippet-level, secondary) — [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/bill-introduced-in-parliament-to-enhance-singapores-food-safety-and-security-regime/)
  - The licensing regime commences "on separate schedules" (commercial guide, secondary) — [Covarage guide](https://covarage.com/guides/regulatory-change/fssa-tranche-1-28-november-2025-fb-product-liability)

### Inferences
- **Inference, not commentary:** when Part 4 commences, the First Schedule as drafted would leave a home kitchen selling takeaway or ready-to-eat food direct to consumers non-licensable, because items 1, 4 and 6 say "(but not a home)".
- **Home activities that would need a licence:** a home kitchen doing any of the following would fall into a licensable class, and running it unlicensed would be an offence (s 102):
  - catering (item 8)
  - temporary-fair stalls (item 11)
  - manufacturing for sale to other food businesses (Part 2 item 4)
- **Likely continuity:** this mirrors today's SFA prohibitions, so the FSSA is likely to formalise the current HBFB boundaries rather than create HBFB licensing. Section 104 regulations (e.g. raw sashimi) would put today's guidance-level bans into law with a fine of up to $5,000.
- **Catering definition and meal-prep:** the item 8 definition (predetermined type, number of people, time and cost; served away from the food premises) is the closest statutory guide to "packed meals". A recurring meal-prep subscription for a fixed number of people at a fixed time and price arguably resembles it. Treat as a risk until SFA clarifies.
- **No HBFB registration in the Act:** no HBFB registration requirement was seen in the text read. The future regime appears to be licensing for First Schedule classes, plus regulation-based conduct bans for non-licensable businesses.

### Gaps
- No commencement notification for Part 4 or s 104 dated 2026 was found. SSO's version table could not be accessed.
- No s 104 regulations ("prohibited conduct" lists) were found published or consulted on.
- SFA's FSSA FAQs for HBFBs were not found.
- The penalty structure of each s 102 subsection was not extracted.
- The section numbering discrepancy for the Part 10 misleading-conduct offence (s 177 vs s 179) is unresolved.
