# Existing platforms and business models for licensed and home-based food sellers (Singapore first, international precedents)

Research date: 17 October 2026. Singapore findings are listed first; anything outside Singapore is labelled **[INTL]**. "UNVERIFIED" means a claim came only from a search-engine snippet, an automated profile, a sponsored article or a page that could not be fetched, or that no current status could be confirmed. Many Singapore home-food app sources date from 2016 to 2021, so current status is unknown unless stated.

## Q1. Singapore: which platforms list or sell home-based food, and how do they handle eligibility, verification, fees, ordering, payment, fulfilment, trust, dietary tags and status?

### Takeaway
No dedicated, well-documented, currently active home-based food (HBFB) marketplace in Singapore could be confirmed for 2024–2026. The dedicated apps that did exist (Hcook, Share Food, Heartland Chefs, FIEAT, Dine Inn; all around 2016) were free or unmonetised, often relied on off-platform price and pickup negotiation, and have no recent coverage. Home-based sellers actually trade through Instagram, WhatsApp, Google Forms, Carousell, Facebook groups and their own order pages. These channels typically use pre-orders with lead times of 4 days to 2 weeks, PayNow, and pickup near MRT stations or paid delivery. Singapore law is permissive: HBFBs need no SFA licence, and SFA does not restrict them from delivery platforms. Each platform decides whether it accepts them.

### Cited Findings

**Regulatory baseline that any Singapore platform must work within**
- HBFBs "currently do not need an SFA licence". The rules apply to HDB flats and private homes alike. The SFA page was last updated 04 Aug 2026 — [SFA HBFB requirements](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- HBFBs may not offer catering (buffet lines and packed meals count as catering) and may not sell to SFA-licensed retailers such as hawker stalls, restaurants, cafes, canteens or temporary fairs. They may not prepare food at home for sale at temporary fairs, even when they hold a stall licence there. Ready-to-eat raw seafood is prohibited — [SFA](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- Ingredients must be legally imported or come from licensed suppliers. Sellers may not use ingredients bought from other HBFBs or food brought back from overseas trips — [SFA](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- The WSQ Food Safety Course Level 1 is recommended, not required — [SFA](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- "SFA does not restrict food businesses, including home-based ones, from operating on food delivery platforms". For approval requirements, SFA tells sellers to contact the platform. HBFBs may promote themselves online but may not display signage at the home — [SFA FAQ on the same page](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- After a food safety incident, SFA may order an HBFB to stop operating or to recall products — [SFA](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- A third-party law guide says home bakers must register a business with ACRA if they carry out profit-making activity regularly (not checked against an official source) — [SingaporeLegalAdvice](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/law-articles/home-baked-goods-licence-singapore)
- History: during the 2020 Circuit Breaker, HBFBs were allowed to accept and prepare orders from 12 May 2020, while home-based private dining remained disallowed (search snippet; page not fetched) — [MSE press release](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/press-release-on-home-based-food-businesses/)

### Inferences
- The Singapore apps that tried a home-cook marketplace (2016) used direct-contact, off-platform pricing and pickup with no monetisation. None shows signs of surviving. A marketplace that does not capture the order and payment gives sellers little reason to stay over Instagram or WhatsApp, and gives the platform no revenue.
- Pre-order with lead times or weekly cut-offs, PayNow, and MRT-area self-collection is the established operating pattern for Singapore HBFBs. A platform should model these natively (cut-off times, slot drops, collection points) rather than impose instant on-demand ordering.
- SFA's rules make "no catering", "no sale to licensed retailers" and "no raw ready-to-eat seafood" platform-enforceable listing rules. Because SFA defers platform acceptance to the platform, the platform's own onboarding is the de facto verification layer for HBFBs.
- Hcook's model (application, home hygiene audit, unannounced revisits, removal after upheld complaints) and Share Food's opt-in hygiene badge are the only local precedents for HBFB-specific verification. Both depended on founders' manual effort.

### Gaps
- Whether GrabFood or foodpanda Singapore currently accept HBFBs without an SFA licence: no official statement found.
- Carousell's current food-listing policy, Lazada SG food rules and Meta's current Commerce Policy on homemade food were not located. **[INTL]** A 2026 local-news investigation describes a thriving, "largely unregulated" homemade food trade on Facebook Marketplace in California, which has no food category ([Almanac, 7 Apr 2026](https://www.almanacnews.com/enterprise-story/2026/04/07/inside-facebook-marketplaces-thriving-and-largely-unregulated-food-business/)).
- No evidence was found for WhyQ, Pinch, Burpple or Eatbook running an HBB marketplace or verified directory, Telegram ordering bots, or "kampung"-style apps. Eatbook and others publish editorial HBB roundups only.
- Halal or vegetarian tagging on any Singapore HBFB app: not documented.
- Current status of Hcook, Share Food, Heartland Chefs, FIEAT, Dine Inn, Homemade SG and SideStop.

## Q2. Singapore direct-ordering tools for licensed F&B and home businesses: pricing and pre-order/festive features

### Takeaway
Singapore sellers can run their own pre-order shop cheaply: Take App has a free tier with 0% commission, HitPay charges payment fees only, and Oddle charges about 10% per order with no monthly fee on bank-partner packages. Delivery marketplaces are cited at 25–30%. A new marketplace must therefore justify any commission with demand and trust it generates, not with order tooling.

### Inferences
- Commission benchmarks for a Singapore seller are roughly 0% (Take App free, HitPay payment fees only), about 10% (Oddle, all-in) and 25–30% (delivery marketplaces). An HBFB- or vegan-focused marketplace charging 10–15% sits between direct tools and delivery apps. It needs to deliver demand and trust signals, not only checkout.
- Because sellers already use Take App and Google Forms with payment-screenshot upload, a marketplace that keeps PayNow-direct or proof-of-payment flows matches existing behaviour. In-app payment (e.g. HitPay or Stripe) is what enables refunds and dispute handling and commission capture.

### Gaps
- Shopify Singapore, Qashier online-store pricing and Beepit Singapore commission were not verified.
- Oddle's current published rate card, and whether its 10% includes payment processing, conflicts across sources.
- Festive pre-order features (slot capacity, cut-off dates, deposit collection) were not confirmed from any vendor documentation.

## Q3. [INTL] International home-cook and licensed-food marketplace precedents: regulation, vetting, pricing, pre-order windows, logistics, quality control, and why they failed or succeeded

### Takeaway
**[INTL]** The home-cook marketplaces that grew are Shef (US) and, in a different licensed-commissary model, CookUnity. Both rely on pre-ordering, mandatory food-safety training or professional kitchens, and roughly 15% take rates (Shef), and both operate where the law explicitly permits them (US cottage food and MEHKO laws). Failures cluster around four causes: regulation outpacing the business (Josephine), funding dependence with high burn and premature expansion (Yumist), owning logistics or kitchens (Holachef), and inconsistent quality or late payments to cooks.

## Q4. Vegetarian/vegan-specific marketplaces and directories, and how they verify vegan claims

### Takeaway
No Singapore marketplace dedicated to verified vegan or vegetarian home or licensed sellers was found. The closest precedents are HappyCow, which manually reviews every listing but requires a permanent location for its Vegan category, vegan fairs and markets that act as offline cold-start channels, and certificate-gated dietary tags on delivery apps (halal). Most platforms otherwise rely on seller self-declaration.

### Cited Findings
- HappyCow's "Vegan" category requires a food business with a **permanent location** serving fully vegan food, with **no animal products used in the kitchen**. "Every listing submission is reviewed manually" by its editing team, who verify location, hours and menu. Approval takes 1–2 weeks. Listings are **free**. The FAQ does not address home kitchens — [HappyCow business FAQ](https://www.happycow.net/business/faq)
- HappyCow's directory includes catering services and food trucks as well as restaurants and shops — [Wikipedia: HappyCow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HappyCow); [Vegan Australia](https://veganaustralia.org.au/?p=75)
- Singapore Vegan Festival ran a "Vegan Market in the Sky" marketplace (2019 and 2021 editions) described as a hub for homegrown vegan brands — [Epicure Asia](https://www.epicureasia.com/?p=19037)
- Everyday Vegan, described as Singapore's first vegan online grocer, said "we mainly participated in fairs and markets" before opening a retail shop — [Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/singapores-first-vegan-online-grocer-opens-retail-shop-meet-plant-based-demand/)
- A Singapore home-based bakery (The Cream Connection) obtained "Sattvik Certified" vegetarian certification. This comes from a 2023 press release; eggless is not the same as vegan — [press release](https://stocks.observer-reporter.com/observerreporter/news/read/43525725/sattvik_certifications_singapore_has_exclusively_)
- Halal analogy for certificate-gated dietary claims in Singapore: MUIS is the sole issuer of halal certificates, certification is voluntary, certified eating establishments must prominently display the original certificate, and it is an offence for non-Muslim-owned, non-certified businesses to make halal claims — [MUIS](https://www.muis.gov.sg/Halal/About); [ask.gov.sg MUIS](https://ask.gov.sg/muis/questions/clks0tqia00pkl90f9jrg42ea)
- **[INTL]** GrabFood Philippines (Davao) requires NCMF endorsement before a merchant can be tagged halal — [PhilstarLife](https://philstarlife.com/news-and-views/659043-grab-ncmf-region-xi-partner-easier-more-credible-halal-food-discovery). - Singapore delivery apps have courted Muslim customers by separating non-halal orders — [Salaam Gateway](https://beta.salaamgateway.com/story/food-delivery-apps-court-singapores-muslims-by-separating-non-halal-orders)
- **[INTL]** Shef cooks self-publish ingredients, spice level and allergy warnings on their menus; no third-party dietary verification was found — [Rideshare Guy](https://therideshareguy.com/shef-review/)

### Inferences
- No Singapore vegan certification is in common use for HBFBs. A realistic verification stack is: seller declaration (vegetarian / vegan / five-pungent-free), required full ingredient lists per item, platform manual review of menus (HappyCow-style), clear distinction between "declared" and "certified" where a third-party certificate exists (Sattvik and similar), and complaint-triggered review or removal.
- The halal regime shows that Singapore users understand the difference between "certified" (document-gated, displayed) and merely claimed. Displaying a "certified" badge only when a document has been checked, and labelling everything else "seller-declared", follows that existing mental model.
- Vegan fairs and markets (Singapore Vegan Festival, vendor markets) are an observed recruitment and cold-start channel for vegan small brands.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on abillion's verification methods, "Vegan Grocer" or vegan pop-up market vetting rules. Veganburg is a restaurant chain, not a marketplace.
- Whether GrabFood or foodpanda Singapore require any evidence for "vegetarian" or "vegan" tags, and GrabFood Singapore's halal-tag process: not found.
- SFA rules on "vegan" or "vegetarian" label claims were not researched here.

### Inferences
- A feasible launch model for a small Singapore app:
  - **Free listing** for a hand-curated first cohort of verified sellers.
  - **Pre-order checkout** with seller-set cut-offs and collection slots.
  - **0% or low commission at launch** (e.g. 0–10%), because sellers can already use Take App free and HitPay at payment cost. This can later move to roughly 10–15% once the platform demonstrably brings orders, in line with Shef and DishDivvy.
  - **Optional seller subscription** for tools, Take App-style.
  - **No buyer fee** initially.
- Seeding supply through vegan fairs and pop-ups, community-group-style moderated feeds and an onboarding concierge (photos, pricing, credential checks) matches the observed cold-start patterns. Seller self-delivery or collection avoids the Holachef and Yumist logistics trap.
- Monetisation-free directories (the 2016 Singapore apps) appear not to have lasted. A plan for revenue (commission on captured orders, or seller subscription) is needed from the start, even if it is waived during launch.

### Gaps
- No Singapore-specific data on HBFB order volumes, average order values or willingness to pay platform commission was found.
- Buyer-fee and listing-fee practices on home-cook platforms were not documented in the sources found.
- Exact reasons the 2016 Singapore apps (Hcook, Share Food, Heartland Chefs, FIEAT) stopped are undocumented. Their disappearance is inferred from the absence of later coverage, not from post-mortems.
