# Asian and Singapore Recipe Apps: Features and UX Conventions (as of October 2026)

Research date: 17 October 2026. App Store listings were read live that day (Singapore, Indonesia, Japan and China storefronts). Version notes said "3 days ago" or similar, so they reflect mid-October 2026. Google Play pages could not be fetched (truncated); Play figures below come from search snippets and are marked as such. Older facts carry their year. Company-stated figures are marked "company-stated".

## 1. How are recipe detail pages and step views structured (video-first, photo per step, tips per step)?

### Takeaway
Japan has two models. Kurashiru and DELISH KITCHEN put an editor-produced short video first, with text ingredients, steps and tips underneath. DELISH KITCHEN also offers a "kitchen mode" that plays one short clip per step, with swipe navigation. Cookpad (Japan and global) and Xiachufang (China) are photo-and-text first: ingredient amounts, numbered steps with an optional photo each, and social proof ("N people cooked this") near the top. Neither model treats a single long video as the default. Unit conventions are often loose (spoons, cloves, "适量" meaning "an appropriate amount").

- In the Japanese app, users write recipes by filling in ingredients and steps in a fixed format. **Xiachufang 下厨房 (China), sample recipe page "蒜蓉蚝油生菜", published 2025-02-08**
- Header: overall score "7.7 综合评分", "188 做过" (people who made it), a favourite (收藏) button and an author link. There is no written intro, only a hero photo. — [Xiachufang recipe page](https://www.xiachufang.com/recipe/107491076/)
- The ingredient list (用料) uses household measures, not grams. Examples: "两勺" (two spoons) of light soy sauce, "5瓣" (five cloves) of garlic, and "适量" (an appropriate amount) for salt, sugar and sesame oil. — [Xiachufang recipe page](https://www.xiachufang.com/recipe/107491076/)
- The method (做法) has 8 numbered steps, 7 of them with a photo. Tips are written into the steps rather than a separate 小贴士 block in this example. The page has no video, a printable-version link, a "people who like this also like" list, and a favourites count of 104,890. — [Xiachufang recipe page](https://www.xiachufang.com/recipe/107491076/)
- Listing cards show ingredient tags and a recency-weighted social-proof line such as "七天内 4 人做过" (4 people made it in the past 7 days). — [Xiachufang Explore](https://www.xiachufang.com/explore/)

**HealthHub / HPB (Singapore)**
- The Nutrition Hub recipe page has a search box and three tabs: "All recipes", "Written" and "Video". Featured recipes are localised healthier versions of familiar dishes, such as Laksa Fried Rice and Braised Chicken Bee Hoon. — [HealthHub Healthy Recipes](https://www.healthhub.sg/programmes/nutrition-hub/healthy-recipes)
- Healthy Ageing Hub list cards show cooking time and servings. Examples: Mee Goreng 45 min, Nasi Kuning 30 min, Chickpea Delight 25 min, all for 2 servings. — [HealthHub Healthy Ageing recipe library](https://www.healthhub.sg/programmes/healthy-ageing/eating-healthy/library-of-recipes)

### Inferences
- The Japanese video-first apps still put the whole recipe in text under the video. Video speeds up comprehension but does not replace scannable text. DELISH KITCHEN's per-step clips plus swipeable kitchen mode is the clearest Asian benchmark for a "cook mode": one step on screen, a looping micro-clip or photo, and large forward/back gestures.
- The "main + side + soup" planner (Kurashiru, DELISH KITCHEN, Cookpad JP's "つくる" tab) reflects the Japanese ichiju-sansai meal structure. A Singapore equivalent might be a rice plus 2–3 dishes plus soup "home dinner" combo, since Chinese-Singaporean home meals are commonly shared dishes. No source in this research confirms that Singapore users want this planner.
- Social proof such as "N people made this in 7 days" and cook counts sits at the top of the detail page in the community apps. Western apps more often lead with star ratings; that is an unverified contrast, since no Western sources were researched here.
- Loose measures (适量, spoons, cloves) are normal in Chinese user recipes. A Singapore app should accept and display them, ideally with an optional metric equivalent.

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched for Kurashiru's in-app step view (whether steps are also split into clips like DELISH KITCHEN's).
- Japanese measure conventions (大さじ/小さじ, i.e. tablespoon/teaspoon) were not verified from a source here.
- No HealthHub recipe detail page was fetched, so its ingredient, nutrition and Healthier Choice structure is unconfirmed.
- No Western-app benchmark was researched in this note. Any "differs from Western apps" statement is inference.

## 2. What community features drive engagement (uploads, cooksnaps, comments, kitchen diaries)?

### Takeaway
"I made this" photo posts tied to a specific recipe are the core loop in community-led Asian apps: Cookpad's Cooksnaps (つくれぽ, tsukurepo, in Japan) and Xiachufang's 作品/做过. Cookpad Japan even uses the count as a quality signal ("hall of fame" at 1,000+ cook reports). Comments are narrowed to recipe-related feedback, and Xiachufang uses a Q&A (答疑) block instead of an open thread. Short-video social platforms (Xiaohongshu, Douyin, TikTok, Lemon8) are a major parallel channel for recipe discovery.

### Cited Findings
- Cookpad describes Cooksnaps as photos of a dish you cooked from a recipe, with notes on how it turned out. Recipes can be shared publicly or kept private and sorted into private themed folders. — [Cookpad App Store SG](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/cookpad-find-share-recipes/id585332633)
- Cookpad Japan Premium lets users filter searches by cook-report (つくれぽ) count and surfaces "殿堂入りレシピ" (hall-of-fame recipes) with 1,000+ cook reports. Popularity-sorted search is also a Premium feature. — [Cookpad App Store JP](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id340368403)
- In 2021, a Japanese article defined つくれぽ as sending feedback after cooking a public recipe and noted that higher counts signal popularity. — [BCN Retail (2021)](https://www.bcnretail.com/market/detail/20210505_221235.html)
- Cookpad Malaysia rules: a Cooksnap must show a dish you actually cooked, with no people, animals or unrelated images. Comments must be feedback on the recipe; insults and off-topic posts are not allowed. — [Cookpad Malaysia community guidelines](https://cookpad.com/my/community)
- Xiachufang recipe pages show "大家做出 193 作品" (193 people made it), a "view all" link and an upload-your-own button. Instead of comments there is a Q&A (答疑) block, which in the example had 47 questions and required login to view all. — [Xiachufang recipe page](https://www.xiachufang.com/recipe/107491076/)
- Xiachufang store listings describe a popularity community (人气社区) where users publish recipes and get likes and feedback. Other listed features are seasonal suggestions by solar term (时令流行 / 节气), ranking lists (榜单推荐) and brand-sponsored cooking contests with prizes (活动奖品). — [Xiachufang App Store CN](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/id460979760); [Tencent App Treasure listing](https://sj.qq.com/appdetail/com.xiachufang)
- Kurashiru lets users post short videos and recipe images, follow creators, like and comment, and add personal notes to official recipes. The listing describes user tips (コツ) as video or image posts. — [Kurashiru App Store JP](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%82%AF%E3%83%A9%E3%82%B7%E3%83%AB-%E6%AF%8E%E6%97%A5%E3%81%AE%E7%8C%AE%E7%AB%8B%E3%81%AB-%E3%83%AC%E3%82%B7%E3%83%94%E5%8B%95%E7%94%BB%E3%81%A7%E6%96%99%E7%90%86%E3%81%8C%E3%81%8A%E3%81%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%8F%E4%BD%9C%E3%82%8C%E3%82%8B/id1059134258)
- Douguo (豆果美食) lists online classes (在线课堂), a notes community (笔记社区) and a mall (商城). — [Douguo App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id434212566)
- **Xiaohongshu:** a Xiaohongshu trend report on youth search habits says beginner cooks start by searching for tutorials, and "#Cola Chicken Wings" was the most-searched dish at 354.7M views. — [Sharecast summary of Xiaohongshu trend report](https://www.sharecast.com/press_note/market_reports/xiaohongshu-unveils-trend-report-on-search-habits-among-chinas-youth--17253140)
- **Xiaohongshu:** marketing sources claim close to 70% of monthly active users use search and that the app handles more than 1 billion searches a day. These are unverified marketing-blog figures. — [EliteAsia](https://www.eliteasia.co/what-is-xiaohongshu-red-note/); [MarketingToChina](https://marketingtochina.com/xiaohongshu-statistics-and-trends/)
- **Xiaohongshu:** in January 2025, during the "TikTok refugee" episode, a Chinese steamed-egg video reached 5 million views in two days. Chinese users troubleshot foreigners' failed attempts in the comments. — [The Standard HK](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/225433/Chinese-steamed-eggs-go-viral-among-TikTok-refugees-on-Xiaohongshu)
- **Douyin:** a 2026 study found its recommender favours dwell time, rewatching and interaction over deliberate search. In its sample, food content was 90.2% vlog/travel and 0.98% instructional tutorials. Its sample scope is limited, so treat this as indicative. — [Frontiers in Communication (2026)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1835114/full)
- **Douyin:** a September 2026 marketing brief says niche flavours and local specialities break out through short video and live commerce. — [China Marketing Brief, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.chinamarketing.ai/intelligence/live-intelligence/2026-09-15)
- **Lemon8 (Singapore):** Lemon8 topped Singapore's iOS Lifestyle category in January 2023. — [First Page Digital](https://www.firstpagedigital.sg/resources/social/unveiling-lemon8-singapores-newest-social-media-sensation/)
- **Lemon8 (Singapore):** its format favours multi-image, step-style posts, including recipes. — [Impossible Marketing](https://www.impossible.sg/?p=83526)
- **Lemon8 (Singapore):** in 2025 Lemon8 required users to link their account to TikTok or have their Lemon8 profile deleted. — [TheSmartLocal](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/lemon8-tiktok-link-accounts/)
- **TikTok (Singapore):** a vendor blog citing DataReportal 2025 gives 3.63 million TikTok users aged 18+ in Singapore. Treat with caution. — [Videotto blog](https://videotto.com/blog/short-form-video-ideas-singapore-small-businesses-2026)
- Cookpad's global app (October 2026) imports recipes from Instagram, TikTok, X and websites through the + button or the share sheet. This acknowledges that social video is where users find recipes. — [Cookpad App Store SG](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/cookpad-find-share-recipes/id585332633)

### Inferences
- The strongest engagement primitive in Asian community apps is a cook-proof photo attached to a recipe, rather than a star rating or free-text review. Recency framing ("in the past 7 days") keeps it fresh.
- Constraining comments to recipe feedback, or to Q&A, is the norm. This is lower-moderation-cost and fits a safety-conscious local app.
- Singapore users likely discover recipes on TikTok, Lemon8 and Instagram. Import-from-link (Cookpad 2026) is a way to capture that behaviour without building a video platform.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025–2026 data on Cooksnap/つくれぽ volumes or their effect on retention.
- No reliable source on recipe-content share or volume on TikTok/Lemon8 specifically in Singapore. A 2022 Statista SEA TikTok survey was referenced in snippets but not retrieved.
- Kitchen Stories and Tasty usage in SEA: no notable evidence found. Nothing suggests they are leading apps in Singapore, but this is unconfirmed.

## 3. How do shoppable recipes and grocery integrations work in Singapore and Asia?

### Takeaway
Japan's leaders integrate supermarket flyers (チラシ), coupons and recipe suggestions keyed to discounted items, and run retail-media businesses on top. Kurashiru claims flyer data covering about 35,000 stores. In Singapore, no current, verified "add all ingredients to cart" recipe feature was found at FairPrice, RedMart/Lazada or Cold Storage. The only documented Singapore shoppable-recipe product, YoRipe (via FairPrice/RedMart), has pivoted to a general hobby app (Weyoco). China's apps sell expert-recommended ingredients and kitchenware in-app.

### Cited Findings
**Japan**
- Kurashiru shows nearby supermarket flyers, suggests recipes that use the featured items and compares prices across local stores. — [Kurashiru App Store JP](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%82%AF%E3%83%A9%E3%82%B7%E3%83%AB-%E6%AF%8E%E6%97%A5%E3%81%AE%E7%8C%AE%E7%AB%8B%E3%81%AB-%E3%83%AC%E3%82%B7%E3%83%94%E5%8B%95%E7%94%BB%E3%81%A7%E6%96%99%E7%90%86%E3%81%8C%E3%81%8A%E3%81%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%8F%E4%BD%9C%E3%82%8C%E3%82%8B/id1059134258)

In May 2026 Kurashiru launched "Kurashiru AI Supply Chain OS", an AI-agent platform for manufacturers, wholesalers and retailers. — [note.com analysis, 2026](https://note.com/gtminami/n/n31df5ac8339b)

### Inferences
- Japan's model works because of dense supermarket flyer culture and retailer data partnerships. Singapore lacks an equivalent open flyer aggregator in these results, so a local app's realistic first step is a good shopping list grouped by aisle or wet-market stall type, plus deep links. Full cart integration would require a FairPrice/RedMart partnership.
- The S$59 free-delivery threshold makes a "single recipe to cart" weak for small households. Aggregating a week's plan into one list (Cookpad and DELISH KITCHEN pattern) fits better.
- The YoRipe pivot is a cautionary data point: a Singapore shoppable-recipe startup did not sustain the cooking-commerce model.

### Gaps
- The FairPrice Group app's in-app recipe pages were not inspected. Whether they offer add-to-cart today is unverified; the web and store listing show no such feature.
- No public grocery partner API for FairPrice, RedMart or Cold Storage was found.
- Wet-market or online wet-market integrations in Singapore were not researched successfully.

## 4. What creator and upload tools exist, and how are recipes moderated?

### Takeaway
Cookpad has the most mature open-upload pipeline. Its 2026 additions include structured recipe forms, saved drafts, import from social links and AI photo-to-recipe conversion. It is moderated by written rules: original or credited content, real cooked-dish photos, no AI-generated or heavily edited images, and silent visibility limits or account suspension without notice. DELISH KITCHEN is fully professional-authored. Kurashiru mixes editor-made videos with user short-video and image posts.

### Cited Findings
- Cookpad global "What's new" (October 2026): weekly menu planning with a shopping list; recipe import from Instagram, TikTok, X and websites; AI photo-to-recipe (photograph a handwritten or printed recipe to digitise it); drafts that can be saved and posted later; folder improvements. — [Cookpad App Store SG](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/cookpad-find-share-recipes/id585332633); [Cookpad App Store ID](https://apps.apple.com/id/app/cookpad-recipe-meal-planner/id585332633)
- Cookpad Japan: users write their own recipes by filling in ingredients and steps in a set format, and can keep private recipes. — [Cookpad App Store JP](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id340368403)
- Cookpad Malaysia guidelines (Malay, valid from 28 September 2026), content rules:
  - Recipes must be home-cookable.
  - A finished-dish photo is required.
  - Sources (book, TV, blog, other member) must be credited, and permission obtained before republishing.
  - Non-recipe content in recipe format (tips, meal logs) is allowed but may be made private or de-ranked in search without notice.

  — [Cookpad Malaysia community guidelines](https://cookpad.com/my/community)
- Cookpad Malaysia guidelines, enforcement:
  - Images are deleted if they are owned by someone else, AI-generated, so heavily edited they differ noticeably from the real dish, unrelated to food, or contain people or animals.
  - Users report content through the site's options.
  - Cookpad may hide content or suspend accounts "tanpa notis terlebih dahulu" (without prior notice).
  - The guidelines contain no halal or dietary labelling rules.

## 5. What do Singapore users expect (time-poor, HDB kitchen, local ingredients, wet markets)?

### Takeaway
Singaporeans say they value home-cooked food but cook relatively little. "Time poverty", cost and small households push them toward hawker food and meal delivery. The evidence points to quick, low-effort, small-batch recipes with local ingredient names and festival or occasion hooks. HDB kitchen constraints and wet-market shopping habits have no hard data in what was found.

### Cited Findings
- **How often Singaporeans cook:** IKEA's 2015 Life at Home survey (1,271 Singapore households) found 22% cooked at home almost every day, versus 49% in London, Paris and Shanghai. — [The Straits Times via Asia News Network, 24 Jun 2026](https://asianews.network/how-meal-deliveries-in-singapore-are-evolving-as-home-cooking-declines/)
- **Preference versus behaviour:** in IKEA's 2024 survey (38,630 people, 39 countries), 37% of Singaporeans preferred a home-cooked meal, against a 28% global average. — [The Straits Times via Asia News Network, 24 Jun 2026](https://asianews.network/how-meal-deliveries-in-singapore-are-evolving-as-home-cooking-declines/)
- **Home-style delivery:** over 75% of Mom's Cooking's customers are two-person households, and its single-person orders rose from 5% to 15% in two years. Yummy Bros (chilled meal prep) has grown 20–30% a year since 2020. — [The Straits Times via Asia News Network, 24 Jun 2026](https://asianews.network/how-meal-deliveries-in-singapore-are-evolving-as-home-cooking-declines/)
- **Healthier ingredients cost more:** one tingkat (home-style meal delivery) caterer says Healthier Choice oils and fresh ingredients cost $2–$5 more than standard alternatives. — [The Straits Times via Asia News Network, 24 Jun 2026](https://asianews.network/how-meal-deliveries-in-singapore-are-evolving-as-home-cooking-declines/)
- A research paper cites Kueh (2024) on "time poverty" in Singapore: people lack time to cook, so they eat cheap, accessible hawker food. — [Scholarly Review paper](https://www.scholarlyreview.org/article/169620-decoding-the-consumers-of-hawker-food/attachment/363893.pdf)
- A 2025 YouGov dining-out report found about one in three people eat out less than a year earlier. Nearly four in ten still eat out at least weekly, rising to 50% among Gen Z and Millennials. — [Time Out Singapore](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/news/one-in-three-singaporeans-are-eating-out-less-than-they-did-a-year-ago-this-survey-says-102925)
- Local resources centre on quick and festive cooking. FairPrice's filters lead with "Under 15 mins / Under 30 mins / One Pot Meal" and multi-ethnic occasions (CNY, Hari Raya, Deepavali). — [FairPrice Recipes](https://www.fairprice.com.sg/recipes/)
- HPB recipes are healthier versions of local dishes (Laksa Fried Rice, Mee Goreng, Nasi Kuning, Nyonya Tomato Fish), with 25–45 minute times and 2-serving yields. — [HealthHub Healthy Ageing recipe library](https://www.healthhub.sg/programmes/healthy-ageing/eating-healthy/library-of-recipes); [HealthHub Eat, Drink, Shop Healthy Challenge](https://www.healthhub.sg/programmes/nutrition-hub/eat-drink-shop-healthy-challenge)
- My Healthy Plate guidance: half the plate fruit and vegetables, a quarter wholegrains, a quarter lean protein. — [HealthHub My Healthy Plate](https://www.healthhub.sg/well-being-and-lifestyle/food-diet-and-nutrition/steady-balancing-meals-with-my-healthy-plate)
- HPB has no standalone recipe app. Recipes live on HealthHub. Healthy 365 offers a meal log, and SG FoodID is a searchable nutrition database. — [HPB Tools](https://www.hpb.gov.sg/healthy-living/food-beverage/tools); [HealthierSG Useful Apps](https://www.healthiersg.gov.sg/apps/)
- Noob Cook (noobcook.com), a long-running Singapore blog by "Wiffy", is aimed at beginners. It keeps recipes simple and adapts them, and was described as useful for overseas Singaporeans. — [SCMP profile](https://scmp.com/article/981983/noob-can-cook)
- Noob Cook's ingredient pages explain local items with substitutes; for example, mee pok, with linguine as a substitute. — [Noob Cook: mee pok](https://noobcook.com/mee-pok/)
- The Singapore cookbook *Agak Agak: Everyday Recipes from Singapore* by Shu Han Lee is titled after the local idiom for cooking by estimation. — [Linden Tree Books listing](https://lindentreebooks.com/agak-agak-everyday-recipes-from-singapore.html)

### Inferences
- Default yields of 1–2 servings, plus "under 15/30 min" and "one pot/one pan" filters, look like baseline expectations in Singapore, given two-person and solo household trends.
- Festival hooks (CNY, Hari Raya, Deepavali, plus lunar 1st/15th vegetarian days; see section 7) are a recurring local content calendar.
- "Agak-agak" estimation culture matches Xiachufang's 适量 convention. Allowing "to taste" quantities with optional metric guidance fits local cooks.
- Healthier versions of hawker dishes (HPB's approach) are a credible content angle for a vegetarian app: plant-based takes on laksa, mee goreng and bee hoon.

### Gaps
- No data found on HDB kitchen size or appliance constraints (single burner, no oven) affecting recipe choice.
- No data found on wet-market shopping share or how recipe apps reference wet markets.
- No Singapore-specific recipe-app usage survey (which apps Singaporeans use) was found.

## 7. How do Asian apps handle halal, vegetarian, Buddhist "no five pungent" content and bilingual or local naming?

### Takeaway
None of the recipe apps or resources reviewed exposes a halal, vegetarian or five-pungent-free filter as a first-class control. FairPrice offers a "Vegetables" main-ingredient filter only, and Cookpad Malaysia's guidelines have no dietary labelling rules. Halal tooling in the region sits in certification and restaurant apps (JAKIM SmartHalal, HalalCentral), not recipe apps. Five-pungent (wuxin/五辛) avoidance is a real Singapore Buddhist practice with variable observance. Local food names have an official reference in the Singapore Mandarin Database.

### Cited Findings
- Many Singaporean Buddhists avoid the wuxin (five pungent): garlic, onions, chives, leeks and shallots. Observance varies: some keep it strictly, others only on the 1st and 15th days of the lunar month. — [Singapore Chinese Cultural Centre Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/popular-culture/from-temples-to-shopping-malls-a-century-of-chinese-vegetarian-food-in-singapore/)
- Monastic cooking substitutes ginger, chilli and soy sauce for umami. The Buddha Tooth Relic Temple food court serves an all-Buddhist-vegetarian, allium-free menu. — [Tabla](https://www.tabla.com.sg/lifestyle/food/buddha-tooth-relic-temple-and-museums-hidden-vegetarian-food-court); [Buddha Tooth Relic Temple](https://www.buddhatoothrelictemple.org.sg/lianxin/what-do-singaporean-budhists-eat)
- Typical "vegetarian" local dishes can contain alliums. For example, vegetarian bee hoon recipes often use garlic and fried shallots. The source page is flagged as AI-generated and low quality. — [FoodGuessr](https://www.foodguessr.com/food/vegetarian-bee-hoon)
- FairPrice recipe filters include "Vegetables" as a main ingredient and occasions such as Hari Raya and Deepavali. No visible vegetarian or halal filter. — [FairPrice Recipes](https://www.fairprice.com.sg/recipes/)
- Cookpad Malaysia guidelines contain no halal or dietary labelling rules. — [Cookpad Malaysia community guidelines](https://cookpad.com/my/community)
- Halal tools in Malaysia are restaurant and product checkers. HalalCentral filters restaurants by certification body and labels self-declared halal outlets — [HalalCentral App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6764237930). JAKIM's SmartHalal app (2017) searches certified products and outlets — [Malay Mail, 2017](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2017/10/17/jakim-releases-halal-consumer-app/1489337).
- Malaysian Standard MS 1500:2009 covers halal food production, preparation and handling, but notes it is not a full certification checklist. — [Malaysia Standards Online (MS 1500)](https://mysol.jsm.gov.my/preview-file/eyJpdiI6IitpSXZ3YjBLK1FGZFEvWm5lSEhRaVE9PSIsInZhbHVlIjoiajN5eFVSbUxLM1orSE5NeUNFQ2JGZz09IiwibWFjIjoiZmNkOWIyYWY3YjI5NDBhZDUwMmFmZWVkOGU3YWQ0NTg0OGUyOTgyZTM5MWZiMmE5ZjQ5ZTFjMjJhN2U5OWYyMCJ9)
- The Singapore Mandarin Database (Promote Mandarin Council) documents local food terms. Example: tau pok (豆卜), a deep-fried tofu puff, named from Hokkien "pok", used in rojak, satay bee hoon, laksa and yong tau foo. — [Singapore Mandarin Database: tau pok](https://www.languagecouncils.sg/mandarin/en/learning-resources/singaporean-mandarin-database/terms/tau-pok)
- Cookpad runs localised community editions; the Malaysia guidelines are written in Malay. — [Cookpad Malaysia community guidelines](https://cookpad.com/my/community)
- Xiachufang publishes Chinese-only pages using Mainland ingredient names (e.g. 生抽 for light soy sauce). — [Xiachufang recipe page](https://www.xiachufang.com/recipe/107491076/)
- HealthHub publishes an Indian recipe collection (e.g. Chickpea Curry, Atta Ladoo) alongside Malay and Chinese-origin dishes, in English. — [HealthHub Healthy Recipes](https://www.healthhub.sg/programmes/nutrition-hub/healthy-recipes)
- Vegetarian Society (Singapore), founded 1999, is non-denominational. Its meetup group was reported at 2,000+ members (undated). Its main recipe output found was a 2005 cookbook, *New Asian Traditions Vegetarian Cookbook*. — [Wikipedia: Vegetarian Society (Singapore)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarian_Society_(Singapore)); [Penang Bookshelf listing](https://penangbookshelf.com/pd-new-asian-traditions-vegetarian-cookbook-susan-amy-kiran-narain-george-jacobs.cfm)

### Inferences
- A Singapore vegetarian/vegan Cook screen can differentiate by treating "five-pungent-free" as a separate, explicit attribute from "vegetarian" and "vegan". Mainstream local and regional recipe sources do not offer it, and common "vegetarian" dishes often contain garlic and shallots.
- Lunar 1st/15th observance suggests calendar-aware prompts, such as surfacing allium-free recipes on those days.
- For Muslim users of a plant-based app, the relevant non-meat flags would be alcohol-containing ingredients (cooking wine, mirin) and certification of processed items. This is an inference; no source found shows any recipe app doing this.
- Local naming should show the Singapore name first (tau kwa, tau pok, kangkung, mee pok), with Chinese characters and an English description. The Singapore Mandarin Database is an authoritative reference for Chinese forms.

### Gaps
- No recipe app was found with a five-pungent-free filter, or with trilingual (English/Chinese/Malay) recipe presentation.
- Entries for tau kwa and kangkung in the Singapore Mandarin Database could not be retrieved (the tau-kwa URL returned 404).
- No current (2025–2026) Singapore Buddhist or vegetarian online recipe community, app, Facebook group or temple programme was confirmed. Searches returned restaurants and older material only.
- No source confirmed how Kurashiru, DELISH KITCHEN or Cookpad JP label vegetarian or vegan recipes.
