# Plant-based and diet/allergen-aware recipe apps: feature benchmark (as of 17 October 2026)

Research date: 17 October 2026. Sources are store listings, official pages, press coverage and third-party reviews. A note marks each source that was reached only as a search-result summary rather than fetched in full ("search summary"). Several App Store pages returned HTTP 404 to direct fetch. For those apps, the facts come from search-indexed listing text.

## 1. Which plant-based recipe apps lead in 2025–2026, and what is their status (active, changed or discontinued)?

### Takeaway
No single plant-based recipe app dominates. The active field divides into three groups:
- **Subscription meal-planners:** Forks Meal Planner, Deliciously Ella "Feel Better", PlantYou and The Happy Pear Recipe Club.
- **Paid or free recipe collections:** Oh She Glows, Vegan Recipe Club (Viva!) and Seeds.
- **New AI-first entrants:** Plentish (2025).

Several requested names are not active recipe apps:
- BOSH! has no app; it publishes web recipes and an API.
- Veganuary does not publish its own app.
- "Planta" is a plant-care app.
- I could not find an app called "Lightly".
- Plant Jammer appears dormant.

Two large mainstream diet-filter apps have closed or are closing:
- **Yummly** closed in December 2024.
- **Mealime** announced that it shuts down on **21 October 2026**, four days after this research date.

### Inferences
- Plant-based apps tend to be either creator-brand subscription bundles or charity/creator recipe collections. Creator bundles (Forks, Deliciously Ella, Happy Pear, PlantYou) pair recipes with meal planning, wellness content and community. Collections (Oh She Glows, Vegan Recipe Club, Seeds) are browse-and-cook apps with little personalization.

- AI conversion ("meat-to-vegan", camera-to-recipe) is now a common feature in new 2025 entrants (Plentish) and Samsung Food. None of the listings reviewed show allergen or accuracy disclaimers for AI output.

## 2. How do apps label dietary fit (computed from ingredients, editorial tags or user preferences), and how do they handle conflicts with stated exclusions?

### Takeaway
Plant-based creator apps mostly rely on editorial tags and curated plan variants: everything is vegan by construction, and "gluten-/soy-/nut-free" are plan or filter options. Mainstream planners (Samsung Food, Mealime) use stored user preferences to filter recommendations and search. Ingredient-computed labelling exists at the data-API layer (Edamam, Spoonacular) but has known failure modes. The prevailing conflict behaviour is silent exclusion from results, not a visible "conflicts with your exclusions" warning, and independent reviewers have reported filters that leak.

### Cited Findings
- **Samsung Food:**
  - Settings > Preferences holds Diet (one primary diet only), Avoidances (allergies, multi-select), Dislikes and Nutrition requirements.
  - "Diet, Avoidances and Dislikes filter recipes from your personalized recipe recommendations that do not match the criteria."
  - Cooking Experience and Favorite Cuisines were not yet used for personalization when the help article was written (about 20 months before this research).

  Source: [Samsung Food help: Managing Your Samsung Food Account (search summary; direct fetch returned 403)](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18365212922644-Managing-Your-Samsung-Food-Account)
- Samsung Food's public search filters by meal type, cook time, cuisine or "14 different diets". Saved "Recipe Box" items can be filtered by cuisine, meal type, diet, cook time, ingredients and nutrition — [Samsung Food App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planner/id1133637674); [Samsung Food help: Getting Started (search summary)](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18365507924372-Getting-Started-with-Samsung-Food-Save-Search-and-Share-Recipes)
- On Samsung Family Hub fridges, setup asks you to select allergen ingredients so that Samsung Food "will avoid them when suggesting recipes". It also sets nutrition goals, dietary restrictions, cooking skill and household members — [Samsung US support (search summary)](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS00087002/)
- A third-party competitor review claims that even paid users report recommendations not reflecting their dietary preferences. This is anecdotal and from a non-neutral source — [MealThinker (search summary)](https://mealthinker.com/blog/samsung-food-alternative)
- **Mealime:** its personalization is preference-driven. Sample plans show Plan Type ("Classic"/"Vegetarian"), Allergies ("Shellfish", "Gluten-Free"), Dislikes ("No tofu", "No olives") and Servings ("for 2"/"for 4"). It claims "over 200 personalization options" — [mealime.com](https://www.mealime.com/)
- Mealime's diets: Classic, flexitarian, pescetarian, low carb, paleo, keto, vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and dairy-free — [Mealime App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1079999103)
- A Mealime reviewer (5 Apr 2021) noted that filtering several allergies left few choices: "More allergy free recipes would be nice" — [Mealime App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1079999103)
- **Forks:** dietary fit is a plan-level choice. Gluten-, soy- and nut-free meal plan options exist, and "instant adjustments for common food sensitivities (nuts, gluten, soy)" are offered at trial start — [Forks Meal Planner App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/forks-meal-planner/id1484372198); [Forks tools page (search summary)](https://Forksoverknives.com/tools/)
- **Vegan Recipe Club:** recipes are browsed by dietary need (for example gluten-free), season, celebration and country, which are editorial categories — [Vegan Recipe Club App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1333585160)
- **Deliciously Ella:** recipes and classes can be filtered by "meal type, duration, allergy, instructor", so allergy is a filter on editorial tags — [ScreensDesign walkthrough](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/deliciously-ella-feel-better)
- **Broftware "Vegan Recipes & Meal Plans":** filters include gluten-free, sugar-free, nut-free, low-fat and quick. This is an editorial-tag model; the app was last updated in Aug 2021 — [App Store id1507212725](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1507212725)
- **Edamam** (computed): "All food database data is enriched with diet, allergy and health labeling, as calculated by Edamam based on the food's ingredients". Its "20+ claims", including Vegan, Vegetarian, Peanut Free and Gluten Free, are "generated automatically". The Diet Recommendations API page is marked retired — [Edamam Diet API (retired) (search summary)](https://developer.edamam.com/edamam-diet-api)
- **Spoonacular** (computed) supports these values:
  - Diets: vegan, vegetarian, ovo-/lacto-vegetarian, pescetarian, ketogenic, paleo, primal, Whole30, gluten free, Low FODMAP.
  - Intolerances: dairy, egg, gluten, grain, peanut, seafood, sesame, shellfish, soy, sulfite, tree nut, wheat.

  It classifies by analysing ingredients; for example, Worcestershire sauce makes a recipe non-vegetarian because of anchovies — [Educative: Spoonacular supported parameter values (search summary)](https://educative.io/courses/plan-meals-and-recipes-with-the-spoonacular-api-in-javascript/supported-parameter-values)
- **ESHA** (nutrition software) makes a recipe "inherit allergens from the Ingredients". It "errs on the side of caution because we have no way of determining contamination or hidden allergens in your real-life situation" — [ESHA help: Allergens (search summary)](https://help.esha.com/hc/en-us/articles/213575886-Allergens)
- **Yummly:** its store listing said it "excludes recipes containing the ingredients you're allergic to". A search summary cited a 2023 dietitian review on New Zealand's Health App Library reporting that allergy-listed foods still appeared in recipes and search. When I fetched the page in Oct 2026, it said only "Yummly app is no longer available", so the dietitian finding is unverified — [Healthify NZ Yummly page](https://healthify.nz/apps/y/yummly-app/)

### Inferences
- Benchmarked apps fall into three labelling models:
  - **Editorial:** an all-vegan catalogue plus allergen tags (Forks, Deliciously Ella, Vegan Recipe Club, Oh She Glows).
  - **Preference filter:** diet, avoidances and dislikes remove recipes from recommendations (Samsung Food, Mealime).
  - **Computed from ingredients:** API-level (Edamam, Spoonacular, ESHA).

  None of the consumer apps reviewed visibly shows a per-recipe "fits you / conflicts with X" badge. Exclusion-by-filter hides conflicts instead of explaining them. A per-recipe computed fit badge with explicit "detected conflict" and "unconfirmed" states would therefore stand out among the apps reviewed. QuinTaste already has such a badge in Cook.
- Samsung Food's one-primary-diet limit and "Dislikes" list show that mainstream apps keep diet (identity), allergens (safety) and dislikes (taste) as separate settings. That three-way split is a useful model for onboarding.
- The ESHA "inherit and err on caution" rule and the Spoonacular hidden-ingredient example (Worcestershire) show why ingredient-derived fit needs a curated ingredient knowledge base. Compound ingredients such as sauces, stock and asafoetida blends hide animal products, alliums or allergens.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve primary help-centre text for Samsung Food (HTTP 403). The wording above comes from a search summary.
- I found no screenshots or documentation of how any plant-based app renders diet badges on recipe cards versus detail pages. Public listings do not describe card-level badge design.
- I found no documentation of any app showing an explicit warning when a recipe opened directly (for example from a link) conflicts with saved exclusions.

## 3. What onboarding questions do these apps ask?

### Takeaway
Mainstream planners ask about diet, allergies or avoidances, dislikes, household size or servings, and sometimes cooking skill, cuisines and nutrition goals. Plant-based creator apps ask fewer food-safety questions and lead with goals ("Eat better") and plan size before a trial or paywall.

### Cited Findings
- **Mealime:** plan type or diet, allergies, dislikes and servings — [mealime.com](https://www.mealime.com/)
- **Samsung Food app/web:** primary diet (one only), avoidances (allergies, multi-select), dislikes, nutrition requirements, cooking experience and favourite cuisines (the last two were not yet used for personalization at the time of writing) — [Samsung Food help (search summary)](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18365212922644-Managing-Your-Samsung-Food-Account)
- **Samsung Family Hub:** allergens to avoid, nutrition goals, dietary restrictions, cooking skill level and household members — [Samsung US support (search summary)](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS00087002/)
- **Samsung Food+:** adds a health profile and goals with calorie and macro targets — [Samsung Newsroom AU, IFA 2024](https://news.samsung.com/au/samsung-elevates-food-experience-at-ifa-2024-a-new-standard-in-smart-dining)
- Samsung Food added free nutrition goals with daily targets and a macro breakdown in v5.51.0 (9 Sep 2025) — [Samsung Food App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planner/id1133637674)
- **Forks Meal Planner:** users set goals, serving sizes and the meals they want to cook. Sensitivity adjustments for nuts, gluten and soy are offered at trial start — [Veganuary best vegan apps](https://veganuary.com/en-us/best-vegan-apps/); [Forks tools page (search summary)](https://Forksoverknives.com/tools/)
- **Deliciously Ella:** onboarding starts with a goal (for example "Eat better"), shows a "quick win" screen of matching recipes, then a 7-day trial prompt and paywall (soft paywall; annual plan tagged "SAVE 60%"). Diet and allergy questions are not shown in the walkthrough — [ScreensDesign walkthrough](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/deliciously-ella-feel-better)
- **DietMantra** (Indian diet app) lists food-preference options "Veg, non-veg, fish, vegan, Jain, and satvik" with day-wise selection — [DietMantra App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6762642270)

### Inferences
- A minimal benchmark-aligned onboarding for a vegetarian/vegan app would cover:
  - Diet identity (vegetarian, vegan, plus religious or cultural variants)
  - Allergens (multi-select)
  - Dislikes
  - Household size or servings
  - Optionally skill or time
- Plant-based creator apps skip allergen questions in onboarding and rely on later filters. That leaves allergen-restricted users without an up-front safety setup.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the onboarding screen flows for Forks, PlantYou, Oh She Glows or Happy Pear.

## 4. What substitution UX exists (inline per-ingredient swaps, variation recipes, AI)?

### Takeaway
Substitution appears in four forms:
1. Whole-recipe swaps within a meal plan (Forks, PlantYou).
2. Per-recipe "substitutes" tabs or lookups (Plentish, Plant Jammer).
3. AI rewriting of an entire recipe to a diet (Samsung Food "Personalize Recipe", Plentish's "meat-to-vegan converter").
4. Ingredient swaps inside smart customization (Samsung Food).

I found no evidence that any app shows allergen re-checks or verification on AI-modified recipes.

### Cited Findings
- **Forks Meal Planner:** "swap out any recipe for one of 1000+ recipes in our library", which is a recipe-level swap within the plan — [Forks Meal Planner App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/forks-meal-planner/id1484372198)
- **PlantYou:** customizable meal plans where users change servings or swap recipes — [PlantYou listing (search summary)](https://similarweb.com/app/apple/1627037864)
- **Samsung Food launch:** "Personalize Recipe" lets Food AI "directly change a saved recipe to better reflect users' dietary requirements". Users "can convert a recipe into a vegan or vegetarian version", and Food AI "can swap out ingredients with consideration to what consumers have in their homes" — [Samsung Newsroom UK](https://news.samsung.com/uk/samsung-announces-global-launch-of-samsung-food-an-ai-powered-personalised-food-and-recipe-service)
- Samsung Food's current listing advertises "Smart recipe customization: adjust servings, swap ingredients, or tweak nutrition" and AI step-by-step "Smart Cook Mode" (v5.39.0, 20 Mar 2025) — [Samsung Food App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planner/id1133637674)
- A TechRadar reviewer reported being "unable to personalize any of recipes like it shows in the trailer" — [TechRadar (search summary)](https://www.techradar.com/computing/software/new-samsung-food-app-can-help-you-master-the-art-of-cooking-right-at-home)
- **Plentish:**
  - "Meat-to-vegan converter": enter a meat dish and get a plant-based version.
  - Each recipe has tabs for Ingredients, Substitutes, Steps and nearby stores.
  - Camera-to-recipe from photographed ingredients.
  - The listing contains no AI-accuracy or allergen disclaimer.

  Source: [Plentish App Store (MX)](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/plentish/id6749341323)
- **Plant Jammer:** if an ingredient is missing, "Plant Jammer lets you easily check what you can substitute for it". Its AI maps ingredients' aroma "flavor fingerprint" to suggest pairings — [SlashGear (search summary)](https://www.slashgear.com/1380285/free-android-apps-use-in-kitchen/); [The Spoon (search summary)](https://thespoon.tech/plantjammer-uses-ai-to-create-instant-flavor-mapped-recipes-for-home-cooks/)
- A BBC reporter's Plant Jammer test produced overcooked sweet-potato patties with "a strong … oat flavor". The app asked for feedback and the instructions changed within an hour — [search summary citing BBC test via BlueStacks/SlashGear results](https://www.bluestacks.com/apps/food-drink/empty-my-fridge-plant-jammer-on-pc.html)
- **Low-FODMAP practice** substitutes garlic-infused oil for garlic, which keeps garlic flavour without the FODMAP-containing solids — [Healthify NZ Monash FODMAP app review (search summary)](https://www.healthify.nz/monash-university-low-fodmap-diet-app)

### Inferences
- Plant-based apps rarely need meat-to-vegan swaps because their catalogues are already vegan. Their substitutions target allergens (nut-, soy- and gluten-free plan variants) and availability (what's in the pantry).
- AI substitution reliability is publicly questioned (TechRadar, BBC test). No disclaimers were seen in listings. A conservative design shows AI or variation swaps as unverified, re-runs diet and allergen fit on the modified ingredient list, and keeps the original visible.
- Garlic-infused oil substitution shows that FODMAP-safe and allium-free are not the same: infused oil keeps garlic flavour and is unsuitable for religious allium avoidance. An app serving Jain or Buddhist users should not reuse low-FODMAP swaps.

### Gaps
- I found no documentation of an inline per-ingredient swap UI (for example tapping "egg" to see flax egg or aquafaba) in any of the named plant-based apps. This may exist in apps whose listings could not be fetched.
- I found no app showing how a substitution affects nutrition or allergen labels in real time.

## 5. What meal-plan and shopping workflows do plant-based apps offer?

### Takeaway
The standard workflow is: pick or receive a weekly plan, swap recipes, scale servings, auto-generate an aisle-sorted shopping list, then add prep guidance. Forks adds weekend prep. Samsung Food adds pantry ("Food List") awareness and shared lists. Deliciously Ella adds one-tap add-to-list. Plentish adds cost estimates.

### Cited Findings
- **Forks:** weekly plans, auto-generated aisle-sorted grocery lists, a Weekend Prep guide and weekly notifications for new plans — [Forks Meal Planner App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/forks-meal-planner/id1484372198)
- **Mealime:** plan the week by picking meals; the grocery list is auto-generated and sorted into categories — [mealime.com](https://www.mealime.com/)
- **Samsung Food:**
  - Weekly plans convert to shopping lists, and family lists can be shared.
  - v5.39.0 (20 Mar 2025) added set-once reusable weekly plans.
  - v5.47.0 (17 Jul 2025) added adding to the planner from search and printing shared lists.
  - v5.53.0 (8 Oct 2025) made tailored plans free for everyone "up to 2 times"; more requires a subscription.

  Source: [Samsung Food App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planner/id1133637674)
- **Samsung Food+:** adds items to the Food List by photographing food (Vision AI), auto-adds purchases at checkout and offers "Search with Your Food List" to prioritise recipes using owned ingredients — [Samsung Newsroom AU, IFA 2024](https://news.samsung.com/au/samsung-elevates-food-experience-at-ifa-2024-a-new-standard-in-smart-dining)
- **Deliciously Ella:** one-tap "add all ingredients to shopping list"; lists can be ticked off and shared; a "My Hub" groups favourites, shopping list, meal planner and tracker — [ScreensDesign walkthrough](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/deliciously-ella-feel-better); [Deliciously Ella membership](https://deliciouslyella.com/en-us/membership)
- **Plentish:** weekly plans with auto shopping lists, estimated meal costs and cooking tips — [Plentish App Store (MX)](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/plentish/id6749341323)
- **Seeds:** in-cook ingredient checkoff, tap to advance steps, in-app timers, Siri "next step" and Live Activities — [Seeds App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/app/id961524722)
- **Forks reviewers (2025)** asked for:
  - Better search
  - Adding recipes to any chosen planner week
  - Searching by leftover ingredients

  Source: [Forks Meal Planner App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/forks-meal-planner/id1484372198)

### Inferences
- Shopping-list generation is table stakes. The differentiators are aisle sorting, sharing, pantry awareness and cost estimation.
- Repeated user requests for "search by leftover ingredients" (Forks) match Plant Jammer's and Samsung Food's pantry-first models. This suggests unmet demand in plant-based planners.

### Gaps
- I found no Singapore-specific grocery integration (for example FairPrice or RedMart) in any of the benchmarked plant-based apps.

## 6. How is nutrition displayed (protein, B12, iron) and are there diet-fit badges?

### Takeaway
Nutrition depth varies. Samsung Food shows per-recipe nutrition and "health scores" across about 218K recipes. Mealime shows per-recipe nutrition and syncs with Apple Health. Deliciously Ella promotes a "plant points" diversity tracker instead of nutrients. Plant-specific micronutrients (B12, iron, complete protein) are left to tracking apps such as Cronometer. Recipe apps do not prominently surface plant-specific nutrient guidance.

### Cited Findings
- **Samsung Food:** "Detailed nutrition information and health scores on over 218,500 recipes". Nutrition goals with daily targets and a macro breakdown are free (v5.51.0, Sep 2025). Adaptive nutrition tracking is a Food+ feature — [Samsung Food App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planner/id1133637674)
- **Mealime:** each recipe includes nutrition information, with Apple Health integration — [Mealime App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1079999103)
- **Deliciously Ella:** the tracker counts plant points, movement, water and sleep, and links to Apple Health. The membership page does not mention nutrition information — [Deliciously Ella membership](https://deliciouslyella.com/en-us/membership); [SheerLuxe (search summary)](https://sheerluxe.com/5-reasons-sheerluxe-loves-feel-better-by-deliciously-ella)
- **Broftware "Vegan Recipes & Meal Plans":** nutrition details per recipe, including calories, protein, vitamins and minerals (last updated 2021) — [App Store id1507212725](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1507212725)
- **Plentish:** nutrition plus environmental impact estimates; profile-based nutrition tracking — [Plentish App Store (MX)](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/plentish/id6749341323)
- **Cronometer:** tracks B12 (fortified foods and supplements), iron and all nine essential amino acids for plant foods. Sources conflict on whether it separates heme from non-heme iron. These sources are vendor-written comparison blogs — [Welling (search summary)](https://www.welling.ai/articles/best-calorie-tracking-apps-vegans-2026); [Nutrola (search summary)](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/free-diet-app-for-vegans-2026)
- **Dr. Greger's Daily Dozen:** a "science-based daily checklist to optimize plant-based nutrition" — [Nuts and Twigs (Feb 2025) (search summary)](https://nutsandtwigs.com/top-vegan-apps/)

### Inferences
- No benchmarked recipe app was found showing B12 or iron callouts on recipes. A vegetarian/vegan recipe app could add simple, sourced nutrient callouts (protein per serving, iron-rich ingredients, a B12 fortification note). This must avoid medical claims.
- Diet-fit "badges" in these apps are mainly static editorial labels ("vegan", "gluten-free") rather than computed and personalized.

### Gaps
- I found no source showing card-level nutrition or badge layouts in Forks, PlantYou or Oh She Glows.
- I found no independent (non-vendor) 2025–2026 review comparing micronutrient display in recipe apps.

## 7. Does any app support allium-free ("no onion, no garlic"), Jain, Buddhist five-pungent, or sattvic diets?

### Takeaway
No major plant-based or mainstream recipe app found offers a built-in Jain, Buddhist (five pungent spices) or sattvic diet setting. Support exists only in three places:
1. Generic "dislikes" or "avoid ingredient" lists, which you build manually (Samsung Food, Mealime, Fig).
2. Indian recipe websites with Jain or no-onion-no-garlic categories.
3. Small Indian diet or scanner apps (DietMantra lists Jain and satvik; Food Check AI advertises a Jain check).

Definitions differ by tradition, so a single "allium-free" toggle cannot capture them all.

### Cited Findings
- **Fig** (food scanner) lets users avoid "Onion and Garlic" among specific ingredients. Its major-diet list (gluten-free, dairy-free, egg-free, vegan, vegetarian, pescatarian, low FODMAP, low histamine, paleo, Whole30) has no Jain option — [Fig Firefox add-on listing (search summary)](https://addons.mozilla.org/firefox/addon/fig/); [Good For You Gluten Free review (search summary)](https://www.goodforyouglutenfree.com/fig-food-scanner-app/)
- **Food Check AI** advertises a Jain feature that "checks for onions, garlic, root vegetables, and other non-jain products" — [Food Check AI App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1579656142)
- **DietMantra** offers "Veg, non-veg, fish, vegan, Jain, and satvik" preference options — [DietMantra App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6762642270)
- **Jain dietary scope:** "onion, garlic, potatoes and other root vegetables are totally avoided by Jains" — [DEV Community Jain app write-up (search summary)](https://dev.to/vikramchandra/recipe-apis-4i6h). Jain restrictions also include honey and underground vegetables such as ginger and turmeric — [ManipalCigna Jain diet (search summary)](https://www.manipalcigna.com/diet/jain-diet)
- **Recipe-site support:**
  - Tarla Dalal's Jain category (about 250 recipes) — [Tarla Dalal Jain recipes](https://www.tarladalal.com/recipes-for-veg-recipes-jain-34?page=11)
  - Piping Pot Curry's "No Onion No Garlic (Jain)" category — [Piping Pot Curry](https://pipingpotcurry.com/category/recipes/no-onion-no-garlic-jain-recipes/)
  - VegCookbook's "30+ No Onion No Garlic Indian Curry Recipes" — [VegCookbook](https://vegcookbook.net/2023/07/30/no-onion-no-garlic-indian-curry-recipes/)
- **Buddhist five pungents (wu hun / 五荤):**
  - A dictionary gloss lists leek, scallion, garlic, rape and coriander. Taoist lists differ (chives, garlic, rapeseed, coriander, rakkyo) — [Yabla dictionary (search summary)](https://french.yabla.com/chinese-english-pinyin-dictionary.php?define=葷); [Kangxi dictionary (search summary)](https://www.kangxizidian.org/en/hanzi/hun/%E8%91%B7.html)
  - Gold Mountain Monastery's cookbook "Vegetables for a Long Life" uses no onions or garlic and cites the Shurangama Sutra on avoiding pungent roots — [Dharma Realm Buddhist Association](https://www.drbachinese.org/vbs/1_100/vbs24/24_7.html)
  - A Chengdu monastery's free meals exclude the "five pungents (wuxin), including onion, garlic, and chives" — [Sixth Tone (search summary)](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1014148)
- **Samsung Food** offers "Dislikes" and "Avoidances" multi-select lists that filter recommendations. These could be used manually for onion and garlic, but there is no religious-diet preset — [Samsung Food help (search summary)](https://support.samsungfood.com/hc/en-us/articles/18365212922644-Managing-Your-Samsung-Food-Account)
- **Monash FODMAP app:** one review says recipes are "not filterable", for example by vegetarian. Low-FODMAP recipes use garlic-infused oil as a garlic substitute — [Healthify NZ Monash FODMAP review (search summary)](https://www.healthify.nz/monash-university-low-fodmap-diet-app)

### Inferences
- **Allium-free, Jain and Buddhist diets are a clear unmet niche in recipe apps.** Mainstream apps reduce them to a manual dislike list. A Singapore app could offer named presets that list their exact exclusions:
  - **Buddhist five pungents:** garlic, onion, leek, scallion/spring onion, chives; coriander and rakkyo optional by tradition.
  - **Jain:** alliums plus root and underground vegetables (potato, carrot, ginger, turmeric root), honey, and often other restrictions.
  - **Sattvic:** alliums, and commonly also mushrooms and stale or fermented foods. This scope is not sourced here and needs confirmation.
- Presets should be transparent ingredient lists the user can edit. They should not be opaque "certified" labels, because traditions vary.
- Hidden alliums (onion or garlic powder in stock cubes, sauces, curry pastes; asafoetida blends that may contain wheat) make ingredient-level detection only as good as the ingredient database. This supports "unconfirmed" states for compound ingredients.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative, sourced definition of sattvic exclusions for app use.
- I found no evidence of Singapore or Malaysia recipe apps (for example Buddhist vegetarian 斋 recipe apps) with five-pungent filters. Chinese-language app stores were not searched.
- The Jain feature claim for Food Check AI comes from a store listing only and was not tested.
- Whether Healthify (formerly HealthifyMe; AI coach "Ria", Swiggy meal ordering) offers a Jain preference could not be confirmed. Searches found no Jain filter.

## 8. What trust and liability language do apps use about diet and allergen suitability?

### Takeaway
Store listings for plant-based recipe apps (Forks, Deliciously Ella, Plentish, Samsung Food) carry no visible allergen disclaimers, and none were found for AI-generated recipes. Robust wording comes from allergy charities, food regulators and allergen-scanner apps. Their consistent messages:
1. The app cannot guarantee a recipe is allergen-free.
2. Users must read product labels.
3. Cross-contact in the user's kitchen is outside the app's control.
4. "Vegan" does not mean safe for milk or egg allergy.
5. Users should consult a medical professional.

### Cited Findings
- **Allergy UK** recipe disclaimer: "not responsible for the outcome of this recipe or its nutritional value. Some ingredients will not be suitable for everyone, please review all ingredients before cooking… and carefully check all food labels". Allergy UK states it does "not have control over the ingredients you use … or the environment in which you are making them" — [Allergy UK weaning recipe disclaimer (search summary)](https://www.allergyuk.org/weaning-recipe-disclaimer)
- A recipe-blog disclaimer reads: "This website does not guarantee that the ingredients listed in any recipe are allergy-free… Do not rely on any statement that a particular recipe is gluten-free, dairy-free, nut-free, soy-free, or free of any other possible allergen" — [Kymberley Pekrul nutrition disclaimer (search summary)](https://kymberleypekrul.substack.com/p/nutrition-disclaimer)
- **Fooducate**'s in-app warning (as reported by BU): data may be outdated or wrong, and user content may be erroneous; "you should ALWAYS READ THE PRODUCT'S LABELS, check with the manufacturer directly, and consult a qualified and licensed medical professional" — [BU SCNC review of Fooducate (search summary)](https://www.bu.edu/scnc/2012/10/29/best-of-the-food-apps-fooducate-for-allergies)
- **UK Food Standards Agency** campaign (March 2024): people with milk, egg, crustacean, fish or mollusc allergy "should never assume a product labelled as vegan is safe to eat". Vegan is a dietary-choice label, while "free-from" is a safety claim requiring cross-contamination controls. 62% of people with animal-allergen reactions (or buying for them) were confident vegan products are always safe — [FoodIngredientsFirst (search summary)](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/consumers-should-not-rely-on-vegan-labeling-for-allergen-free-claims-stresses-uk-industry-body.html); [New Food Magazine (search summary)](https://newfoodmagazine.com/?p=222500)
- The **UK Food and Drink Federation** recommends that "a suitable precautionary allergen statement should be used alongside vegan labelling" — [FDF guidance on allergen-free and vegan claims](https://www.fdf.org.uk/globalassets/resources/publications/fdf-guidance-allergen-free-and-vegan-claims.pdf)
- **Singapore Food Agency (SFA)** on precautionary allergen labelling:
  - PAL appears as "May contain [allergens]" or "Produced in a facility that processes products that may contain [allergens]".
  - It should be backed by a cross-contamination risk assessment and "used only as necessary, as this would limit food choices".
  - "Internationally, there is no formal legal definition or framework for the use of PAL".
  - Prepacked foods containing known hypersensitivity-causing ingredients must declare them on labels.

  Source: [SFA Food Allergens: Food Labels (search summary)](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-information/food-allergens/food-labels)
- The **UK FSA** PAL guidance recommends naming the specific allergen (for example "may contain peanuts", not generic "nuts") and using PAL only when a cross-contact risk cannot be removed — [FSA precautionary allergen labelling](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/precautionary-allergen-labelling)
- **Plentish**'s listing has no disclaimers about AI accuracy or allergen safety — [Plentish App Store (MX)](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/plentish/id6749341323)
- I found no source showing an allergen disclaimer or AI caveat on Samsung Food's AI "Personalize Recipe" — [Samsung Newsroom UK](https://news.samsung.com/uk/samsung-announces-global-launch-of-samsung-food-an-ai-powered-personalised-food-and-recipe-service)
- The **ESHA** approach "errs on the side of caution because we have no way of determining contamination or hidden allergens in your real-life situation" — [ESHA help (search summary)](https://help.esha.com/hc/en-us/articles/213575886-Allergens)

### Inferences
- Suitable wording for a recipe-app fit badge, drawn from the sources above, would say:
  - The fit is calculated from listed ingredients.
  - It is not a guarantee.
  - Users should check product labels for "may contain" statements.
  - Kitchen cross-contact is outside the app's control.
  - "Vegan" or "vegetarian" does not mean free from milk, egg or other allergens.

  This matches QuinTaste's existing "computed dietary fit, not independent ingredient verification" stance.
- Singapore's SFA framing ("May contain…", risk-assessed, no formal international PAL definition) supports showing product-label PAL as a separate, user-checked field, not something the app infers.
- AI-generated or modified recipes are a gap in industry trust language. Plainly labelling AI output as unreviewed would distinguish an app on trust.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full terms of service or in-app disclaimer text for Forks, Deliciously Ella, Mealime or Samsung Food. Their legal wording on diet and allergen suitability is not confirmed.
- I retrieved only partial SFA PAL text. Exact wording requirements are unconfirmed.

## 9. What are the pricing, paywall models and user review sentiment?

### Takeaway
Pricing clusters at about US$3–7 per month or US$20–60 per year:
- Free download with a subscription paywall (Forks, Deliciously Ella, Samsung Food+, Plentish, PlantYou, Seeds).
- One-time paid collections (Oh She Glows $2.99).
- Free charity apps (Vegan Recipe Club).

Review sentiment praises recipe quality and ease. Complaints cover weak search, trial and paywall friction, filters that do not reflect preferences, and loss of saved data at shutdown.

### Cited Findings
- **Forks:** free plan plus monthly, quarterly or annual subscription. The site cites "as low as $1.85/week". A 2025 review called the "2-week trial … a lie" (one 5-day plan only) — [Forks Meal Planner App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/forks-meal-planner/id1484372198); [Forks tools page (search summary)](https://Forksoverknives.com/tools/)
- **Deliciously Ella:** 7-day free trial; £27.99/year in the UK; US $6.99/month or $32.99/year (search summary); soft paywall after goal selection — [Deliciously Ella membership](https://deliciouslyella.com/en-us/membership); [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/deliciously-ella-feel-better)
- **Samsung Food:** free, with in-app purchases at $6.99/month and $59.99/year. It holds 4.8/5 from 6.4K ratings — [Samsung Food App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planner/id1133637674)
- **Plentish:** Plus costs $5.99/month or $59.99/year — [Plentish App Store (MX)](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/plentish/id6749341323)
- **So Vegan:** $3.99/month or $19.99/year with a 7-day trial. **Oh She Glows:** $2.99. **Vegan Recipe Club:** free — [Veganuary best vegan apps](https://veganuary.com/en-us/best-vegan-apps/); [Vegan Recipe Club App Store (search summary)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1333585160)
- **Mealime:** Pro was $2.99/month; it became free for all ahead of the 21 Oct 2026 shutdown. It holds 4.8/5 from 54K ratings. One reviewer (17 Jun 2022) praised "options for food preferences and allergies" — [Mealime App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1079999103)
- **Yummly** users at closure (Dec 2024) reported crashes and no good way to keep saved recipes — [search summary on Yummly shutdown](https://www.plantoeat.com/blog/2024/12/yummly-is-closing-discover-the-best-meal-planning-alternative)
- **Deliciously Ella** review excerpt: "Great recipes (though there are quite a lot that use coconut milk, which is very high in saturated…" — [Deliciously Ella membership](https://deliciouslyella.com/en-us/membership)

### Inferences
- Recent shutdowns (Yummly 2024, Mealime 2026) that deleted user data without an export make data portability a visible trust issue. A local-first app with private export, like QuinTaste, has a credible differentiator.
- Creator-led subscription apps bundle wellness content to justify their price. A recipe-only vegetarian app may need to compete on locality (Singapore ingredients and hawker-style dishes) and dietary precision (religious diets) rather than content volume.

### Gaps
- I found no aggregated review-sentiment data (for example App Store review mining) for plant-based apps in 2025–2026.
- Current 2026 prices for Forks, PlantYou, Happy Pear and Seeds are unconfirmed; only historical or promotional prices were found.
