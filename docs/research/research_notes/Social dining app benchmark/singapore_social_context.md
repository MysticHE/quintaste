# Singapore social context for a plant-based social dining feature (research notes, compiled 17 October 2026)

Scope: local evidence for an app that helps vegetarians, vegans and plant-based eaters in Singapore meet over food. Every fact is dated where the source allows. Items marked "(search excerpt)" were seen only in search-result summaries because the primary page blocked fetching (HTTP 403) or was not opened, so verify them before quoting externally. Items marked "(secondary)" come from blogs or guides rather than the agency or author of record.

## 1. Loneliness, social isolation and evidence that shared meals build connection

### Takeaway
The strongest Singapore evidence shows that young adults (21 to 34) report the highest loneliness and the most anxiety about meeting people in person. Older adults' isolation is a policy priority, delivered through communal meals at Active Ageing Centres, and only frequent (weekly or more) participation is linked to lower loneliness. Close friendships are still formed almost entirely in person. Evidence that shared meals cause connection is mainly from the UK and Japan; I found no Singapore causal study.

### Cited Findings
**Young and working-age adults**
- IPS Singapore Perspectives 2024 pre-conference poll: 2,356 citizens and PRs aged 21 to 64, fieldwork November to December 2023. On a 3-item loneliness scale (lack companionship, left out, isolated; each scored 1 to 3), mean scores were 5.53 for ages 21 to 34, 5.09 for 35 to 49 and 5.03 for 50 to 64. Singles aged 21 to 34 scored higher than peers who were dating. — [TODAY, 29 Jan 2024, archived by IPS](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/today_youths-in-singapore-more-likely-to-report-higher-levels-of-social-isolation-and-loneliness-ips-poll_20240129.pdf)
- Same poll: 53% of 21 to 34 year olds found it easier to talk to people online than offline, compared with 46% of 35 to 49 and 31% of 50 to 64. 56% of 21 to 34 year olds felt anxious sometimes about talking to people in person, compared with 43% and 32%. — [TODAY via IPS](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/today_youths-in-singapore-more-likely-to-report-higher-levels-of-social-isolation-and-loneliness-ips-poll_20240129.pdf)
- IPS adjunct senior research fellow Dr Chew Han Ei linked youth isolation to missed pandemic-era transitions such as orientations and informal workplace contact. — [TODAY via IPS](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/today_youths-in-singapore-more-likely-to-report-higher-levels-of-social-isolation-and-loneliness-ips-poll_20240129.pdf)
- Secondary write-ups of the same poll report 16% of respondents feel isolated from others "often" and 17% feel left out "often" (secondary, not confirmed in the IPS-archived article). — [Connected to India](https://www.connectedtoindia.com/singaporeans-aged-21-34-feel-left-out-yet-okay-with-staying-single-ips-survey/)
- CNA Talking Point campaign survey (around October 2024; non-random, self-selected): of more than 1,000 responses, about half were from people aged 21 to 35, and nearly 60% of those considered themselves lonely. About 15% said they had nobody to talk to about something important (search excerpt). — [CNA, 26 Oct 2024, archived by IPS](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/cna_young-singaporeans-open-up-about-why-they-feel-lonely-yet-struggle-to-lean-on-others_261024.pdf)
- IPS Singapore Perspectives 2026 ("Fraternity") survey "Measuring Social Capital in the Digital Age" (press release 15 January 2026): 93.0% of respondents met all their close friends in person. Schools and workplaces remain the main starting points, and 71.0% of close friendships use a mix of in-person and online contact. Respondents with more diverse friends report higher trust in neighbours and fellow Singaporeans and a stronger sense of belonging. Most respondents are cautious about AI chatbots, though younger and heavier AI users trust them more. — [IPS press release PDF](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/sp2026-press-release_160126.pdf)
- Coverage of the same 2026 survey: about 89% to 89.5% have at least one close friend outside family and partners, so roughly 1 in 10 have none. 23% report at least one online friendship, rising to over 43% of 21 to 35 year olds against 20% of those aged 50 and over. About 1 in 10 turn to AI for companionship. — [Time Out Singapore, 23 Jan 2026](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/news/are-singaporeans-lonely-new-ips-study-reveals-the-state-of-local-friendships-012326); [Yahoo News Singapore](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/1-10-poreans-not-close-050000145.html) (search excerpt)

**Seniors**
- MOH written reply, 12 January 2026 (PQ by Dr Wan Rizal): one-person households aged 65 and above more than doubled from 42,100 (2014) to 87,200 (2024), per the Department of Statistics. Duke-NUS CARE (2018) found adults aged 60 and above who live alone are twice as likely to report depressive symptoms. Measures cited are Age Well SG, Silver Generation Office outreach, AAC buddying and befriending, and community mental health teams. — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/addressing-loneliness-and-psychological-distress-among-seniors-living-alone/)
- Duke-NUS (Lee, Visaria and Chan, *Journals of Gerontology: Series B*, 2025) used THE SIGNS cohort (Singapore citizens and PRs aged 60 and over, waves 2016 to 2017 and 2019, analytical N of about 1,273). Frequent neighbourhood participation (daily or at least weekly at RCs, NCs, CCs, CDCs or other neighbourhood events) was associated with a 19.5 percentage-point lower probability of loneliness two years later, against about 29.6% with no participation. Infrequent participation (monthly or less) showed no significant effect. Only 6.0% of the sample participated frequently. — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/psychsocgerontology/article/80/6/gbaf039/8037582)
- Duke-NUS study in *Gerontology* (September 2025), 2,577 older adults: informal caregiving was associated with a 28.9% higher loneliness score, without smaller social networks (search excerpt). — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12622986/)
- NUS FASS (Chua, Ho and Feng, released 7 January 2026): many seniors who live alone are socially active. The authors warn that treating "living alone" as the risk indicator can misdirect resources. — [NUS News](https://news.nus.edu.sg/when-seniors-live-alone/)
- Duke-NUS (2021, *Journal of the American Geriatrics Society*): lonely older adults lived at least three years less and spent less of their remaining life in good health (search excerpt). — [Duke-NUS](https://www.duke-nus.edu.sg/newshub/media-releases/impact-of-loneliness-on-life-and-health-expectancy)
- Age Well SG says Active Ageing Centres will use activities "such as communal meals" as a starting point to build social interaction and reduce isolation. Over 230 AACs give 8 in 10 seniors access to a centre near home (search excerpt). — [MOH, Age Well SG](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/age-well-sg-to-support-our-seniors-to-age-actively-and-independently-in-the-community/)
- AAC operators run communal dining, for example MWS "Ling Ling Time" (exercise, games, talks and communal meals), REACH and NTUC Health. "Goodlife Makan" is a community kitchen that brings isolated seniors together to cook and eat (search excerpts). — [MWS](https://www.mws.sg/active-ageing-centres/); [REACH](https://reach.org.sg/services/senior-service/active-ageing/); [NTUC Health](https://ntuchealth.sg/active-ageing/active-ageing-services)

**Newcomers and expatriates**
- InterNations Expat Insider 2025: Singapore ranked 34th of 46 for "ease of finding friends" and 32nd of 46 overall. Indonesia ranked first for finding friends. — [The Independent SG](https://theindependent.sg/4-southeast-asian-countries-are-friendliest-to-expats-but-singapore-isn-t-one-of-them/)
- Population in Brief 2025 (June 2025): total population 6.11 million, with 3.66 million citizens, 0.54 million PRs and 1.91 million non-residents. About two-thirds of non-residents are foreign workers; the rest are mainly domestic helpers, dependants and students (search excerpt). — [population.gov.sg](https://www.population.gov.sg/population-in-brief-2025-key-trends/); [Malay Mail, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2025/09/29/singapores-population-hits-611m-driven-by-rise-in-non-residents-and-migrant-workers/192794)
- IPS-REACH "Consensus Conference on Local-Foreign Integration" (24 residents of mixed residency status, reported January 2026): "community life" reached the highest consensus (77.8%) on norms of mutual respect and reciprocal effort. Multiculturalism reached only 6.7%. — [IPS press release PDF](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/sp2026-press-release_160126.pdf)

**Shared meals and connection (mostly non-Singapore)**
- Dunbar, "Breaking Bread: the Functions of Social Eating" (2017; UK national survey with The Big Lunch): people who eat socially more often report higher happiness and life satisfaction, more trust and more friends to rely on. Path analysis suggests the effect runs from eating together to bonding. Evening meals that made people feel closer involved more people, more laughter and reminiscing, and alcohol. — [University of Oxford, 16 Mar 2017](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2017-03-16-social-eating-connects-communities)
- Japanese study (*Journal of Epidemiology*, 2021; 710 older adults): eating alone and a small social network were both associated with depression. Among those living alone, network size mattered regardless of eating style. — [PMC7940972](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7940972/)

### Inferences
- Young adults are the clearest high-loneliness, high-social-anxiety segment. About half prefer online communication, so chat before meeting (the app's current invite-then-chat model) fits them. Many still feel anxious in person, so structured small groups, opener prompts and public venues lower the barrier.
- Close friendships still form in person and depend on repetition: 93% met close friends in person, and only weekly-or-more participation was linked to lower senior loneliness. Recurring formats such as standing weekly hawker lunches or monthly potlucks are therefore likely to matter more than one-off matches.
- Singapore's government already uses communal meals as its entry point against isolation for seniors (AACs). A plant-based app could complement this, but the AAC channel is offline and aimed at people aged 60 and over.
- Diverse friendship networks are linked to trust. Mixed-age and mixed-ethnicity groups are a defensible design goal, and the plant-based niche naturally crosses religious lines (Buddhist, Taoist, Hindu, ethical vegan).
- Dunbar links closeness partly to alcohol. Many plant-based and religious vegetarian diners avoid alcohol, so laughter, shared dishes and reminiscing prompts are the alcohol-free levers to design for. This is an inference; it was not tested.

### Gaps
- The full SP2026 survey report (sample size, fieldwork dates, any loneliness score comparable to 2024) was not retrieved; only the press release and media coverage were.
- A widely repeated THE SIGNS 2016 to 2017 figure, that 34% of seniors felt lonely (43% of those living alone), surfaced in a search excerpt without a confirmable primary page. Verify before use.
- I found no 2025 or 2026 MSF-commissioned loneliness study and no national loneliness strategy. Policy sits under the National Mental Health and Wellbeing Strategy (October 2023) and Age Well SG.
- No Lien Foundation loneliness study for 2023 to 2026 was found.
- No Singapore study isolates shared meals as a cause of reduced loneliness. AAC communal dining is policy intent; its outcomes have not been measured.
- No Singapore loneliness data split by vegetarians or vegans, or by expatriates specifically, was found.

## 2. Local dining norms: hawker centres, chope, bill splitting, mealtimes and meeting venues

### Takeaway
Hawker centres are Singapore's UNESCO-recognised "community dining" institution, and their norms (chope with tissue packets, self-service, low prices) make them natural, low-risk public meeting places. Splitting bills by PayNow transfer is common enough that wallets built features for it, but I found no survey on how friend groups split bills. Plant-based diners face practical pitfalls at hawker stalls because shrimp paste, fish sauce, lard and egg are common, and "halal" does not mean "vegetarian".

### Cited Findings
- Singapore's hawker culture was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity on 16 December 2020. It was Singapore's first inscription, under the title "Hawker Culture in Singapore: Community Dining and Culinary Practices in a Multicultural Urban Context". — [NEA media release](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/hawker-culture-is-singapore-s-first-inscription-on-unesco-s-representative-list-of-the-intangible-cultural-heritage-of-humanity); [NEA hawker culture page](https://www.nea.gov.sg/our-services/hawker-management/programmes-and-grants/hawker-culture)
- Scale: a written parliamentary reply reported that NEA manages 119 hawker centres with more than 14,000 stalls as of 31 March 2024 (search excerpt). A 2025 article counted 122 markets and hawker centres. NEA publishes a current list, last seen dated 17 August 2026 (not opened). — [NEA list of hawker centres, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.nea.gov.sg/docs/default-source/hawker-centres-documents/list-of-hcs_-17-august-2026.pdf); [Dollars and Sense, 2025](https://dollarsandsense.sg/business/2025-hawker-stall-rentals/)
- "Chope" is reserving a table by leaving a small item, classically a tissue packet, while queuing. Locals treat it as a trust-based convention. A Milieu survey found more people aged 45 and over than aged 18 to 29 disapprove of choping. — [Milieu Insight](https://mili.eu/insights/chope-culture-in-singapore); [Visit Singapore](https://VisitSingapore.com/editorials/foodie-culture/)
- Vegetarian food is embedded in hawker culture. An estimated 160 hawker stalls sold Chinese vegetarian food by 1988, and Fortune Centre became a vegetarian-food hub in the 1990s. Vegetarian food is now sold in food courts, hawker centres and mall chains. — [Culturepaedia (Singapore Chinese Cultural Centre), last updated 21 Jul 2026](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/popular-culture/from-temples-to-shopping-malls-a-century-of-chinese-vegetarian-food-in-singapore/)
- Many Chinese and Malay hawker dishes contain shrimp paste, fish paste, dried anchovies, lard or egg. A MUIS halal certificate excludes pork, lard and alcohol but not fish, shrimp paste or egg, so halal is not vegetarian (secondary). — [Zurich Kotak guide for Indian vegetarian and Jain travellers](https://www.zurichkotak.com/knowledge-center/travel-insurance/veg-food-in-singapore)
- At Tekka Centre, MUIS-certified stalls sit beside Hindu-run vegetarian stalls (secondary). — [Singapore Spirit](https://www.singapore-spirit.com/es/guias/comida-halal-singapore/)
- Bill splitting: Google Pay added a "Split a Bill" feature in Singapore in September 2020, aimed at meals with friends and running over PayNow. Banks describe the common pattern of one person paying by card while others transfer their share by PayNow. — [9to5Google, 25 Sep 2020](https://9to5google.com/2020/09/25/google-pay-adds-split-a-bill-feature-in-singapore-on-android-ios/); [Techgoondu, 28 Sep 2020](https://www.techgoondu.com/2020/09/28/google-pay-gets-more-social-in-singapore-lets-users-split-lunch-bills-easily); [HSBC Singapore](https://www.hsbc.com.sg/ways-to-bank/transfers/paynow/peer-to-peer-money-transfer/)
- On dates only: a Women's Weekly survey of 1,363 Singaporeans found only 30% think a first-date bill should be split (date not confirmed). — [Women's Weekly Singapore](https://www.womensweekly.com.sg/family/who-should-pay-for-the-first-date-in-singapore)
- Timeleft Singapore groups are scheduled at six people to absorb no-shows; each diner pays for their own food and drink, and venues are kept in or near the city centre (September 2024). — [SG Magazine, 16 Sep 2024](https://sgmagazine.com/lifestyle/i-had-dinner-with-strangers-using-timeleft-app-it-might-be-the-cure-for-the-loneliness-epidemic-in-singapore/)
- HDB void decks, corridors, parks, beaches and many BBQ pits are "public places" under liquor law. Drinking there is banned from 10.30pm to 7am, while homes and licensed restaurants and coffee shops are exempt. — [MHA, 2015](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/liquor-control-supply-and-consumption-act-to-take-effect-from-1-april-2015/); [Singapore Legal Advice](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/?p=2748)

### Inferences
- Hawker centres, kopitiams and food courts suit first meetings. They are public, busy, cheap, multi-diet and pay-per-dish, so each person can order and pay separately with no bill to split. Default first-meet suggestions should favour them, especially centres with several vegetarian stalls.
- Tables at hawker centres and kopitiams are typically small and shared. Groups of 2, 4 or 6, as in the app's current model, fit both these tables and Timeleft's six-person norm. Larger gatherings (up to 30) better suit restaurants, CC rooms or parks. Table sizes are an inference; I found no source.
- Given the 30% first-date figure, an explicit "everyone pays their own" or "split evenly by PayNow" field on plans would remove ambiguity. The app should not handle payments itself; PayNow peer transfers already cover this.
- Dish-level pitfalls (shrimp paste, fish sauce, egg, lard, alcohol) mean plans need declared diet and avoidance fields per person, separate from any halal signal.

### Gaps
- No source found on typical Singapore mealtimes (for example lunch and dinner peaks), weekday versus weekend social dining, or typical group sizes at hawker centres.
- No Singapore survey on how friends split bills or how often PayNow is used for it.
- No source on etiquette for sharing a table with strangers at hawker centres beyond chope; current norms after COVID were not found.
- No official count of vegetarian or vegan hawker stalls for 2025 or 2026.

## 3. Vegetarian and vegan community: size, groups, recurring events and where people find each other

### Takeaway
Strict vegetarians and vegans are about 5% of Singapore residents (YouGov, October 2023), with a much larger flexitarian (18%) and part-time religious-vegetarian population. Buddhist and Taoist practice drives 1st and 15th lunar-month vegetarianism and festival diets such as the Nine Emperor Gods. Organised vegan socialising is small and fragmented across Meetup, Telegram and Facebook, and Meetup events often draw single-digit attendance or are cancelled.

### Cited Findings
**Size**
- YouGov (October 2023; 1,027 residents aged 18 and over; online, weighted): 51% meat eaters, 18% flexitarians, 5% fully plant-based (vegetarian or vegan, not split out) and 3% pescatarians. Flexitarianism is higher among women (21% vs 16%) and Baby Boomers (close to a quarter); meat eating is highest among Gen Z. — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/articles/47585-world-food-day-singapore-major-dietary-preferences-most-favourite-cuisines-2023)
- Wikipedia's country table gives Singapore as 5% vegetarian (about 380,000 people, 2023). A travel site's claim that 40% of Singaporeans are almost entirely plant-based is untraceable and conflicts with YouGov. — [Wikipedia, vegetarianism by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarianism_by_country); [LTL Singapore](https://ltl-singapore.com/vegetarian-in-singapore/)
- Census 2020 (residents aged 15 and over): Buddhists 31.1%, Christians 18.9%, Muslims 15.6%, Taoists 8.8%, Hindus 5.0%, no religion 20.0%. — [SingStat Census 2020 press release](https://singstat.gov.sg/-/media/files/news/press16062021.ashx); [Census 2020 Statistical Release 1](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/publications/reference/cop2020/cop2020-sr1)

**Religious and part-time vegetarianism**
- Mahayana Buddhists in Singapore avoid the "five pungent foods" (wuxin: garlic, onion, chives, leek and related plants). Many Chinese abstain from meat on the 1st and 15th of each lunar month, and devotees keep a vegetarian diet for at least 10 days during the Nine Emperor Gods Festival. — [Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/popular-culture/from-temples-to-shopping-malls-a-century-of-chinese-vegetarian-food-in-singapore/)
- The Nine Emperor Gods Festival runs from the 1st to the 9th day of the 9th lunar month and is described as the largest Chinese religious festival in Singapore and Southeast Asia. More than 20 temples observe it, and some post "zhaijie" signs that ban meat on the premises. — [Culturepaedia, Nine Emperor Gods](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/en/social-practices/the-nine-emperor-gods-festival-in-singapore/)
- Temple dining is long established. The Singapore Buddhist Lodge (Kim Yam Road) opened a non-profit vegetarian restaurant in 1953 and has served three free vegetarian meals daily since the late 1970s, about 800 portions on ordinary days in the 2010s and several thousand at festivals. Bright Hill Temple has served free vegetarian meals to the public on Vesak Day. — [Culturepaedia](https://culturepaedia.singaporeccc.org.sg/popular-culture/from-temples-to-shopping-malls-a-century-of-chinese-vegetarian-food-in-singapore/)
- The Central Sikh Gurdwara (Towner Road) serves free lacto-vegetarian langar lunches daily, open to all and funded by donations (secondary; hours and menu not verified). — [Seth Lui](https://sethlui.com/best-free-food-guide-singapore/)

**Organised groups and channels**
- The Singapore Vegetarian Meetup Group has 5,473 members, a 4.6 rating from 1,191 ratings and 555 past events, organised by Bianca Polak and six others; no upcoming events were listed when fetched on 17 October 2026. Recent listings, with years not shown, include a "Singles Vegan Potluck" (fully booked at 9), a "Vegan Teppanyaki Night" (cancelled, 2 RSVPs), a dog shelter tour (cancelled, 1) and the "Inaugural Singapore International Vegan Festival" (22). The group links Telegram's "SG Vegan Kakis" channel (t.me/sgvegankakis) and the "Vegans of Singapore" discussion group (t.me/vegansofsg). — [Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/singapore-vegetarian/)
- A search excerpt of the same group listed late-2025 events including a D'life vegan meetup (23 attendees), a Veganuary high tea and movie, and a December 2025 "strictly vegan" Sunday brunch potluck (search excerpt; dates not confirmed on fetch). — [Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/singapore-vegetarian/)
- The Vegetarian Society (Singapore), founded in 1999 and self-described as the largest local vegetarian community, now operates as the Centre for a Responsible Future (CRF). — [Meetup group page](https://www.meetup.com/singapore-vegetarian/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarian_Society_(Singapore))
- Animal Allies (the outreach arm of VSS) ran "PlantForward" peer support. In 2016, 131 people had joined its WhatsApp or Facebook support group (dated). — [Malay Mail, 16 Nov 2016](https://www.malaymail.com/news/life/2016/11/16/veganism-gaining-ground-in-singapore/1251373); [Animal Allies groups page](https://animalallies.sg/groups/)
- An older Facebook group, "Singapore Social Vegans" (facebook.com/groups/sgsocialvegans), was referenced around 2010; its current activity is unknown. — [PetFinder.my](https://petfinder.my/blogs/posts/24957-VeganLand.htm)
- SgVeganCommunity (新加坡纯素学会) ran "Vegan for Everything" MRT posters from 3 July to 1 October 2025. It publishes monthly event roundups (for example World Vegan Month, 1 November 2025; Veganuary 2026 recap; April 2026 roundup) that list teasers rather than named events. A headline claiming "Singapore plant-based food market grows 40% in 2025" is unsourced, so treat it as low reliability. — [SG Vegan Community news](https://www.sgvegancommunity.net/news)
- The Singapore Vegan Festival (organiser EatRoamLive) ran from 2019, with its fourth edition on 27 August 2022 at Raffles City Convention Centre. No 2025 or 2026 edition was found. — [TTG Asia, 1 Aug 2022](https://www.ttgasia.com/2022/08/01/81555/); [Time Out Singapore](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/restaurants/singapore-vegan-festival)
- Meetup's discovery page lists multiple vegan groups and events in Singapore. — [Meetup find: vegan, Singapore](https://www.meetup.com/find/sg--singapore/vegan/)

### Inferences
- Order-of-magnitude market: 5% of roughly 3.5 to 4.2 million residents suggests about 150,000 to 210,000 strict vegetarian or vegan residents. Adding 18% flexitarians and part-time religious vegetarians widens the addressable pool several-fold. This is an inference; the YouGov sample is 1,027 and online.
- The app's existing Allium-free filter matches a real local practice (Buddhist wuxin avoidance), not a niche preference. Diet profiles should distinguish vegan, lacto-ovo, lacto (Hindu or Sikh style), no five pungent (Buddhist) and part-time (1st and 15th, festival) eaters.
- Lunar calendar moments (1st and 15th of each month, Nine Emperor Gods, Vesak) and Veganuary and World Vegan Month (November) are recurring hooks for event prompts.
- Existing vegan socialising is concentrated in one large Meetup group plus Telegram channels, with low turnout per event and frequent cancellations. That suggests demand exists but there is no low-friction, small-group, safety-aware format. This is an inference from listings, not measured.
- Temple and gurdwara meals are public, free and communal. Recommending them as venues could suit some users, but doing so needs cultural sensitivity and the institutions' consent.

### Gaps
- No member counts for Telegram ("SG Vegan Kakis", "Vegans of Singapore") or for current Facebook groups were retrievable.
- No source on Jain community size or Jain dining gatherings in Singapore, nor on ISKCON or Hindu temple community meals.
- Exact 2026 dates for the Nine Emperor Gods Festival and Vesak were not retrieved.
- No reliable source for "vegan markets" in Singapore, including the "Boring Market" named in the brief; none appeared in searches.
- The year of the Meetup group's "Inaugural Singapore International Vegan Festival" listing could not be confirmed, and no festival by that name appeared elsewhere.
- No split of vegetarian versus vegan prevalence; YouGov combines them.

## 4. Existing Singapore apps and services for meeting people over food or activities, and what is missing

### Takeaway
Timeleft (launched in Singapore in July 2024) is the main "dinner with strangers" product. Several small local start-ups and many government or charity befriending programmes exist, often kopi-themed and aimed at seniors. None of the services found is built around plant-based diets, ingredient avoidance or religious vegetarian practice. Bumble's standalone friends app was not launched in Singapore at its debut.

### Cited Findings
- Timeleft announced its Singapore launch on 18 June 2024, with the first dinner on Wednesday 3 July 2024. Singapore and Malaysia were its first two Asian markets. Users take a personality quiz, are matched into groups of five to six, the venue is revealed on the day and the app provides icebreakers. — [Free Malaysia Today, 8 Aug 2024](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2024/08/08/heres-to-chilling-while-dining-with-total-strangers)
- Timeleft Singapore pricing (September 2024): S$19.98 for a single meetup, S$149 a month or S$499 for six months, plus the diner's own meal. Dinners run on Wednesdays and can be booked up to three weeks ahead. Users are banned after two unannounced absences, and the company says it is not a dating app. — [SG Magazine, 16 Sep 2024](https://sgmagazine.com/lifestyle/i-had-dinner-with-strangers-using-timeleft-app-it-might-be-the-cure-for-the-loneliness-epidemic-in-singapore/)
- A Straits Times feature (17 February 2026) describes Timeleft as matching people for dinner, drinks or a run and lists Singapore among its cities (search excerpt; page returned 403). — [PressReader, The Straits Times, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.pressreader.com/singapore/the-straits-times/20260217/282114938033071)
- Timeleft self-reports more than 4 million members across more than 200 cities (company blog). — [Timeleft blog](https://timeleft.com/blog/best-apps-to-make-friends/)
- User posts report fixed-price menus of about S$25 to S$45 and about S$70 for three months (user-generated, low reliability). — [Lemon8](https://www.lemon8-app.com/@brianwonggghj/7440400979935134209?region=sg)
- SCMP Week Asia reported about six Singapore companies set up to help "reserved" young locals make friends, naming Sotravel, Dinner With Strangers (started in 2022 by Joell Tee, 26) and Friendzone (search excerpt; page returned 403; publication year not confirmed). — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/week-asia/economics/article/3249123/more-singapore-companies-set-help-reserved-young-locals-break-ice-make-new-friends)
- A US small-group events app, 222, was reported to be aiming to expand to Singapore by 2026 (search excerpt of a Business Times item; not verified). — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/meet5)
- Bumble launched a standalone "Bumble For Friends" app first in the Philippines, Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, the UK and the US; Singapore was not on the launch list. ### Inferences
- What is missing is a diet-aware, ingredient-aware social layer: matching on vegan, vegetarian, no-five-pungent, halal-or-no-alcohol and allergy constraints, and plans at venues that actually suit everyone. Timeleft picks the restaurant for the group, which is risky for strict vegans. That Timeleft is not diet-aware is inferred; I did not verify its dietary handling.
- Timeleft's pricing (about S$20 per meetup plus food) and Wednesday-only cadence leave room for a free or low-cost, any-day, self-organised model. Hawker-centre meetups keep food costs low.
- Government and charity befriending (AACs, kopi programmes, PA interest groups) is strong for seniors and volunteers but offline and not diet-specific. Partnering with or pointing to these could help older plant-based users rather than competing with them.
- Fragmented vegan channels (Meetup, Telegram, Facebook) lack identity signals, safety tooling and small-group mechanics. Those are the app's differentiators: profile, invitation acceptance, chat before meeting, public venues, block and report.

## 5. Regulations: home-based food, home dining, cooking classes, home gatherings, halal and alcohol

### Takeaway
Home-based food businesses, including home-based private dining, need no SFA licence but must follow food-safety law and HDB or URA home-based business conditions. Those conditions are: residents only, no catering, no selling to licensed retailers or temporary fairs, no ready-to-eat raw seafood, and no nuisance. I found no official rule specific to home cooking classes; the closest analogue is HDB's cap of three tuition students at a time. Alcohol cannot be consumed in public places from 10.30pm to 7am. Since 29 June 2026 the Online Safety Commission regime creates duties for platforms and group administrators.

### Cited Findings
**Home-based food and private dining**
- SFA (page last updated 4 August 2026): home-based food businesses do not currently need an SFA licence but must comply with food laws. They must not offer catering, including buffet lines or packed meals, and licensed caterers cannot cater from home. They must not sell to SFA-licensed establishments (hawker stalls, restaurants, cafes, canteens) and must not prepare food at home for temporary fairs, even with a fair stall licence. Ready-to-eat raw seafood is strictly prohibited. Ingredients must come from licensed sources, not from other home-based businesses or overseas trips. SFA recommends the WSQ Food Safety Course Level 1. — [SFA, Requirements for Home-based Food Businesses](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- SFA does not bar home-based businesses from delivery platforms or online promotion, but HDB and URA rules prohibit advertisements, signage or posters at residential premises. — [SFA](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- SFA's scheme page (last updated 27 March 2026) says the HDB/URA Home-based Business Scheme lets residents run small food businesses without HDB or URA approval if they meet its conditions, and that SFA "will enforce these laws". — [SFA, About HDB/URA Home-based Business Scheme](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/about-hdb-ura-home-based-business-scheme)
- Minister Grace Fu (written reply, 3 October 2022): home-based private dining businesses are "permitted to operate" under the HDB and URA home-based business schemes. SFA does not license them because they are small-scale and low-risk, and it does not track their number. They remain subject to the Environmental Public Health Act and the Sale of Food Act. — [MSE](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-home-based-private-dining-businesses-in-singapore/)
- History: during COVID-19, home-based private dining was barred and then allowed from 19 June 2020 with SafeEntry (dated; superseded) (search excerpt). — [MSE, May 2020](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/press-release-on-home-based-food-businesses/)

**HDB and URA home-based business conditions (mostly secondary; the HDB page was not retrieved)**
- The business must be run by flat owners, registered occupants or tenants (with owner consent). Third parties and non-resident employees cannot work in the flat. It must not change the residential character of the flat or cause disamenity to neighbours, or HDB or URA can require it to cease or relocate. — [sbo.sg](https://sbo.sg/business/basics/start-business-from-hdb-flat-singapore/)
- Permitted examples include small-scale baking and "private tuition for not more than 3 students at a time" (GoBusiness, as quoted by sbo.sg; GoBusiness itself returned 403). — [sbo.sg](https://sbo.sg/business/basics/start-business-from-hdb-flat-singapore/); [99.co](https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/hdb-rules-know-about/)
- One guide says HDB home businesses cannot advertise "anywhere, even online" and cannot register the flat as a business address. This conflicts with SFA's narrower statement that only advertising at the premises is prohibited, so treat it as unresolved. — [99.co](https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/hdb-rules-know-about/); compare [SFA](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/businesses-that-currently-do-not-need-licence-permit/home-based-food-businesses/requirements-for-home-based-food-businesses)
- Guides describe home-based food as takeaway, delivery or pre-arranged collection, with "no dine-in". That conflicts with the 2022 ministerial statement permitting home-based private dining (secondary). — [99.co, HDB home café guide](https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/hdb-home-cafe-complete-guide/); [Home & Decor](https://www.homeanddecor.com.sg/property/hdb/hdb-home-cafe-rules-regulations)
- Alcohol and higher-risk foods need separate licensing (secondary). — [99.co](https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/hdb-home-cafe-complete-guide/)
- Moving to commercial premises or catering needs an SFA licence; a guide cites S$195 a year for a Food Shop licence (secondary). — [SFA licence requirements](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/licence-permit/requirements-for-licence-permit-for-food-retail); [zeroabsd guide](https://www.zeroabsd.com/guides/sfa-food-licence-types-singapore.html)

**Alcohol**
- Liquor Control (Supply and Consumption) Act, in force 1 April 2015: no drinking in public places from 10.30pm to 7am, and no retail takeaway liquor sales in the same hours. Homes and licensed premises are not public places. Geylang and Little India are Liquor Control Zones with extra weekend and public-holiday restrictions and penalties 1.5 times higher. A public-event consumption permit can be sought from the Police. — [MHA](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/liquor-control-supply-and-consumption-act-to-take-effect-from-1-april-2015/); [Singapore Legal Advice](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/?p=2748)

**Halal and Muslim diners**
- MUIS halal certification has been voluntary since 1978 (search excerpt). — [MUIS](https://www.muis.gov.sg/Halal/About)
- MUIS Halal Development guidance (29 November 2024): traditional mirin, at about 14% alcohol, is not halal, and alcohol "cooking off" does not make a dish permissible. Aji-mirin or halal-certified alternatives are suggested. — [muslim.sg](https://muslim.sg/articles/is-mirin-halal)

**Online platform duties (new in 2026)**
- The Online Safety (Relief and Accountability) Act 2025 (passed 5 November 2025) and the Online Safety Commission began operating on 29 June 2026. Phase one covers online harassment (including sexual harassment), doxxing, online stalking, intimate image abuse and image-based child abuse. Statutory torts set duties for communicators, for administrators of groups or pages, and for platforms, which must take reasonable measures on notice. For harassment and stalking, victims go to the platform first and may escalate to the OSC if the platform does not respond adequately within 24 hours. The Commissioner can order content removal and account restrictions. — [MinLaw](https://www.mlaw.gov.sg/the-online-safety-commission-begins-operations-on-29-june-2026/); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/33286/online-safety-commission-operational-from-29-june-2026)

### Inferences
- **"Home dining" applications:** hosting paid home dining in an HDB flat or private home appears lawful without an SFA licence only within the HBB conditions. That means residents cook (no hired chef or helpers), there is no catering-style buffet or packed meals, there is no raw ready-to-eat seafood, ingredients are legally sourced, and there are no neighbour nuisance or signs at the premises. The app should require hosts to self-declare these points, keep exact addresses private until confirmation (already planned), and avoid language implying SFA approval or licensing. Online advertising by HDB hosts is unclear (see conflict above), so a "Not sent / Not approved" review step is prudent.
- **Free versus paid:** a casual potluck or non-commercial meal at home between friends is a social gathering, not a "business". The HBB rules and food-safety duties attach to selling food. The app should separate free social gatherings from paid home dining; the latter triggers the HBB and food-safety self-declarations. This distinction is inferred from the "business" framing; there is no explicit official statement.
- **"Cooking class" applications:** no official rule was found. By analogy with HDB's three-students-at-a-time tuition cap and the no-disamenity rule, HDB-hosted classes are safest kept very small, around three participants. Larger classes belong in community kitchens, CCs or commercial premises. Any food sold or packed for takeaway falls under SFA home-based food rules. The app's Cooking class category should require host type (HDB, private residence or commercial venue) and capacity, and flag classes above about three at an HDB flat for review. This is an inference that needs confirmation from HDB, URA and SFA.
- **Alcohol:** hosts should not sell alcohol. BYO at home is legal at any hour, but group plans at void decks, parks or BBQ pits must end drinking by 10.30pm. An "alcohol-free" plan tag also serves Muslim, Buddhist and many Hindu diners.
- **Halal:** "vegetarian" or "vegan" must never be shown as "halal". If offered, a separate "MUIS-certified venue" or "no alcohol in food" attribute should be declared and unverified unless sourced.
- **Online safety:** as a platform with group chats and event pages, the app likely falls within OSRAA's platform and administrator duties. Notice-and-response workflows (report, block, removal within a defined time) should be production requirements, and event organisers may carry administrator duties. This needs legal review; it is not established by the sources.

### Gaps
- The official HDB and URA home-based business pages could not be retrieved (403 or not found), so headcount limits for private dining guests, customer or visitor caps, and current advertising rules remain unconfirmed.
- No official SFA, HDB, URA or MOM guidance was found on home-based cooking classes or workshops.
- No current rule on maximum guests for private social gatherings at home was found; COVID-era caps have lapsed. The brief's "PA/URA rules for home gatherings" could not be substantiated beyond the HBB scheme.
- Whether the app itself would be a "platform" under OSRAA, and which duties apply to event organisers, needs legal advice.
- Whether app-facilitated paid hosting by non-residents (for example tenants without owner consent) is common or enforced is unknown.

## 6. Language, older adults and women's safety

### Takeaway
English is the main home language for under half of residents (48.3% in 2020), and Mandarin, Malay and Tamil remain significant, especially for older and religious-vegetarian communities. Older adults prefer offline interaction. Online harm, especially catfishing, is common, and the new Online Safety Commission gives victims recourse, but I found no Singapore data specifically on women meeting strangers through friendship or dining apps.

### Cited Findings
- Census 2020 (residents aged 5 and over, language most frequently spoken at home): English 48.3% (up from 32.3% in 2010), with over 80% of English-dominant speakers also using a mother tongue. The infographic gives Mandarin 29.9%, Chinese dialects 8.7%, Malay 9.2%, Tamil 2.5% and others 1.4% (read from the infographic layout; verify against SingStat tables). Ethnic composition: Chinese 74.3%, Malays 13.5%, Indians 9.0%. — [SingStat Census 2020 press release](https://singstat.gov.sg/-/media/files/news/press16062021.ashx); [Census 2020 infographic](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/modules/infographics/census-2020)
- Official social-cohesion branding uses all four languages; the SP2026 "Fraternity" identity carries Chinese, Malay and Tamil renderings alongside English. — [IPS press release PDF](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/sp2026-press-release_160126.pdf)
- Age gradient in channel preference: only 31% of 50 to 64 year olds find it easier to talk online than offline, against 53% of 21 to 34 year olds. Only 20% of those aged 50 and over have online friends, against over 43% of 21 to 35 year olds. — [TODAY via IPS](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/today_youths-in-singapore-more-likely-to-report-higher-levels-of-social-isolation-and-loneliness-ips-poll_20240129.pdf); [Time Out Singapore](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/news/are-singaporeans-lonely-new-ips-study-reveals-the-state-of-local-friendships-012326)
- MDDI surveys (fieldwork November 2024 to February 2025; 2,008 citizens and PRs aged 15 and over; released about October 2025): 84% encountered harmful online content in the past year. Among those who experienced harmful online behaviour, catfishing was the most common, mostly on WhatsApp (56%) and Facebook (41%). Two-thirds called for stronger regulation (search excerpt). — [MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/mddi-surveys-show-more-than-4-in-5-respondents-encounter-harmful-online-content--two-thirds-call-for-stronger-regulation/)
- IPS Working Paper 68, "Online Harms in Singapore: From Evidence to Action" (November 2025), includes a section on gender differences in perceived severity of harms (contents not read). — [IPS WP 68](https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/wp-68-online-harms-in-singapore.pdf)
- SHE (SG Her Empowerment) 2023 study: female Gen Z respondents were twice as likely as male Millennials to be concerned about intimate image abuse. — [SHE](https://she.org.sg/news/study-safeguarding-online-spaces)
- A Stomp commentary cites 84% of women in Singapore feeling at least "somewhat safe" and refers to CNA Women reporting on dating-app fatigue (survey source and date not given; low reliability). — [Stomp](https://www.stomp.sg/stomps-hot-takes/if-spore-somewhat-safe-most-women-why-are-we-always-guard)
- Timeleft Singapore's safety design is limited to city-centre venues, extra attendees and bans for no-shows; the review mentions no identity verification. — [SG Magazine](https://sgmagazine.com/lifestyle/i-had-dinner-with-strangers-using-timeleft-app-it-might-be-the-cure-for-the-loneliness-epidemic-in-singapore/)
- Muslims are 15.6% of residents aged 15 and over (2020), relevant to halal and no-alcohol signals. Hindus (5.0%) and Buddhists and Taoists (39.9% combined) include many lacto-vegetarian and part-time vegetarian eaters. — [SingStat Census 2020](https://singstat.gov.sg/-/media/files/news/press16062021.ashx)

### Inferences
- English-first UI is reasonable, but diet labels and dish names should carry Chinese terms (for example 素, 斋, 五辛 or no five pungent, 全素 for vegan) and Malay and Tamil equivalents. Religious vegetarian communities and older diners are where non-English use concentrates; this is inferred from Census language data and religious practice.
- Older adults favour in-person contact and are served by AACs, so features for them should support phone-light flows, large text and meeting at familiar CCs or hawker centres rather than relying on long chats.
- Women's safety: given catfishing prevalence and the absence of local data on stranger-meeting apps, defaults should be public venues only, group sizes of 4 or more for first meets (or women-only and mixed-group options), a profile visible only to accepted participants, block and report with clear response times aligned to OSRAA's 24-hour platform window for harassment and stalking, and no exact home address until confirmation. These are design inferences, not evidence-tested.
- The app's existing exclusion of dating features aligns with Timeleft's explicit "not a dating app" positioning and reduces safety ambiguity.

### Gaps
- No Singapore survey on women's attitudes to meeting strangers via friendship or dining apps, and no AWARE or SHE data specific to in-person meetups.
- No data on language preferences among vegetarian or vegan Singaporeans specifically.
- Digital literacy or app adoption among seniors aged 65 and over was not researched here; the IPS polls cover only ages 21 to 64.
- The IPS Working Paper 68 gender findings were not read.
- No source on accessibility needs (mobility, hearing) at hawker centres or community venues.
