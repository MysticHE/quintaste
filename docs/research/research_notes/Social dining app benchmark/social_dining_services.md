# Food-centred social apps and services: social dining, supper clubs, home dining, cooking classes and community meals (benchmark as of October 2026)

Research date: 17 October 2026. Every fact below has an inline source. Older facts are labelled with their source date. "Undated" means the source page shows no publication date. Several official help-centre pages (help.timeleft.com, scmp.com, vegetarian-society.org) blocked or refused direct fetches. For those, the notes rely on search-result excerpts, and this is flagged where it applies.

## Landscape and service status (which products are live, changed or discontinued)

### Takeaway
Timeleft is the dominant "dinner with strangers" product. It is live in Singapore and sells in-app purchases priced in SGD. Eatwith, Traveling Spoon, Withlocals and the relaunched Airbnb Experiences cover hosted home dining and cooking classes, but none of them shows a meaningful Singapore home-dining supply. Several older names in the brief are discontinued, absorbed into other products or unverifiable: Grub Club, HungryGoWhere, Let's Lunch and SocialTable. Singapore's own scene is mostly individual hosts who run sign-ups through Instagram or Telegram, not a marketplace.

## How are tables formed (algorithmic matching vs open listing vs host-curated) and what group sizes are used? Why?

### Takeaway
There are three models. (1) Algorithmic matching into fixed small tables of 4–6, with 6 the canonical size: Timeleft, 222, Socially. (2) Open public listings with host-set minimums and maximums: Eatwith, Airbnb, Traveling Spoon. (3) Host-curated or invite-only lists: Butter, Partiful, A Good Swallow, Singapore Telegram supper clubs. The stated reason for small tables is intimacy without awkwardness. Even 12-seat supper clubs break into tables of six.

### Cited Findings
**Algorithmic matching (Timeleft, 222)**
- Timeleft matches on "personality, shared interests, and age" into "groups of 4-6 that feel intimate but not awkward. No swiping through profiles. No bios." — [App Store (US)](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6466442949)
- The Timeleft homepage says groups mix age range, personality and language preference, and that the aim is "interesting" rather than perfect matches. Members choose city, language and availability; "We reveal the location and your group just before." — [Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/)
- One reviewer's quiz asked about name, birthday and whether you enjoy politically incorrect humour. Tables were six people. — [Vulcan Post (Aug 2024)](https://vulcanpost.com/?p=867937)
- The same reviewer felt the algorithm "may filter out people with very different views", which made it feel safer than chance encounters. — [Vulcan Post (Aug 2024)](https://vulcanpost.com/?p=867937)
- Timeleft's fixed weekly rhythm: Wednesday dinners, women-only dinners on Tuesday at 7pm in 50+ cities, Saturday coffee, group runs, and "Last Drinks" at a surprise bar after dinner in select cities. — [App Store (SG)](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/timeleft-make-new-friends-irl/id6466442949)
- Standalone Drinks: open only to subscribers, not single-ticket users; tables capped at 6; food not guaranteed; weekly in Paris (undated). — [Timeleft help](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/20303660945308--Standalone-Drinks-on-Thursdays) (search excerpt; direct fetch returned 403)
- A +1 can join only if that person has their own account and is added to the booking in the app. — [Timeleft community guidelines](https://timeleft.com/community-guidelines/)
- 222 uses a long questionnaire (values, interests, drug tolerance, character traits). Its AI is trained on feedback data and claims to improve after several events. One AFP-reported dinner had five strangers, roughly a table of six. — [Tribune PH/AFP (Sep 2025)](https://tribune.net.ph/2025/09/07/ai-powered-meet-up-apps-fight-loneliness)
- In 2022, 222 dinners were weekly and cost $2.22. — [TechCrunch (Nov 2022)](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/14/2440702/)

**Open listings with host-set capacity (Eatwith, Airbnb, Traveling Spoon)**
- Eatwith hosts choose "private events" (fixed groups such as birthdays or corporate bookings), "open events" (anyone can book, strangers mix) or both, and set minimum and maximum guests. The help example uses a private-event minimum of 2. — [Eatwith help](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/5662208-hosting-private-and-or-open-events-on-eatwith)
- Traveling Spoon's Egypt listing allows up to 4 guests aged 18+, and its Bali experience is private. — [Airbnb listing](https://www.airbnb.co.in/experiences/7201315)

**Host-curated and invite lists**
- Partiful hosts can cap RSVPs and add co-hosts. Guests must RSVP before the location or guest list is revealed, and need no account. These details come from secondary or low-authority sources. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/partiful); [jbsagolf blog](https://jbsagolf.com/blogs/partiful-app)
- Neighbourhood Supper Club caps sessions at 12, and each diner shares a table with "five other strangers", served family-style. — [Eatbook](https://eatbook.sg/neighbourhood-supper-club/)

### Inferences
- QuinTaste's People groups of 2, 4 or 6 sit within the market norm. 6 is the dominant stranger-table size; 4 shows up at the small end of Timeleft's 4–6 range and in Traveling Spoon caps. A 2-person group is closer to dating or one-to-one. Timeleft avoids that framing and runs women-only tables as its main safety-oriented segmentation.
- QuinTaste's Gatherings at 2–30 match Eatwith/Meetup-style open listings more than matched tables. Peers commonly split large events into tables of 6, which QuinTaste could copy as seating guidance.
- No peer offers people-first, invite-then-chat formation where an initiator picks people and they accept before planning together. Timeleft deliberately hides profiles. QuinTaste's model is closer to Partiful (initiator-led) plus a discovery directory, which raises the safety bar because profiles are visible before meeting.

### Gaps
- Timeleft's matching algorithm is not documented beyond "personality, interests, age, language". No published reason for 6 was found beyond the App Store copy "intimate but not awkward".
- Eatwith's typical or maximum open-event sizes are not published.

## How do hosts get vetted, what happens before approval, and how are cooking classes and home dining distinguished from casual gatherings?

### Takeaway
Paid-host platforms all gate hosts before listings go live. The rigour ranges from a document/photo review (Eatwith, within 14 business days) to Airbnb's identity, expertise and licence checks, to Traveling Spoon's video interview plus in-person site visit. Casual-gathering apps such as Timeleft vet attendees (age, face photo, real name, phone and email) rather than hosts, because venues are restaurants. In Singapore, home-based private dining needs no SFA licence but must stay inside the HDB/URA home-based business schemes and food-safety law.

### Cited Findings
**Eatwith**
- Applicants submit a profile plus a short draft experience with photos of themselves, the venue and the food. The Community Team approves it or asks for missing items "within a maximum of 14 business days". A demo event is recommended but not required. After approval, the host completes the menu, payment preferences and calendar without re-review. — [Eatwith help](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/2680329-how-do-i-become-an-eatwith-host)
- Experiences must take place within "one singular day". — [Eatwith help](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/2680329-how-do-i-become-an-eatwith-host)
- Hosts and guests "must verify their identity". Guests do not get the address or contact details until the host validates the booking. — [Eatwith help](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/432234-how-do-i-know-if-a-guest-can-be-trusted)
- The host-requirements section includes articles titled "Food safety and hygiene certifications for hosts", "Cleaning & Sanitizing Guidelines", "Do I need a business license?" and "Who can get a Star Chef experience badge?". Only titles were visible. — [Eatwith host collection](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/collections/128277-eatwith-hosts)
- Older claims of Eatwith home visits (Toque) and an acceptance rate of about 4% (Reviewed.com) are not in the current help centre. Treat them as historical. — [Toque](https://www.toquemag.com/?p=9377); [Reviewed](https://reviewed.com/refrigerators/news/meet-eatwith-the-airbnb-of-home-cooked-meals)

**Traveling Spoon**
- The process is an online application, then a video interview with a preliminary home tour, then a site visit by a trained ambassador. The ambassador rates cultural storytelling and food quality and notes accessibility details such as stairs and pets. Liability cover comes through insurance partners. — [Traveling Spoon safety](https://travelingspoon.com/safety)
- Regional community leads recruit and vet hosts in some areas. — [PYMNTS (2019)](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/traveling-spoon-travel-experiences-local-food)

**Airbnb Experiences (page dated 29 Sep 2026 per fetch)**
- Hosts must verify identity and "where applicable, pass background checks".
- Expertise means "at least 5 years of hands-on work", press or endorsements, or family/multigenerational knowledge. Airbnb may verify credentials.
- Hosts must keep relevant licences and insurance, choose a safe venue and have an emergency plan.
- Listings need at least 5 photos and at least 3 itinerary items, and are reviewed before going live.
- Airbnb may warn, suspend or remove hosts who fall short.
- — [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/whats-required-to-host-airbnb-experiences-743)

**Airbnb's Singapore food guidance**
- Serving home-cooked food to paying guests is "not prohibited per se", but the URA/HDB home-based business guidelines apply: no employed workers, small scale, no nuisance.
- Supplying alcohol needs a liquor licence; BYOB may not.
- — [Airbnb help 1725](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1725)

**Singapore regulation**
- Home-based private dining is permitted under the HDB and URA schemes. SFA exempts it from licensing as low risk, but it remains subject to the Environmental Public Health Act and Sale of Food Act. SFA does not track how many operate (MSE written reply, 3 Oct 2022). — [MSE](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-home-based-private-dining-businesses-in-singapore/)
- Home-based food businesses may not offer catering (buffet lines or packed meals) or sell to retailers or fairs. — [SFA](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-retail/home-based-food-business)

**Timeleft (attendee vetting, no hosts)**
- Members must be 18+ (21 in the UAE). Timeleft may request ID for age, and requires one clear recent face photo, a real name, confirmed email and phone, and one account per person. — [Timeleft community guidelines](https://timeleft.com/community-guidelines/)
- Dinners are not hosted or guided. — [Tasting Table (19 May 2025)](https://www.tastingtable.com/1859085/dinner-parties-with-strangers-timeleft)

**How classes and home dining are told apart from casual events**
- Eatwith uses one host flow for dinners, classes, tours and tastings. — [Eatwith help](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/2680329-how-do-i-become-an-eatwith-host)
- Airbnb applies its expertise standard to every experience. — [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/whats-required-to-host-airbnb-experiences-743)
- Timeleft and Partiful have no paid-host concept. — [Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/)
- Singapore community-centre cooking classes run as paid courses: for example, a 3-hour OnePA vegetarian workshop at $40–50 with ingredient fees paid to the trainer (Sep 2026). — [OnePA](https://www.onepa.gov.sg/courses/vegetarian-cooking-workshop-c027229747)
- Some plant-based courses can be paid with SkillsFuture credit. — [Biz IQ Academy](https://biziqacademy.com/plant-based-delights/)

### Inferences
- The industry line between "casual gathering" and "class/home dining" is money plus host responsibility for food and premises. Paid, host-run food events get pre-publication review; casual meetups at restaurants do not. This supports QuinTaste keeping Cooking classes and Home dining as application-gated categories while member Gatherings post without approval.
- A credible QuinTaste host review would need, at minimum: identity verification, photos of host/venue/food, a declaration of HDB/URA home-business compliance, no catering-format offers, an alcohol/BYOB declaration and a staffed review SLA. Eatwith's 14 business days is a benchmark. An in-person or video check (Traveling Spoon) is the gold standard but costly.
- Asking for neighbourhood only, not the exact address, matches Eatwith: the address is released only after the host validates the booking.

### Gaps
- The bodies of Eatwith's food-safety certification and business-licence articles were not visible.
- Eatwith's "Star Chef" badge criteria were not found.
- No Withlocals vetting detail was found.
- No current HDB/URA guest-number cap for home dining was found.

## How do these products capture and communicate diet and allergen requirements to hosts and tablemates?

### Takeaway
Diet capture is thin across the category. Timeleft asks about diet at booking and tries to choose a suitable restaurant, but tells members to check the menu themselves. Eatwith puts the duty on hosts through its charter ("respect any dietary restrictions… guests have informed you", list ingredients on request) and relies on pre-booking messaging. Partiful lets hosts add dietary questions to RSVPs. None of the sources shows structured allergen data being shared with tablemates. A fully plant-based host such as Neighbourhood Supper Club sidesteps the problem.

### Cited Findings
- Timeleft FAQ: "Note them when booking. Timeleft tries to find a suitable venue, but you should check the menu beforehand." — [Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/)
- Before the dinner, participants answered questions about dietary preferences and budget. — [Tasting Table (May 2025)](https://www.tastingtable.com/1859085/dinner-parties-with-strangers-timeleft)
- Timeleft classifies restaurants into three budget tiers ($–$$$) based on the typical price of a main course plus a drink, varying by city. — [Timeleft help](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/16452259807388-How-do-budget-preferences-work-for-Timeleft-dinners) (search excerpt; fetch 403); [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/?p=867937)
- Members can report "dietary requirements not being respected" by a venue, and reporting "will never affect your account". — [Timeleft community guidelines](https://timeleft.com/community-guidelines/)
- Shared dishes at Timeleft tables may not suit everyone's tastes and can cost more. — [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/?p=867937)
- Eatwith host charter Art. 5.3: hosts must "respect any dietary restrictions about which your Guests have informed you" and describe meals "honestly and in great detail", listing ingredients if asked. — [Eatwith host charter](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/1498030-hosts-charter)
- Eatwith guest help includes articles titled "What if I have special dietary requirements or allergies?" and "How do I know if my host can accommodate my allergies / dietary restrictions?" Only titles were visible. — [Eatwith guest collection](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/collections/3727660-eatwith-guests)
- Airbnb advises guests to tell the host before booking, to state allergies plainly and to distinguish allergy from intolerance or preference. Hosts should say promptly if they cannot accommodate. — [Airbnb help 2655](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2655)
- Traveling Spoon's booking form included a dietary preferences field (older third-party article). — [LBB](https://lbb.in/delhi/travelling-spoon-cook-make-friends-bangalore)
- Partiful lets hosts add dietary questions to the RSVP. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/partiful)
- SocialTable (2017) profiles included food likes and dislikes for compatibility. — [Concrete Playground](https://concreteplayground.com/sydney/food-drink/new-service-socialtable-dinner)
- Neighbourhood Supper Club's menu is fully plant-based and it says it is not halal-certified. — [Eatbook](https://eatbook.sg/neighbourhood-supper-club/)
- Some experience platforms now let users filter for vegan, gluten-free or halal (third-party travel guide). — [Mighty Travels (Jun 2024)](https://www.mightytravels.com/2024/06/7-insider-tips-for-first-time-eatwith-and-withlocals-users-maximizing-authentic-local-experiences/)

### Inferences
- Structured diet and avoided-ingredient fields that tablemates and organisers can see before confirming would be a genuine differentiator. QuinTaste's profile already records diet and avoided ingredients. Peers mostly rely on free-text notes or private messages to the host.
- Peers do not certify that food is safe for a diet; they shift the duty to the host or the venue. QuinTaste's existing "unconfirmed" ingredient language fits this market practice.
- Halal and allergen disclosure matter in Singapore. Even a vegan host flags "not halal-certified", so a declaration field for Home dining is worth considering.

### Gaps
- The full text of Eatwith's dietary help articles could not be retrieved.
- No source shows any product revealing diet information to fellow diners at a matched table.

## How do booking and confirmation work: deposits, cancellations, no-shows, reminders and venue reveal timing?

### Takeaway
Matched-table apps sell a paid seat or subscription and reveal the venue late: Timeleft gives group hints on Tuesday evening and the restaurant on Wednesday morning or the day of the event. They enforce attendance with peer-reported no-show penalties. Host marketplaces take full prepayment and apply per-event or 24-hour cancellation windows. They let hosts accept requests (expiring after 72 hours) or use instant booking, and release the address only after confirmation.

### Cited Findings
**Timeleft**
- Reveal timing: vague group insights (industry mix, nationalities, zodiac signs) appear on Tuesday evening, and the restaurant is revealed on Wednesday morning. Members can flag in the app that they are running late. — [Vulcan Post (Aug 2024)](https://vulcanpost.com/?p=867937)
- The App Store gives the same pattern: "Venue and a preview of attendees are revealed on the day of the event". — [App Store (SG)](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/timeleft-make-new-friends-irl/id6466442949)
- The restaurant is revealed on the morning of the dinner. — [Tasting Table (May 2025)](https://www.tastingtable.com/1859085/dinner-parties-with-strangers-timeleft)
- No-show policy: tablemates report a no-show in post-event feedback. The penalty is a flat 35% of the one-month subscription price, charged automatically to the saved card up to two days after the event. — [Timeleft help](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/25012703925148-What-Is-the-No-Show-Penalty-Fee) (search excerpt; fetch 403)
- Community rule: "Turn up, or cancel in time." — [Timeleft community guidelines](https://timeleft.com/community-guidelines/)
- One reviewer said that cancelling on the day more than a couple of times gets you removed (user claim, unverified). — [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/timeleft.com?page=2) (search excerpt)
- Attendance is not guaranteed: tables of 3 instead of 6 have been reported. — [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1859085/dinner-parties-with-strangers-timeleft); [FMT](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2024/08/08/heres-to-chilling-while-dining-with-total-strangers)
- Newer app versions allow booking several events at once ("multi-booking"). — [App Store (US)](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6466442949)

**Eatwith**
- Guests get a full refund before the event's own cancellation deadline, shown at checkout and in My Reservations. After the deadline there is generally no refund and the host keeps the payment. No-shows and late arrivals get no refund. If the host cancels, the guest is credited in full, typically within 2–10+ working days. — [Eatwith cancellation](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/986192-what-is-the-cancellation-policy)
- Host-side cancellation: hosts must let guests cancel "regardless the cancellation policy" if the host cannot attend. — [Eatwith host charter](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/1498030-hosts-charter)
- Booking modes: instant booking confirms automatically. Otherwise it is a request; hosts should reply within 48h, and pending requests expire after 72h. — [Eatwith private/open](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/5662208-hosting-private-and-or-open-events-on-eatwith); [Eatwith host collection](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/collections/128277-eatwith-hosts)

**Airbnb Experiences**
- The default is a 1-day policy: a full refund until 24 hours before the start, with an optional 3-day policy. A host who cancels may be charged 20% of the booking value and receives no payout, with waivers for major disruptions. — [Airbnb help 2039](https://airbnb.com/help/article/2039)

**Partiful**
- Location and guest list are withheld until the guest RSVPs. Hosts can poll guests on dates and send text blasts (secondary sources). — [jbsagolf blog](https://jbsagolf.com/blogs/partiful-app); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/partiful)

### Inferences
- Late venue reveal is Timeleft's signature, but it is also a top complaint. QuinTaste's model of a public place agreed in chat by confirmed participants avoids the complaint while keeping the address private until acceptance, as Eatwith does.
- QuinTaste's rule that plan edits reset confirmations has no direct peer equivalent. It is stricter than Eatwith, where hosts change details unilaterally.
- Peer-reported no-shows tied to a financial penalty are the strongest anti-flake mechanism found. Without payments, QuinTaste could use reliability signals (attended/no-show reports from co-participants), but that requires a production backend.
- An expiry for pending requests (Eatwith: 72h) is a useful pattern for QuinTaste's pending invitations.

## What do users praise or complain about (awkwardness, mismatched groups, flakiness, safety)?

### Takeaway
Praise centres on low-effort, structured ways to meet people, good icebreakers and app UX. Complaints centre on flakiness (half-empty tables), hard-to-reach support and refunds, subscription billing, secret venues complicating logistics, the fatigue of re-introducing yourself weekly, and the cost of shared-dish ordering. Safety concerns lead platforms toward verification, women-only formats and structured reporting rather than host supervision.

### Inferences
- Flakiness is the single most cited failure. QuinTaste's requirement that the group reaches its selected size and every participant confirms is a stronger guard than Timeleft's ticket-and-penalty model, as long as late drop-outs are surfaced quickly.
- Pre-meal chat directly addresses the "awkward reintroduction" and "unhosted" complaints, because people arrive having already talked.
- Diet-aligned matching addresses the cost and taste complaints about shared dishes. Matching vegetarian or vegan people removes a source of table friction that Timeleft's general-population tables carry.
- Users rely on Meetup, Facebook and Telegram for plant-based socialising, and HappyCow offers no events. This supports a dedicated diet-aligned social layer.
