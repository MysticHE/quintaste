# Trust, Safety and Consent for Stranger-Meetup Apps (Singapore, 2025–2026)

Research date: 17 October 2026. Labels used below: **[LAW]** = statute or binding regulation; **[GUIDANCE]** = regulator guidance or code that may only bind designated services; **[VENDOR]** = a company's own practice or claim; **[RESEARCH]** = peer-reviewed or academic work. Older facts carry their date.

## 1. Safety features shipped by leading apps (verification, age gates, block/report, safety centres, share-my-plans, public venues, women-only spaces, moderation SLAs)

### Takeaway
Leading apps use layered controls. They verify that a person is real (selfie or liveness check, sometimes an ID), check age, and offer one-tap block or report that permanently separates the two people. They also hold meetups in public, vetted places and let users share their plans with a trusted contact. Group-dining services like Timeleft add small groups, no one-to-one contact before the meal, AI chat screening with human review, graded penalties and a 30-day appeal. None of the benchmark apps publishes a formal moderation SLA. The only concrete figures found are a 24-hour report-review target claimed by Plus 1 and Timeleft's claim that AI triages reports in under a minute.

### Cited Findings
**Verification and liveness**
- [VENDOR] Tinder's Face Check (Match Group press release, 22 Oct 2025) is mandatory for all new users in seven countries and in California, with more US states to follow. New users record a short video selfie. The check confirms a live person, matches the face to the profile photos and detects the same face across several accounts. Verified users get a "Photo Verified" badge. — [Biometric Update](https://biometricupdate.com/202510/match-group-making-face-liveness-mandatory-for-us-tinder-users); [Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/tinder-rolls-out-mandatory-facial-verification-for-new-users-in-u-s/)
- [VENDOR] Tinder deletes the selfie video after verification but keeps a "non-reversible, encrypted face map" for the life of the account to detect duplicate accounts. This retention has drawn privacy concerns. Face Check is separate from Tinder's ID Check, which uses a government ID to verify age and identity. Earlier pilots in Canada and Colombia reportedly reduced duplicate or fraudulent profiles. — [Biometric Update](https://biometricupdate.com/202510/match-group-making-face-liveness-mandatory-for-us-tinder-users); [Mobile ID World](https://mobileidworld.com/tinder-launches-mandatory-facial-recognition-verification-for-new-california-users/)
- [VENDOR] Bumble (dating, March 2025) added optional government-ID verification with a profile badge. It launched in the US, UK, Australia, Canada, France, India, Ireland, Spain, Germany, Mexico and New Zealand; **Singapore was not on the launch list**. — [TechCrunch, 17 Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/17/bumble-heightens-safety-measures-with-new-id-verification-feature/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/03/17/bumble-verified-closer)
- [VENDOR] Bumble For Friends (BFF) describes a two-step photo verification that checks people match their profile pictures. No source confirmed government-ID verification for BFF specifically. — [Bumble BFF safety guidance](https://bumble.com/bff/friendship/safe-online-friends)
- [VENDOR] Bumble's 2025 dating-feature announcement grouped ID Verification with "Share Date". — [Bumble The Buzz](https://bumble.com/the-buzz/bumble-dating-features)
- [VENDOR] Plus 1, a social-plans app, says every member completes ID and photo verification before posting or joining plans, hosts approve who joins, and reports have a stated 24-hour review target. — [Plus 1 safety page](https://joinplus1.com/safety)
- [VENDOR] Eatwith's current help article says hosts and guests share profile information and must verify their identity. Guests get the host's address and contact details only after the booking is validated, which is progressive disclosure. Older reports (2013–2014) described staff home visits, "EatWith Verified" badges and a $1 million insurance plan; whether these still apply is unconfirmed. — [Eatwith help](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/432234-how-do-i-know-if-a-guest-can-be-trusted); [TIME, 2013](https://techland.time.com/2013/05/02/five-noteworthy-startups-from-techcrunch-disrupt-ny/slide/eatwith-airbnb-for-the-restaurant-industry/)

**Age gates**
- [VENDOR] Meetup members and organizers must be at least 18, and groups must target over-18s only. — [Meetup help: groups and events policies](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002897532)
- [VENDOR] Tinder's ID Check uses a government ID to verify age and identity. — [Biometric Update](https://biometricupdate.com/202510/match-group-making-face-liveness-mandatory-for-us-tinder-users)

**Block, report, unmatch and permanent separation**
- [VENDOR] Timeleft: to report a chat message, press and hold it, tap Report and confirm, with no reason or form needed. Reporting a profile works differently: the person's earlier group-chat messages stay visible, and they may keep participating unless Timeleft bans them. Banned users are removed from all group chats and member lists and get an automated email; the empty seat is not guaranteed to be refilled. — [Timeleft: How are group chats moderated](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/29193330963868-How-Are-Group-Chats-Moderated); [Keeping the Timeleft community safe](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/27963681898652-Keeping-the-Timeleft-Community-Safe)
- [VENDOR] At Timeleft, anyone you report for a guideline breach or for making you uncomfortable is never placed with you again, automatically and permanently. — [Timeleft Safety Framework, 15 Jun 2026](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-framework/)
- [VENDOR] Hinge says reporting is confidential and that "you will not see their profile again, nor will they see yours". — [Hinge help](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/360010851434-Preciso-de-reportar-algo-que-aconteceu-no-Hinge)
- [VENDOR] Meetup: members report a group via Manage membership (non-members via the three-dot menu), then choose "Report this group" and a reason. Trust & Safety reviews every report but **does not disclose outcomes**. Organizers can remove members. Fake, impersonation and ban-evasion accounts are prohibited. — [Meetup: Reporting a group or event](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/39257846459789-Reporting-a-Meetup-group-or-event); [Meetup policies](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002897532)

**Group-dining safety design (Timeleft, the closest benchmark)**
- [VENDOR] Timeleft vets every venue and uses small groups, typically six people. There are no one-to-one dates or private contact exchange before meeting. Safety tips cover before, during and after the event, and guests can leave at any time. — [Timeleft Safety Framework, 15 Jun 2026](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-framework/)
- [VENDOR] Timeleft events are never held in private homes or isolated spaces. Guests are told to share the restaurant location and expected arrival time with a trusted friend or family member, and never to send money. — [Timeleft safety tips](https://timeleft.com/safety-guidelines/); [Is Timeleft safe](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/14235930616988-Is-Timeleft-Safe)
- [VENDOR] Timeleft's Community Guidelines ban harassment, hate speech, commercial solicitation, impersonation and threats. Breaches can lead to warnings, restrictions or, in severe cases, police involvement. Timeleft reserves the right to remove accounts without notice and does not guarantee appeals on every removal. — [Timeleft community guidelines](https://timeleft.com/community-guidelines/)
- [VENDOR] Timeleft screens every chat message, so spam, slurs and scams may be intercepted before they reach the chat. — [Timeleft group chat moderation](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/29193330963868-How-Are-Group-Chats-Moderated)
- [VENDOR] Each Timeleft report is categorized into one of 9 harm categories, prioritized and routed. AI assesses reports in context with human oversight and can process a report "in under a minute", compared with hours in the earlier human queue. About 80% of enforcement actions are corrective warnings. Lower-level issues get two chances before a ban, serious ones one, and the most serious (threats, harassment, immediate risk) none. Banned users have 30 days to appeal, and decisions can be upheld, modified or overturned. Timeleft claims about 99% of experiences have no safety incident; no incident or report counts were published. It also describes crisis protocols, law-enforcement cooperation and welfare-emergency procedures. — [Timeleft Safety Framework, 15 Jun 2026](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-framework/)
- [VENDOR] "Women-only dinners" appears in Timeleft's site navigation, but the safety framework does not describe it. — [Timeleft Safety Framework](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-framework/)
- [VENDOR] Meetup requires an in-person host at every event who, at minimum, tells members the location and is the point of contact during the event. Meetup may remove groups reported as having no in-person host. Group and event descriptions must be accurate enough for members to make informed decisions. — [Meetup policies](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002897532)

**Share-my-plans**
- [VENDOR] Tinder's Share My Date (April 2024) lets users share a link with the date, time, location and the match's photo. Details can be edited, and links reportedly expire after a few months. — [ABC7 News, 2024](https://abc7news.com/tinder-app-new-safety-feature-share-my-date-online-dating-apps/14733110/)

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern for small-group meals is structural, not verification alone: public vetted venues, no private one-to-one channel before the first meal, small groups, and permanent "never matched again" separation after a report. These keep meeting risk low even when identity checks are light.
- Verification is moving from optional badges to mandatory liveness checks for new accounts (Tinder, Oct 2025). A Singapore launch would be judged against that bar. However, a retained face template is biometric personal data under PDPA (see section 3) and needs data minimization, a defined retention period and deletion when the account closes.
- A share-my-plans link is effectively a trusted-contact disclosure. Tinder's version includes the match's photo, which reveals a third party's data to people outside the app, so a privacy-preserving design would share the venue and time but not the other participants' photos or names by default.
- Women-only spaces exist as a product concept (Timeleft) but are poorly documented. If offered, they need a stated eligibility method and a misuse policy.

### Gaps
- No benchmark app publishes a measured moderation SLA, such as median time to action. Plus 1's 24-hour figure is a vendor target, not an audited one.
- I could not retrieve Bumble's official safety-features help page (HTTP 403), so the exact BFF feature list (Unmatch, Private Detector, Snooze/Incognito) is unconfirmed for 2025–2026.
- Hinge's and Tinder's current safety-centre pages were not retrieved, and no Hinge transparency report was found.
- The details of Timeleft's women-only dinners (eligibility checks, availability in Singapore) were not found.
- In-app check-ins during an event (e.g., "are you OK?" prompts) were not documented by any benchmark source retrieved.

## 2. UX and HCI research on safety for in-person meetups, harassment reporting and trust signals

### Takeaway
CSCW and CHI research, mostly participatory design with women, shows that safety work before a meeting falls mainly on users. Women want tools that support their own risk judgement, such as richer conversation before meeting and risk-detection help, rather than blanket automation. Reporting research consistently finds users see reporting as ineffective, confusing and opaque, which is why many block quietly instead. That limits the platform's ability to stop repeat offenders. Few studies directly test which trust signals reduce harm, a real evidence gap.

### Cited Findings
- [RESEARCH] Zytko et al. (CSCW 2020) evaluated a messaging-interface prototype meant to improve women's decisions before meeting face-to-face, using speed-dating events in a mixed-methods study. — [Preprint](https://dougzytko.com/research/cscw_speeddating_pre-print.pdf)
- [RESEARCH] A participatory design study with women, described as "a demographic at disproportionate risk of harm through app use", examined how mobile social matching apps (including non-dating social matching) could put safety first. — [PACM HCI, Vol 7 GROUP, 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3567559)
- [RESEARCH] Bull, Aljasim & Zytko (CSCW '21 Companion) studied how opportunistic social matching systems, which match people during in-person encounters, could be designed for women's safety. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3462204.3481751)
- [RESEARCH] Interviews with young women explored how AI could support their existing risk-awareness strategies for online-to-offline harm, augmenting rather than replacing their own judgement. — [arXiv 2204.00688](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.00688)
- [RESEARCH] Datey & Zytko (CSCW 2024), "Just Like, Risking Your Life Here", ran participatory design of user interactions with risk-detection AI to prevent online-to-offline harm through dating apps. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3686906)
- [RESEARCH] A 2025 CHI Extended Abstracts paper analysed how dating platforms characterise, respond to and safeguard against harms, and how much they rely on users' self-protection. Only the title and venue were retrievable. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706599.3719825)
- [RESEARCH] A qualitative study of women in urban Turkey (2024, Feminism & Psychology, not a CHI/CSCW venue) describes extensive individual "safety-building labor", including controlling the time and place of first meetings and planning a safe exit. — [Gurcan & Ustunel, SAGE 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09593535231194436)
- [RESEARCH] Reporting systems are often perceived as "ineffective, confusing, and time-consuming". About 47% of harassment victims choose not to report, and many block or mute instead, which limits moderation systems' ability to stop bad actors harming others. The authors propose moving reporting from an "inquisitorial" model to an "adversarial" one with more voice for the reporter. — [arXiv 2506.07041, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07041)
- [RESEARCH] The WAM! study of Twitter harassment reports (2015): over three weeks reviewers assessed 811 reports and escalated 161, leading to 70 suspensions, 18 warnings and one deleted account. It documents the friction in the reporting and review process. — [arXiv 1505.03359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.03359.pdf)
- [RESEARCH] The UseCRT project (UCL/KCL/REPHRAIN) evaluates the usability of content-reporting tools across major platforms, combining policy analysis, UI inspection and user studies of reporters' mental models. — [UCL UseCRT](https://sec.cs.ucl.ac.uk/projects/MW-USECRT/)
- [GUIDANCE/NGO] PEN America and Meedan call platform reporting "deeply flawed" and recommend making it more transparent, efficient, equitable and effective. — [PEN America](https://pen.org/press-release/new-report-to-reduce-online-abuse-social-media-platforms-must-overhaul-flawed-system-for-users-to-report-harassment-and-threats)

### Inferences
- Design implications backed by this work: (a) give users conversation space and context before meeting, such as group chat before the meal, rather than relying only on badges; (b) let users control the time, place and an exit; (c) make reporting low-effort and tell the reporter what happened to their report, because opaque outcomes (Meetup's stated practice) feed the perception that reporting is pointless; (d) treat block as a signal, for example by prompting "Do you also want to report?", so quiet blocks still reach moderators.
- Because the research shows safety labor falls on women, platform defaults should carry the load: public-venue enforcement, automatic separation after a report, and plan sharing that needs one tap, not manual work.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that directly measures whether verification badges, or which trust signals, reduce real-world harm at in-person meetups. Most HCI work is participatory or qualitative with small samples.
- No Singapore-specific HCI study of stranger meetups or women's dating and meetup safety was found.
- No study was found on read receipts or typing indicators as safety and consent factors in meetup chat.

## 3. Consent and privacy: progressive disclosure, location precision, read receipts, visibility pause, data deletion (with PDPA obligations)

### Takeaway
The benchmark apps share personal details only as needed: Eatwith releases the host's address only after a validated booking, Timeleft allows no private contact exchange before the meal, and Tinder's plan sharing is opt-in and editable. Under PDPA, profile and chat data is personal data. That brings consent, notification, purpose, retention, protection and accountability obligations, plus mandatory breach notification within 3 calendar days once a breach is assessed as notifiable. A biometric face template (if liveness verification is adopted) and precise location raise the stakes.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] Eatwith gives guests the host's address and contact details only after the booking is validated. — [Eatwith help](https://intercom.help/eatwith/en/articles/432234-how-do-i-know-if-a-guest-can-be-trusted)
- [VENDOR] At Timeleft there are no one-to-one dates or private contact exchange before meeting; the group meets at a vetted public venue. — [Timeleft Safety Framework](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-framework/)
- [VENDOR] Tinder's Share My Date is opt-in, editable and time-limited (links reportedly expire after months). — [ABC7 News, 2024](https://abc7news.com/tinder-app-new-safety-feature-share-my-date-online-dating-apps/14733110/)
- [VENDOR] Tinder keeps an encrypted face map for the account's lifetime and deletes it when the account closes, which has prompted privacy concerns about retention. — [Biometric Update](https://biometricupdate.com/202510/match-group-making-face-liveness-mandatory-for-us-tinder-users)
- [LAW] PDPA breach notification: a notifiable breach must be reported to the PDPC as soon as practicable and **no later than 3 calendar days** after it is assessed as notifiable. Affected individuals must be notified as soon as practicable. A breach is notifiable if it involves 500 or more individuals or is likely to cause significant harm. — [PDPC: Required to notify](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/Report-Data-Breach/Before-You-Report-A-Data-Breach-3/Info)
- [GUIDANCE] The PDPC Advisory Guidelines on Key Concepts set out how the PDPA's obligations apply, with chapters on consent, purpose limitation, notification, access and correction, accuracy, protection, retention limitation, transfer limitation and accountability. — [PDPC Key Concepts guidelines](https://pdpc.gov.sg/Guidelines-and-Consultation/2020/03/Advisory-Guidelines-on-Key-Concepts-in-the-Personal-Data-Protection-Act)
- [GUIDANCE] The PDPC Advisory Guidelines on Children's Personal Data in the Digital Environment (28 March 2024) apply to services likely to be accessed by children, with "child" meaning under 18. Children aged 13–17 can give valid consent if the policies are readily understandable to them; for under-13s, parental or guardian consent is required. The guidelines are non-binding, but the PDPC will likely interpret the PDPA consistently with them. — [Bird & Bird, 2024](https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/singapore/singapore-pdpc-issues-advisory-guidelines-on-the-pdpa); [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/pdpc-issues-advisory-guidelines-on-childrens-personal-data-in-the-digital-environment/)
- [LAW, from 29 Jun 2026 in force for first phase] Doxxing and online stalking are among the first five harms under the Online Safety (Relief and Accountability) Act 2025. "Non-consensual disclosure of private information" is a later-phase category. A platform that leaks precise location or home addresses therefore carries growing legal exposure. — [MDDI, 2026](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety--relief-and-accountability--act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/)

### Inferences
- Defensible defaults for a Singapore meal app: first name only, neighbourhood or planning area rather than an address, no exact GPS ever shown to other users, and the venue revealed to confirmed participants only. Visibility should be pause-able, with pausing stopping new invitations while keeping existing chats. Deleting an account should delete profile, chat and any biometric data within a stated period, subject to legally justified retention of safety records (e.g., ban records kept to prevent ban evasion), which should be disclosed in the privacy notice.
- A retained biometric template (Tinder model) is a high-risk data class. If a Singapore launch uses liveness checks, the minimal option is a provider that returns pass or fail without the platform keeping the template, with any retention justified and time-bound.
- Precise location, home addresses and the identities of other attendees should be treated as doxxing-sensitive data, because doxxing is now both a POHA offence (since 1 Jan 2020) and an OSRA first-phase harm (since 29 Jun 2026).

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the text of the PDPC retention-limitation chapter, so no specific retention period for chat or safety records is cited. PDPA sets no fixed period; the test is purpose and legal or business need, but this should be confirmed against the guidelines.
- No regulator guidance was found on read receipts, typing indicators or "last active" signals as consent or safety issues.
- No PDPC decision specific to dating, friendship or meetup apps was found in this search.

## 4. Singapore law and guidance applicable to a meetup or social-dining app (status as of October 2026)

### Takeaway
Three regimes matter most for a small Singapore meetup app. (1) The **Online Safety (Relief and Accountability) Act 2025 (OSRA)**: the Online Safety Commission has operated since **29 June 2026**. Victims of online harassment and stalking must report to the platform first and may escalate to the OSC if the platform does not respond adequately within **24 hours**. OSC directions are legally binding, and platforms have a statutory duty to take reasonable measures once notified. (2) The **PDPA** for all personal data. (3) The **Protection from Harassment Act (POHA)** for harassment, stalking and doxxing. IMDA's online-safety codes and the OCHA codes of practice bind only **designated** large services or app stores. They still set the benchmark expectations, and the app-store age-assurance code affects distribution of 18+ apps from 1 April 2026. I found no Singapore law written specifically for platforms that arrange in-person meetups.

### Cited Findings
**OSRA and the Online Safety Commission [LAW]**
- Parliament passed the OSRA Bill on 5 November 2025. — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/intellectual-property/singapore-online-safety-relief-and-accountability-bill-passed-by-parliament-on-5-november-2025); [gov.sg explainer](https://www.gov.sg/explainers/parliament-nov2025/)
- The OSC and specified statutory-tort provisions started on **29 June 2026**. Francis Ng was Commissioner-Designate from 1 to 28 June 2026 and became Commissioner on 29 June. The first five harms are intimate image abuse, image-based child abuse, doxxing, online harassment (including online sexual harassment) and online stalking. — [MDDI, 2026](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety--relief-and-accountability--act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/); [MinLaw](https://www.mlaw.gov.sg/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety-relief-and-accountability-act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/)
- The Act covers 13 harm categories in total. Later phases, with no dates published, are online impersonation, inauthentic material abuse, online instigation of disproportionate harm, incitement of violence, incitement of enmity, publication of false material, statements harmful to reputation, and non-consensual disclosure of private information. — [MDDI, 2026](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety--relief-and-accountability--act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/)
- **Platform first, then 24 hours:** "For online harassment and online stalking, you should first report the matter to the relevant online platform. If the platform does not respond, or does not respond adequately within 24 hours, you may then submit a report to the OSC." Doxxing, intimate image abuse and image-based child abuse can be reported to the OSC immediately. — [OSC: Reporting to the OSC](https://onlinesafety.mlaw.gov.sg/getting-help/reporting-to-the-osc/)
- The OSC can direct removal of content or require accounts to be limited or disabled. "The OSC's directions are legally binding – non-compliance is a criminal offence." Directions can go to communicators, group or page administrators, and platforms. — [OSC: Reporting to the OSC](https://onlinesafety.mlaw.gov.sg/getting-help/reporting-to-the-osc/); [MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety--relief-and-accountability--act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/)
- Under the statutory torts, platforms must take reasonable measures to address specified harms after notice. Platforms with greater reach face additional requirements, such as on response time; the thresholds were not stated on the MDDI page. Victims can sue for damages and injunctions. — [MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety--relief-and-accountability--act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/)
- **Identity disclosure:** the Commissioner can require platforms to provide identifying information about suspected users, such as name or verified contact details. Victims can apply for disclosure for specified purposes such as bringing a court claim, with conditions against misuse. — [MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety--relief-and-accountability--act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/); [gov.sg explainer](https://www.gov.sg/explainers/parliament-nov2025/)
- Press coverage (22 Sep 2026) reports the OSC received 500+ reports in its first two months, with the youngest victim aged seven. Only the headline was retrievable. — [Malay Mail, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2026/09/22/singapore-online-safety-commission-gets-500-plus-reports-in-first-two-months-youngest-victim-seven/236090)

**IMDA codes under the Broadcasting Act [GUIDANCE that binds designated services]**
- The Code of Practice for Online Safety – Social Media Services has been in effect since 18 July 2023 and applies to services designated under the Broadcasting Act. It sets outcomes to improve user safety, especially for children. — [IMDA Code PDF](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/regulations-and-licensing/regulations/codes-of-practice/codes-of-practice-media/code-of-practice-for-online-safety-social-media-services.pdf)
- IMDA's 2025 Online Safety Assessment Report found HardwareZone's under-18 bar could easily be bypassed. IMDA accepted a revised age check using sgID (Singpass) as meeting the code, and said it would assess effectiveness. — [IMDA Online Safety Assessment Report 2025](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/regulations-and-licensing/regulations/online-safety/2025/online-safety-assessment-report-designated-social-media-services.pdf)
- The Code of Practice for Online Safety for App Distribution Services was issued on 15 Jan 2025 and took effect on 31 Mar 2025. Designated app stores must put in place age-assurance measures (age estimation or verification) to stop children reaching their highest age-rated (e.g., 18+) apps, and must submit implementation plans that comply with data-protection law. — [IMDA press release, 2025](https://imda.gov.sg/resources/press-releases-factsheets-and-speeches/press-releases/2025/online-safety-code-of-practice-for-app-distribution-services); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/30237/new-code-of-practice-for-online-safety-for-app-distribution-services-effective-31-march-2025)
- Age-assurance obligations for app stores applied from **1 April 2026**. Users must verify their age to download 18+ apps, using a government ID, facial age analysis or usage data. The code also requires reporting mechanisms that are effective, transparent and easy to use, with reports assessed and acted on in a "timely and diligent manner". — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/12629-infocomm-media-development-authority-code-of-practice-for-online-safety-for-app-distribution-services-including-age-assurance-measures); [MustShareNews](https://mustsharenews.com/imda-app-stores-screen-ages/)
- [VENDOR] Microsoft (17 Mar 2026) said it would verify age for 18+ content in its Singapore storefronts via Singpass, facial age estimation from a selfie, or an uploaded government ID. — [Microsoft Source Asia](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/2026/03/17/microsoft-to-introduce-age-assurance-measures-in-singapore/)
- [GOVERNMENT SERVICE] sgID, the Singpass integration for developers, offers a derived boolean field confirming a user is over 18 without revealing the birthdate. It is free, but its report page says no new features are planned (maintenance mode). — [sgID product page](https://products.open.gov.sg/sgid); [sgID report](https://reports.open.gov.sg/sgid)

**Online Criminal Harms Act (OCHA) [LAW + codes for designated services]**
- Most OCHA provisions took effect on 1 Feb 2024 and the rest on 24 Jun 2024. Codes for online communication and e-commerce services took effect on 26 Jun 2024 for designated high-scam-risk services. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/singapore-introduces-codes-of-practice-under-the-online-criminal-harms/); [SPF: Introduction to OCHA](https://www.police.gov.sg/Knowledge-Hub/Legislation/Online-Harms-Act-Overview/Introduction-to-OCHA)
- In August 2026, SPF issued a new Messaging Code covering seven designated messaging and conferencing services (WhatsApp, Telegram, WeChat, iMessage, FaceTime, Google Messages, Google Meet). It requires measures that make contact from unknown accounts harder or warn users about it. A new Social Media Code replaces the 2024 code for online communication services, and an enhanced E-Commerce Code was issued. SPF says scam cases on designated services fell about 37% between 2024 and 2025. — [SPF, Aug 2026](https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/news/2026/08/20260818_issuance_of_codes_of_practice_under_the_online_criminal_harms_act); [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/spf-issues-new-and-enhanced-codes-of-practice-under-the-online-criminal-harms-act-1/)
- OCHA directions can be issued when a designated officer suspects online activity is preparatory to, or in furtherance of, a scam or malicious cyber offence. — [SPF: Introduction to OCHA](https://www.police.gov.sg/Knowledge-Hub/Legislation/Online-Harms-Act-Overview/Introduction-to-OCHA)
- Scam context: 2025 scam and cybercrime cases fell to 41,974 (about 25% lower) and losses to S$913.1m (down 19%). The top five scam types by loss were investment, government-official impersonation, job, phishing and business email compromise; romance scams were not in the top five. — [SPF, Feb 2026](https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/police-life/2026/02/scams-and-cybercrime-fell-by-almost-a-quarter-in-2025); [Malay Mail, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2026/02/26/singapore-police-scam-cases-drop-248pc-in-2025-losses-fall-to-s913m-but-youths-remain-most-affected/210482)

**Protection from Harassment Act (POHA) [LAW]**
- Section 3: intentionally causing harassment, alarm or distress through threatening, abusive or insulting words or conduct, including electronically. Section 4 covers conduct likely to cause such feelings even without intent. Section 7, unlawful stalking, covers a course of conduct such as following, communicating, loitering or surveillance. Sources disagree on section 3 maximum penalties ($5,000 and/or 6 months vs 12 months), so check SSO. — [Singapore Legal Advice guide](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/law-articles/singapore-protection-harassment-act/); [Yuen Law guide](https://yuenlaw.com.sg/singapore-law/protection-from-harassment-act-guide/)
- Doxxing became an offence through amendments partly in force from 1 Jan 2020. — [Allen & Gledhill, 2020](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/13999/changes-to-protection-from-harassment-act-partially-in-force-from-1-january-2020-new-offence-of-doxxing-introduced)
- The Protection from Harassment Court (since June 2021) hears expedited protection-order applications within 2–3 working days, or within 24 hours where there is violence or a risk of it. — [Singapore Law Watch](https://singaporelawwatch.sg/Headlines/victims-turning-to-the-court-to-stop-harassment-such-as-cyber-bullying-and-doxxing); [Yuen Law guide](https://yuenlaw.com.sg/singapore-law/protection-from-harassment-act-guide/)

### Inferences
- The OSC's 24-hour escalation rule effectively sets a **de facto response benchmark for any Singapore platform, whatever its size**. A user harassed through the app's chat can go to the OSC if the app has not responded adequately within 24 hours of their report. A production app therefore needs a staffed (or reliably on-call) intake that can acknowledge and triage harassment and stalking reports within 24 hours. It also needs time-stamped report receipts that users can show the OSC as proof they reported to the platform first.
- Because OSC directions to limit or disable accounts and to disclose identity information are binding, with non-compliance a criminal offence, the app needs operational capability before launch. That means the ability to take down content and suspend accounts quickly, a lawful-request contact point, and records linking accounts to contact details (e.g., verified phone or email) so it can respond to identity-disclosure directions.
- IMDA's social media code and OCHA's codes apply only to designated services, so a startup meal app is unlikely to be designated at launch. Their requirements (accessible reporting, timely and diligent action, protections for children, friction for unknown contacts) are still the regulator's stated baseline and a reasonable design target.
- On age: the app should be rated 18+ in app stores, so store-level age assurance applies in Singapore from 1 April 2026, and should add its own adult check. A self-declaration is the weakest option; sgID's over-18 boolean (free, no birthdate disclosed) is the most privacy-preserving strong option in Singapore. Its maintenance-mode status is a dependency risk.

### Gaps
- The full OSRA text was not retrieved. Missing are the exact statutory definitions of online harassment, doxxing and stalking, the reach threshold for "greater reach" platforms and their response deadlines, penalty amounts, and whether a small app counts as a "platform" for the statutory-tort duties.
- The exact report-handling provisions of the IMDA Social Media Services Code (timelines, informing reporters of decisions) were not confirmed against the code text.
- No Singapore law or guidance was found that is specific to platforms facilitating in-person meetups (no licensing, venue or host-vetting rules). Hosted home-dining or cooking-class events may raise separate food-safety, licensing or insurance questions that this search did not cover.
- The detailed romance-scam figures for 2025 were not in the retrieved SPF sources.

## 5. Handling reports (acknowledgement, review time, appeal) and honest labelling for local-only prototypes; anti-patterns that damage trust

### Takeaway
A production-ready report pipeline should do five things. It should acknowledge receipt immediately with a time-stamped reference, triage by harm severity with urgent paths for threats and immediate risk, act within 24 hours for harassment and stalking given the OSC's escalation window, and tell the reporter the outcome category. Sanctioned users should get a reason and an appeal window; Timeleft offers 30 days. Prototypes that cannot deliver reports must say so plainly ("Not sent") and point users to real help. Trust is damaged by fake or seeded profiles that are not labelled, by misleading interface pressure, and by hidden outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] Timeleft's pipeline is categorize (9 harm categories), prioritize and route; AI triage with human oversight; graduated enforcement (two chances, one chance or no warning depending on severity); a 30-day appeal with uphold, modify or overturn outcomes; automatic permanent separation of reporter and reported. — [Timeleft Safety Framework, 15 Jun 2026](https://timeleft.com/blog/safety-framework/)
- [VENDOR] Meetup does not disclose review outcomes to reporters, citing privacy. — [Meetup: Reporting a group or event](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/39257846459789-Reporting-a-Meetup-group-or-event)
- [VENDOR] Plus 1 states a 24-hour review target for reports. — [Plus 1 safety](https://joinplus1.com/safety)
- [LAW] OSC escalation is available if the platform "does not respond, or does not respond adequately within 24 hours" (harassment and stalking). — [OSC](https://onlinesafety.mlaw.gov.sg/getting-help/reporting-to-the-osc/)
- [GUIDANCE] IMDA's app-store code requires reporting mechanisms that are "effective, transparent, easy to access and easy to use", with reports assessed and acted on in a "timely and diligent manner". — [Digital Policy Alert summary](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/12629-infocomm-media-development-authority-code-of-practice-for-online-safety-for-app-distribution-services-including-age-assurance-measures)
- [RESEARCH] Users find reporting "ineffective, confusing, and time-consuming". Many block instead of reporting, which undermines prevention. — [arXiv 2506.07041](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07041)
- [LAW/ENFORCEMENT, US] The FTC's 2019 suit alleged Match sent "you've got interest" notices from accounts already flagged as likely fake, exposing users to romance scams to drive subscriptions. Most counts were dismissed in 2022 under the Communications Decency Act. In 2025 Match Group agreed to pay $14 million and stop deceptive advertising, cancellation and billing practices. — [CBS News, 2019](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/match-com-lawsuit-ftc-sues-match-com-over-messages-from-fake-users/); [Bloomberg, Mar 2022](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-03-24/match-group-avoids-844-million-in-ftc-claims-on-judge-s-ruling); [Global Dating Insights, 2025](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/from-the-web/match-group-settles-ftc-user-deception-charges/)
- [LAW/ENFORCEMENT, Singapore] Singapore's consumer regulator (CCS, formerly CCCS) treats dark patterns as an enforcement priority under the Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act. Examples: a travel platform's undisclosed paid "Preferred" ranking and 5-minute countdown timer (changed voluntarily), a fake-review case involving AI and a third-party platform, and a court order against a subscription trap. Misleading interface features can be unfair trade practices. — [Rajah & Tann: dark patterns](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/the-rising-response-to-dark-patterns-lessons-from-new-regulations-and-enforcement-actions-by-global-regulators/); [Rajah & Tann: CCS enforcement in practice](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/ccs-consumer-protection-enforcement-in-practice/)
- [VENDOR] Meetup prohibits fake, malicious and impersonation accounts and accounts created to evade bans. — [Meetup policies](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002897532)
- [LAW, later phase] "Online impersonation" and "inauthentic material abuse" are OSRA categories (statutory torts) to be implemented after the first phase. — [MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/online-safety-commission-and-online-safety--relief-and-accountability--act-2025-to-start-on-29-june-2026/)

### Inferences
**Report lifecycle that would meet the Singapore context (synthesis, not a legal opinion)**
1. Report entry in at most two taps from any message, profile, group or event, with no reason required to submit (Timeleft model) and an optional category plus evidence attachment.
2. An immediate in-app receipt with a reference ID and a server timestamp, so the user has proof of reporting to the platform for an OSC escalation.
3. Automatic protective action on submit: hide the reported person from the reporter, never place them in the same group again, and offer block.
4. Severity triage: imminent danger goes to an emergency-services prompt (999) plus urgent human review; harassment and stalking to a human decision within 24 hours; doxxing and intimate image content to immediate removal or hold, because the OSC accepts these with no wait.
5. Outcome notice to the reporter (at least "action taken" or "no violation", without disclosing the other person's sanction details) and a reasoned notice to the sanctioned user with an appeal window (30 days is the benchmark).
6. Capability to execute OSC directions (takedown, account restriction, identity disclosure) and a published contact point.
7. Records and retention for reports and evidence that are justified under PDPA purpose and retention limits.

**Honest labelling for a local-only prototype (no staffed review)**
- The prototype should never imply a report was sent or reviewed. Use explicit states such as "Saved on this device · Not sent", say no one at the platform will see it, and give real routes for urgent help: emergency 999 and police, the platform-first rule, and the OSC at osc.gov.sg for doxxing and intimate-image abuse. This matches the QuinTaste repository's current local-only Social contract, where reports remain "Not sent" and peers are explicitly fictional (AGENTS.md).
- Block and "never match again" can work fully on the device, because they don't need a server, and should be labelled as device-local.
- Seeded or sample people must be visibly labelled as fictional on the card, the profile and in chat. Any "simulate acceptance" control must read as a simulation. The FTC/Match fake-notice allegations and CCS's fake-review enforcement show that unlabelled synthetic social proof is both a trust and a consumer-protection risk.

**Anti-patterns to avoid**
- Unlabelled seeded or bot profiles, or engagement notifications generated from accounts flagged as suspicious (the Match 2019 allegations).
- Pressure timers, fake scarcity ("2 seats left" when that is untrue) and undisclosed paid placement of events or hosts (CCS travel-platform case).
- Opaque reporting with no receipt or outcome (the Meetup practice and the research findings).
- Reporting that depends on the reporter choosing a perfect category before it can be submitted.
- Silent account removal without a reason or appeal, which Timeleft's own guidelines reserve the right to do. That reservation conflicts with its published 30-day appeal, which shows the gap between written policy and promised practice.
- Subscription or cancellation friction (the subject of the FTC's 2025 Match settlement and CCS's subscription-trap order).

**Minimum before a production launch (synthesis)**
- Authenticated accounts with a verified contact point (phone or email) and an adult check, ideally sgID over-18 or liveness plus an age check, with the app listed as 18+ in app stores.
- A staffed trust and safety intake that can meet the 24-hour harassment and stalking window, plus a written enforcement ladder, appeals process and crisis or law-enforcement protocol.
- Server-side block, report and separation that applies across devices, plus moderation tooling for takedown, restriction and identity disclosure under OSC direction.
- Public-venue enforcement for first meetings, venue disclosure only to confirmed participants, and opt-in sharing of plans with a trusted contact.
- PDPA basics: notices and consent, retention schedule, access and correction, account deletion, a breach-response plan meeting the 3-day PDPC notification deadline, and a named data protection officer. The last item is a standard PDPA accountability requirement, but it was not cited in the sources retrieved here.
- A safety centre with tips for before, during and after meetups, scam warnings ("never send money"), and Singapore help routes (police, OSC, Protection from Harassment Court).

### Gaps
- No primary source was found on what a Singapore platform must tell a reporter about outcomes. The OSC framework assesses adequacy within 24 hours but no retrieved source defines "adequate".
- The FTC's own 2025 settlement page was not retrieved. The settlement summary comes from a trade-press source, and the "25–30% of new registrations were fake" figure from an unofficial page was excluded as unverified.
- No primary CCS dark-patterns guideline document was found, only enforcement cases summarized by law firms.
- Whether a local-only prototype with fictional profiles needs any regulatory disclosure beyond honest labelling was not addressed by any source found. This is an inference from consumer-protection principles, not a cited rule.
