# Recipe media format: photo-led vs short-video-led vs hybrid (evidence as of Oct 2026)

Evidence grading used below: **[R]** peer-reviewed / rigorous; **[S]** small or uncontrolled study; **[I]** industry or company data (self-reported); **[V]** vendor, agency or competitor marketing claim (unverified); **[C]** commentary. Classic (pre-2022) items are labelled with their year.

## 1. What engagement data compares recipe video with photo/text?

### Takeaway
Video wins *discovery and reach* (social feeds, Japan's app market shift), but no public, controlled dataset compares in-app engagement, retention or conversion for photo-led vs video-led recipes. Surveys of where people get recipes still put written sources level with or ahead of video, with video's share highest among under-30s.

### Cited Findings
- **Tasty (classic, 2016):** Tasty's main Facebook page was the third-largest video account on Facebook in September 2016, with nearly 1.7 billion video views; videos averaged 22.8 million views in their first 30 days. [I/press] — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/just-15-months-old-tasty-driving-buzzfeed-video-facebook/)
- **Facebook cooking video plateau (classic, ~2017–18):** citing CrowdTangle data via Digiday, Marketing Dive reported that views of cooking how-to videos on Facebook were flat or declining, which pushed down ad rates for most "hands-in-pans" producers. Tasty was the exception and still commanded premium prices. The rise came from a platform algorithm, not a durable format advantage. [C/press] — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebooks-food-videos-show-declining-viewership/510086/)
- No 2024–2026 Tasty or BuzzFeed engagement figures were found in public search results. — (see Gaps)
- **Japan, Kurashiru (video app):** dely's May 2025 regulatory disclosure reports more than 44 million cumulative Kurashiru downloads since the 2016 launch. The company renamed itself Kurashiru, Inc. on 1 Oct 2025. [I, regulatory filing] — [dely disclosure, May 2025](https://finance-frontend-pc-dist.west.edge.storage-yahoo.jp/disclosure/20250526/20250526565424.pdf)
- **Japan, DELISH KITCHEN (video app):** its Play Store listing claims the No.1 user count among Japanese recipe services from Oct 2025 to Apr 2026 (Sensor Tower/Similarweb). The comparison set is three major services with more than 3M monthly app users and more than 25M monthly web users. The underlying numbers are not shown. [V] — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tv.every.delishkitchen&hl=en)
- **Japan, Cookpad (photo/text UGC) vs video apps:** a secondary analysis attributes Cookpad's decline partly to a shift from 2016 onward from "search and read" to "watch on video", with chef-supervised short phone videos from Kurashiru and YouTube clips. It also blames a 2016 management dispute, so format was not the only cause. [C] — [note.com analysis](https://note.com/ccre1018/n/n4342c2d9ce31?hl=en). Forbes (2019) reported that dely had overtaken Cookpad in video traffic. Cookpad then launched its own video service, and its shares fell about 90% over four years. [press, classic] — [Forbes 2019](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jsimms/2019/09/04/from-app-to-table-japanese-food-app-kurashiru-might-soon-deliver-meal-kits/)
- **Where people get recipes (US, 2025):** friends and family are the top source of cooking inspiration (41%), with YouTube at 36%. About 38% of 18–29-year-olds use TikTok, while people over 60 lean on cookbooks and Facebook. [I] — [Instacart 2025 survey](https://company.instacart.com/ideas/cooking-at-home-survey)
- **Where people get recipes (US, 2026, n=2,008, ±2%):** food blogs and websites 48%, YouTube 40%, family recipes 31%, physical cookbooks 27%, TikTok 22%. Over 40% of Gen Z use TikTok for recipe ideas, yet 71% of Gen Z plan to buy a cookbook. [I, reported by trade press] — [PPC Land summary of Inspired Taste study](https://ppc.land/food-blogs-beat-ai-for-recipes-what-a-2026-study-found/)
- **Think with Google (classic, 2014–15):** 59% of 25–34-year-olds cooked with a smartphone or tablet to hand, while people over 35 were more likely to print recipes. Food how-to content on YouTube had 419M views in 2014, "How to cook that" was a top-10 YouTube how-to search, and searches for "best recipes" on YouTube rose 48% year on year. [I, classic] — [Think with Google PDF](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/129/cooking-trends-among-millennials.pdf)
- **Pinterest × Dotdash Meredith:** a year-long deal for nearly 200 original short videos from Allrecipes, Serious Eats, Food & Wine and other brands. Publishers are producing short video for distribution platforms. [I] — [Pinterest Newsroom](https://newsroom.pinterest.com/news/pinterest-x-ddm/)

### Inferences
- Video is a proven **acquisition and attention** format on feeds and in Japan's app market. The evidence does not show that video recipes produce more *cooking*, retention or conversion inside an app.
- Written or structured recipes remain the dominant reference format across age groups. Video skews young and toward discovery.
- Japan's case mixes format with marketing spend and corporate turmoil. It is suggestive, not causal.

### Gaps
- No public A/B test or cohort data from any recipe app compares retention or conversion for photo-led vs video-led recipe pages.
- No current (2024–2026) Tasty, Allrecipes or Dotdash recipe-page video metrics, and no Kurashiru or DELISH KITCHEN MAU, view-through or completion figures, were found. IR decks may contain them but were not retrieved.
- No current Think with Google cooking-video data was found. The 2014–15 figures are dated.

## 2. What does HCI/learning research say about video vs static images for procedural tasks? Does video improve cooking success?

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses find dynamic visuals beat static pictures most clearly for **procedural-motor** skills, where the movement itself must be learned. The average advantage across all content is small (g ≈ 0.23). Cooking-specific studies show video *adds* to self-efficacy when paired with a written recipe, but they do not show better cooking outcomes than a well-illustrated written recipe.

### Cited Findings
- **Höffler & Leutner (2007, classic) [R]:** 26 studies and 76 comparisons, overall d = 0.37 (95% CI 0.25–0.49) favouring animation over static pictures. The effect is larger for highly realistic, video-based animation (d = 0.76, CI 0.39–1.13) and largest when procedural-motor knowledge is taught (d = 1.06, CI 0.72–1.40). — [Leibniz-IPN record](https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis); [PDF](https://wikicap.ulb.be/images/e/ef/Hoffler_Leutner_2007.pdf)
- **Later meta-analyses are more modest [R]:** Berney & Bétrancourt (2016) found g = 0.226 across 140 comparisons, and Castro-Alonso et al. (2019) found g = 0.23 across 82, both favouring animation. — summarised in [Instructional Science 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w); see also [Berney & Bétrancourt EARLI 2016](https://tecfa.unige.ch/perso/sandra/pdf/Earli2016_berney_betrancourt_FINAL.pdf)
- **Re-analysis (2021) [R]:** animations beat static pictures considerably when **the specifics of the displayed change** must be learned, for example how a motion is performed. — [ResearchGate: "When learning from animations is more successful than learning from static pictures"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351895693_When_learning_from_animations_is_more_successful_than_learning_from_static_pictures_learning_the_specifics_of_change). The secondary search could not confirm the author list of the Springer item, so attribute this as "a 2021 re-analysis".
- **Procedural task studies (classic) [R]:** static images *with motion cues* can match animation. One study found dynamic picture presentation better than static, and **fewer pictures** better than many. — [ScienceDirect: multimedia learning of a procedural task](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563212000167); [ResearchGate: video and static pictures for procedural contents](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223831054_Using_video_and_static_pictures_to_improve_learning_of_procedural_contents)
- **Cooking-specific, Surgenor et al. (2017, classic) [S]:** a cooking video plus printed recipe raised self-efficacy and enjoyment more than a printed recipe alone, as cited by a 2025 review. Participants in the béchamel-sauce experiment said **a sequence of images showing each stage** would have helped more than written text instructions. — [ResearchGate: "The impact of video technology on learning: A cooking skills experiment"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315741906_The_impact_of_video_technology_on_learning_A_cooking_skills_experiment); [PMC 2025 review section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707162/)
- **Kaesberg (thesis 2019; J Am Coll Health 2023) [S]:** over five weeks, students received either recipe cards or recipe cards plus video. Both groups improved self-efficacy, with **no significant difference between groups** and no change in meals cooked per week. The author concludes video helps when meals are simple and videos short. — [ISU thesis](https://ir.library.illinoisstate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2192&context=etd); [JACH](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07448481.2021.1978462). *Conflict:* a 2025 review summarises this work as "video technology improved … self-efficacy" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707162/)). The thesis shows no advantage of video over cards.
- **2025 pilot (Frontiers Public Health) [S, no control, n=14]:** short videos (mean 4.8 min) with a photo recipe book moved produce-prep comfort from 5.10 to 5.86, which was not significant (p = 0.104). The review it contains cites Roy et al. (2024): **eye-catching dish photos** are key on recipe cards, and **bird's-eye angles** help in cooking videos. It also cites Nour et al. (2018): short videos reduced perceived time, cost and skill barriers (n=16). — [PMC12707162](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707162/)
- **Head-to-head evidence is missing:** no randomised trial was found that isolates video vs written or photo recipes and measures actual cooking performance. — search summary in [Kaesberg/JACH results](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07448481.2021.1978462) (see Gaps)

### Inferences
- Video's learning advantage is concentrated where **motion is the content**: folding, pleating, kneading, knife grip, wok tossing, judging dough or batter texture. For assembly or ordering steps (add X, stir, simmer 10 min), well-chosen stills plus text are close to equivalent.
- Cooking evidence supports **video as a supplement to a written recipe**, not a replacement. Every positive cooking study paired video with text or a card.
- "Fewer, well-chosen stills with motion cues" is a credible low-cost middle ground. Examples are a 2–3 frame sequence with arrows for dumpling pleats.

### Gaps
- No RCT measures dish success (taste, doneness, errors) for video vs photo recipes.
- Mayer's segmenting and signalling principles were not re-retrieved from a primary source in this pass. Treat them as background theory consistent with the findings above, not as new evidence.
- No cooking-specific study on learning from overhead montage (Tasty-style) vs per-step clips was found.

## 3. How do people actually use video while cooking? Per-step loops vs one long video

### Takeaway
People cooking from video constantly **pause, re-watch and jump** to align playback with their own pace. Research systems keep restructuring video into **step/action units with text**. This directly supports per-step short clips or loops plus step text over one continuous montage.

### Cited Findings
- **Tuncer, Brown & Lindwall, CHI 2020 [R]:** in video analysis of pairs doing practical tasks with instructional videos, users had to **pause repeatedly** to follow along. Pausing coordinates watching with doing. — [ACM DL "On Pause"](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3313831.3376759)
- **Chang et al., CHI 2019 [R]:** three studies (n=20 think-aloud with YouTube, n=16 voice prototype, n=6 Wizard-of-Oz) identified seven navigation intents. They include a *pace-control pause*, a *content-alignment pause* and a *replay jump*, meaning re-watching a segment to check for a mistake or confirm understanding. Voice users paused before jumping more often. — [paper PDF](https://kixlab.github.io/website-files/2019/chi2019-VoiceVideoNavigation-paper.pdf); [project](https://minsukchang.com/proj-voicevideo/)
- **CHI 2022 workshop (KIXLAB) [S]:** cooking videos were chosen for their complex procedural content. Participants valued navigating by **action units** and seeing which ingredients each step uses. — [PDF](https://kixlab.github.io/website-files/2022/chi2022-workshop-StarLab-paper.pdf)
- **StoryNavi survey (arXiv 2024) [S]:** cooking videos scored lower (mean 3.095) than educational videos and product reviews on viewers seeking specific information rather than watching the whole video. — [arXiv 2410.03207](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03207v1)
- **Vid2Coach (arXiv 2025) [S, n=8 BLV]:** turns how-to videos into step-wise task assistants, evaluated in participants' own kitchens against the original video plus transcript. — [arXiv 2506.00717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00717)
- **AROMA (arXiv 2025) [S, n=8 BLV]:** participants **skip, reorder or modify steps** for preference, ingredients or context, which linear video does not accommodate. The system needs offline video pre-processing. — [arXiv 2507.10963](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10963)
- **Text adapted for voice (arXiv 2023) [S]:** separate work rewrites text recipes for voice interaction, a hands-busy alternative to video. — [arXiv 2306.09992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09992)
- **DELISH KITCHEN product pattern [C, 2018-era reviews]:** individual steps are shown as short videos with on-screen text. A **"Kitchen Mode"** lets cooks move back and forth between steps, tapping thumbnails jumps to a scene, and some clips have narration besides BGM. The whole-dish video is about 1 minute. — [Appllio](https://appllio.com/app-delishkitchen); [ASCII](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/001/644/1644841/2/)
- **Sound off (classic, 2019) [I]:** 69% watch video with sound off in public and 25% even in private. 80% are more likely to finish a video when captions exist (n=5,616 US adults). — [3Play Media summary of Verizon Media/Publicis](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/); [Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx). A sponsored Sharethrough figure of 75% mobile on mute is a different study. [V] — [Digiday sponsored](https://digiday.com/sponsored/75-percent-of-people-watch-mobile-videos-on-mute/?facet1=crm)

### Inferences
- A continuous 1-minute montage suits **feed discovery**. While cooking, people need **step-addressable** media: a clip that loops silently on the current step, with the text visible next to it.
- Sound cannot carry essential information. Quantities and timings must be on-screen text or captions.
- Hands-busy cooking favours large step navigation (next/previous) and, optionally, voice. Auto-advance video conflicts with users' own pace.

### Gaps
- No public telemetry from Kurashiru or DELISH KITCHEN on step-clip replays, loop usage or Kitchen Mode adoption.
- No study directly compares per-step loops against a single long video for cooking outcomes.

## 4. Trade-offs: production cost, size/data, offline, accessibility, UGC moderation

### Takeaway
Photos are far cheaper to produce, store, cache offline and make accessible. Video multiplies storage, bandwidth and caption obligations, and adds moderation load for user content. Hybrid "photo-first, clip only where motion matters" keeps most of the learning benefit at a fraction of the cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Accessibility obligations (WCAG 2.2, the project's target):** prerecorded video with audio needs **captions** (SC 1.2.2, A) — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html). Video needs an **audio description or media alternative**, such as full text steps (SC 1.2.3, A) — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-description-or-media-alternative-prerecorded.html). Auto-starting motion longer than 5 s needs **pause/stop/hide** (SC 2.2.2, A) — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html). Auto-playing audio needs control (SC 1.4.2, A) — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-control.html). Motion triggered by interaction should be disableable (SC 2.3.3, AAA) — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)
- **Blind/low-vision cooks:** both 2025 BLV studies had to pre-process video into structured, groundable steps before it was usable. Raw recipe video is a poor accessible format on its own. — [AROMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10963); [Vid2Coach](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00717)
- **Current QuinTaste media footprint (local project evidence, not web):** 13 active catalogue JPEGs total 2,087,498 bytes (about 160 KB average; largest 270,260 bytes). Market photos are capped under 220 KB each and 1,200,000 photo-URI characters per collection. FoodDocument writes are guarded at 1,500,000 serialized bytes, and creator recipe exports exclude photo bytes. — `AGENTS.md` in this repository
- **Singapore bandwidth is not the constraint:** median mobile download was 127.75 Mbps in early 2025 and 164.75 Mbps at end-2025 (Ookla via DataReportal). — [Digital 2025 Singapore](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-singapore); [Digital 2026 Singapore](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-singapore)
- **Video-import tools depend on text:** ReciMe relies heavily on caption text, so Reels where the recipe is only shown or spoken can extract incompletely. [V/C] — [Ladle comparison](https://getladle.com/best/apps-to-save-tiktok-and-instagram-recipes)

### Inferences
- **Size arithmetic.** These are assumed bitrates; Apple's HLS bitrate table could not be retrieved, so verify against the real encoding ladder before using them.
  - A 15-second step clip at 1.5 Mbps is about 2.8 MB, roughly 17× the project's average photo.
  - A 60-second montage at 2 Mbps is about 15 MB.
  - Ten step clips per recipe is about 28 MB, versus about 1.6 MB for ten step photos.
- **Offline:** the project's 7-day saved/offline menu model and FoodDocument byte guard could hold photos. Offline video would need a separate file cache with explicit download consent and eviction, which is out of scope for the current AsyncStorage-based design.
- **Production cost:** overhead per-step clips need a fixed rig, consistent lighting, an editor and caption authoring. Photos can be shot during the same session. Short step loops cost less than a narrated or edited montage but more than stills.
- **UGC video moderation:** reviewing a clip means watching it, not glancing at an image. Audio, faces, music rights and hidden text all add review surface. Given the project's existing "Ready to submit · Not sent" creator boundary with no editorial review service, UGC video should stay out of scope until review exists.
- Singapore's fast networks reduce *latency* concerns, but they do not remove CDN egress cost, device storage, offline use or the accessibility work.

### Gaps
- No sourced per-recipe production cost was found for photo vs video. Tasty, Kurashiru and DELISH KITCHEN do not publish unit costs.
- No authoritative bitrate ladder was retrieved; the Apple HLS spec page did not render.
- No sourced data on moderation cost per minute of food UGC video.

## 5. Trends 2025–2026: short-form vertical video and apps that import social video into text

### Takeaway
Short vertical video dominates recipe *discovery*. A growing app category exists to convert TikTok, Reels and YouTube recipes back into **structured text cards** with the video linked. This is strong circumstantial evidence that people cook from text and steps, using video for inspiration and occasional technique checks.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 SideChef study counted more than 4.6 million #TikTokFood posts. Commentary notes that saved social recipes often sit unread in jumbled folders while cookbooks remain valued for reliability. [C] — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/there-are-more-than-4-6-million-food-posts-on-tiktok-alone-why-then-do-we-still-love-cookbooks-276505)
- **Pestle:** on-device AI reads the caption, parses ingredients and steps, and saves "a clean recipe card" **with the original video link kept**. [V, own site] — [pestleai.com](https://pestleai.com/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pestle-recipe-manager/id1574776971)
- **ReciMe:** imports from Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, Pinterest and the web. Reviewers call it the most reliable importer, but it is caption-dependent. Free-tier import caps apply; a competitor claims 5 per week, unverified. [V/C] — [Ladle](https://getladle.com/best/apps-to-save-tiktok-and-instagram-recipes); [Sugo](https://sugo.kitchen/blog/every-way-to-import-a-recipe)
- Many 2026 "save recipes from TikTok/Instagram" guides exist, which shows a crowded category. Most are competitor marketing. [V] — [Swoodie](https://swoodie.app/blog/save-recipes-from-tiktok-instagram-youtube-2026); [Peel](https://trypeel.app/blog/best-apps-to-save-recipes-from-instagram)
- In Singapore, average daily social media time fell 8.4% year on year to 2 h 02 min, while platforms used per month rose to 7.2 (Jan 2025). [I] — [We Are Social SG](https://wearesocial.com/sg/blog/2025/02/digital-2025-top-digital-and-social-media-trends-in-singapore/)

### Inferences
- The import-app category exists because a video is hard to cook from: no scannable quantities, no step addressability and no scaling. Users want the **text recipe as the working artefact** and the video as provenance or reference.
- An app does not need to win the feed format war. It should be the place where the cooking happens: structured steps, scaling and timers, which QuinTaste Cook already has, with optional linked or embedded technique media.

### Gaps
- No rigorous data shows what fraction of imported or saved social recipes are actually cooked, or whether people cook from the extracted text or the original video.
- No 2025–2026 Pinterest Predicts or TikTok food report figures on video vs static recipe saves were retrieved.

- **Japan:** Kurashiru reported 44M+ cumulative downloads (May 2025). - No Singapore data-plan or data-cap analysis. With these speeds, cost to the user is likely low, but this was not verified.

## 7. Recommendation framework: when photos suffice vs when video adds value

### Takeaway
Use a **photo-led hybrid**. Every recipe gets a hero photo, structured text steps and optional step photos. Add **short, silent, captioned, looping per-step clips (about 5–15 s) only for motion-critical techniques**, never one long montage as the primary format. This aligns with the meta-analytic evidence (motion helps when motion is the content), with in-kitchen navigation research (step-addressable, pausable) and with the project's storage, offline and creator-review constraints.

### Cited Findings
- Motion-critical skills gain most from realistic dynamic visuals (procedural-motor d = 1.06; realistic video d = 0.76; classic 2007). The overall average gain is small (g ≈ 0.23). — [Höffler & Leutner](https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis); [Instructional Science 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w)
- Users pause, replay and navigate by step while cooking. — [CHI 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3313831.3376759); [CHI 2019](https://kixlab.github.io/website-files/2019/chi2019-VoiceVideoNavigation-paper.pdf)
- Stage images can be as helpful as, or more helpful than, text, and fewer well-chosen pictures beat many. — [Surgenor et al. (2017)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315741906_The_impact_of_video_technology_on_learning_A_cooking_skills_experiment); [procedural-task study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223831054_Using_video_and_static_pictures_to_improve_learning_of_procedural_contents)
- A dish photo is a key recipe-card feature, and overhead angles help in videos (Roy et al. 2024, as cited). — [PMC12707162](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707162/)
- Captions and pause controls are required, and sound is off for most viewers in public. — [W3C 1.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html); [W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html); [Verizon/Publicis 2019](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/)

### Inferences (decision rules)

**Photos (hero plus optional step stills) are enough when steps are:**
- additive or sequencing (add, mix, simmer, plate);
- judged by an end state a still can show (golden colour, consistency at a stage);
- simple dishes for which video showed no advantage over cards (Kaesberg).

**A per-step clip adds real value when:**
- the hand motion is the skill: dumpling or bao pleating, kneading and windowpane, rolling and folding, knife technique, tossing, wrapping kueh or popiah;
- the cue is temporal: when to flip, how a sauce coats, how fast to stir;
- 2–3 stills with arrows cannot convey it.

**Format rules for any clip:**
- silent by default, captioned, and showing the quantity and time text next to it;
- no autoplay sound and a visible pause control;
- respects reduced motion by showing a poster frame and requiring a tap to play;
- loops on the current step and is step-addressable;
- streamed with a poster image, not stored in FoodDocument or AsyncStorage;
- excluded from offline Saved unless the user explicitly downloads it.

**Avoid:**
- a single long montage as the cooking interface;
- UGC video before an editorial review service exists;
- autoplay video in browse grids (cost and WCAG 2.2.2).

**Cheaper middle step:**
- 2–4-frame "technique strips" (stills with motion arrows), shot in the same photo session, before investing in video production.

**Success metrics for a later in-app test:**
- step-completion rate;
- "Finish cooking" rate;
- re-open within 7 days;
- saves;
- clip play and replay per step;
- data per session.

Run it as an A/B on a few technique-heavy recipes rather than switching formats wholesale.

### Gaps
- None of these rules has been validated with QuinTaste users. A small instrumented test on 3–5 technique recipes would be needed.
- No evidence was found on whether video increases the *conversion* of browse to cook or to a saved recipe in an app context.
